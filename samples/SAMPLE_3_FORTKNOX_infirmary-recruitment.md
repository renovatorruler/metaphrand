# FORT KNOX MOVIE — sequence 2: the infirmary and the offer (v1)

---

INT. FIELD HOSPITAL - CONVERTED GYMNASIUM - DAY

A high school gym with the hoops winched up to the rafters. Cots in rows on the game lines. The scoreboard still reads HOME 54 GUEST 51 from some Friday night that nobody has thought about since.

Rain on the roof. A woodstove going in the corner with its pipe run out a window hole.

Four men hold down a corner by the bleachers. GIVENS, 40s, a sergeant's way of sitting even in a gown, his left arm ending above the wrist in a wrap of clean bandage. CATES, 30s, burns down one side of his neck, playing solitaire with a deck gone soft as cloth. ROLLO, 20s, leg in a fixator, external pins through the shin.

A fourth cot sits close to them. The man in it, MERCER, 30s, gray-faced and still, has a chest tube scar and a paperback he has not turned a page of in an hour. He is always just there. Nobody remembers him arriving.

An orderly works down the row swapping dressings. He hangs one man's used wrap on a line by the stove to dry.

CATES
(watching that)
They're boiling them now. Boiling and reusing.

GIVENS
They did that in Vicksburg too.

CATES
That supposed to make me feel better?

GIVENS
It's supposed to make you feel like history.

Rollo has a little transistor radio on his chest, volume low. A confident voice in it.

RADIO (V.O.)
...decisive action along the Ohio, where Free State forces continue to hold the initiative...

ROLLO
Hold the initiative.

He clicks it off. Lies there looking at the rafters.

ROLLO (CONT'D)
When I got hit we were fighting at the river. My cousin got hit six months before me, and they were fighting a hundred miles NORTH of the river.
(beat)
Anybody else notice the war keeps getting closer to my mama's house?

Nobody says anything. Cates lays a red nine on a black ten.

ROLLO (CONT'D)
I'm just saying. If we're winning, we're winning in a real strange direction.

GIVENS
(quiet)
We're not winning.

It sits there. Nobody argues with it, and the not-arguing is louder than the saying.

CATES
My discharge came through. Tuesday. They're sending me back up with a rifle and no plate. Kid at supply said just wear the vest, most of what's flying is fragments anyway.

GIVENS
(dry)
Well. As long as it's only fragments.

ROLLO
What happens to us if we lose? For real. Anybody heard what that looks like?

CATES
Same as it always looks. The generals sign something, then they go home and write books, and guys like us stand in lines the rest of our lives.

The rain fills the pause. Then, from the fourth cot, a voice they have never heard. Flat. Unhurried.

MERCER
You three done being sad about it?

They all look. Mercer has not moved. He is looking at the rafters like Rollo was.

GIVENS
It talks.

MERCER
Six days I've been listening to you. You're not wrong about the river. You're not wrong about the plates.
(now he turns his head)
When's your paper come through, Sergeant?

GIVENS
Thursday. Why.

MERCER
Because I'm interested in making a difference in this war before it's over. And I think the three of you might be too. There's a man who'd like to buy you supper Thursday night.

CATES
What man?

MERCER
The kind that pays for supper.

He goes back to his paperback. Turns a page for the first time all day.

EXT. SMALL TOWN - MAIN STREET - NIGHT

A dead little downtown under one working streetlight. Sandbags around the post office. Every window dark except a diner at the end of the block, and it closes as we watch, a woman in an apron locking the door and pulling the shade.

Givens, Cates, and Rollo on crutches come up the sidewalk in civilian coats that don't fit over their dressings. Mercer waits outside the one solid building on the street.

A shuttered bank branch, the name pried off the stone, cleaner stone left where the letters were.

ROLLO
Supper, huh.

MERCER
It's the only building in town with a door that locks.

He knocks twice. The door opens from inside.

INT. SHUTTERED BANK - NIGHT

Blacked-out windows. Two camp lanterns on the old counter. The vault door stands open at the back, and inside the vault somebody has set up a card table and folding chairs, because it is the one room in town with no windows at all.

At the table: PRYOR, 50s, heavy, in a suit that fit him two wars ago. No rank, no pins, no papers in sight. A pot of coffee and five cups.

He pours without asking who wants any.

PRYOR
Sergeant Givens. Corporal Cates. Private Rollo.
(to Rollo)
How's the leg holding those pins?

ROLLO
Leg's fine. Who are you?

PRYOR
A man who reads infirmary rosters.
(he sits)
Before all this. Not the army. Before. What did you do?

GIVENS
Drove a truck. Regional. Flatbed and reefer.

PRYOR
Heavy loads?

GIVENS
Steel coil out of Gary, when there was a Gary.

PRYOR
(to Cates)
You?

CATES
Quarry. Shot-firer. I sent the holes.

PRYOR
Licensed?

CATES
Was.

PRYOR
(to Rollo)
And you?

ROLLO
(beat, a little embarrassed)
Power company. Lineman apprentice. Eight months.

PRYOR
Substations?

ROLLO
Mostly I held the ladder.

Pryor nods like a man checking boxes he already checked.

GIVENS
You're building something. Say what.

PRYOR
What do you think I'm building, Sergeant?

GIVENS
Losing side's only got one job left worth recruiting for. Something you don't come back from. Some bridge, some airfield. You want men with nothing at home, and you found three of us in a gym.
(beat)
So say it plain and I'll hear it. I've made my peace with most of it.

PRYOR
(pause)
No. That's the other fellas.

He reaches into his coat. Lays something on the card table and slides it into the lantern light.

An old tourist postcard, creased and sun-faded: a squat granite building behind fences, on a green lawn. Gold lettering along the bottom: GREETINGS FROM THE BULLION DEPOSITORY, FORT KNOX, KY.

Nobody touches it.

ROLLO
(reading it again to be sure)
That's...

He laughs. One bark of it, honest.

Nobody joins him. The laugh runs out of room and dies somewhere over the card table.

PRYOR
Hundred and forty-seven million ounces, last honest count. It is the reason their paper is worth anything from here to Japan. It sits eighty miles from where you got shot, gentlemen, behind a fence being watched by a garrison that got gutted twice for the spring push.
(he taps the postcard once)
Everybody in this war is trying to kill soldiers. Soldiers are the one thing both sides still grow plenty of.
(beat)
I don't want to kill anything. I want to rob the till.

Silence. The lanterns hiss.

GIVENS
That's not a mission. That's a bank job.

PRYOR
That's right.

GIVENS
Whose orders?

PRYOR
(pouring himself more coffee)
Now that's the first good question anybody's asked all week.

CUT TO BLACK.
