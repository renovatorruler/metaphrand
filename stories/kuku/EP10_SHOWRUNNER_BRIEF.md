# कुकु और अक्षर — showrunner brief for the next episode

Companion to `2026-08-11_EP9_ba_bada_SPEC_SCREENPLAY.md` (the finished EP9 script). Hand both to whoever writes EP10. This file is the accumulated canon plus the mistakes EP9 paid for — following it should prevent a second rewrite.

## The show

Hindi-language animated series teaching one Devanagari letter per episode to a 4–7 year old. **अक्षर घाटी (Letter Valley)** — a soft green Himalayan valley of small friendly dragons. Cozy, warm, real kid-scale stakes, never fearsome. Episode shape: cold open (dream/flash-forward, ~2:00) → title (~1:07) → main story (12–13:00) → credits (~0:40).

**Art style is LOCKED: 3D papercraft.** Layered cut-paper illustration with real depth — matte construction-paper textures, visible paper edges and folds, warm storybook palette, soft studio lighting. Volumetric like a lit diorama built from card. Never flat 2D, never sticker-like, never glossy plastic 3D render.

## Cast (fixed identities, fixed colours)

- **कुकु / Kuku** — GREEN, the hero. Breathes glowing letters of light.
- **फ्यूरिया / Furia** — CRIMSON-RED, fastest, impulsive.
- **वैस्पर / Vesper** — SKY-BLUE, reads wind and paths.
- **कैस्टर / Castor** — GOLDEN-YELLOW, changes his own size.
- **लेडा / Leda** — PINK, notices and names patterns; usually the one who solves it.
- **दादी / Dadi** — large elderly silver-white WINGED dragon grandmother, round glasses, knitted shawl. The moral centre.
- **ऋषि / Rishi** — elderly white-maned WINGLESS dragon sage, simple robes, wooden staff. Not a human, not bald, not Gandhi-like — a dragon.
- **चील / Cheel** — the eagle antagonist, visually ominous and usually distant, but **not a
  dark-power user**. Carries Rishi's old boon: *"जो उड़े — वो मुझसे कभी जीत न पाए"*. Her threat is
  the boon, flight, strength, theft, and cunning—never black-smoke fire or a dark aura.
- **कालू / Kalu** — the black dog. **बकरी का बच्चा** — cream goat kid with a bell.

Exactly five child dragons. Never a sixth, never purple or orange, never duplicates.

## Magic rules (the story must obey these)

1. **What Kuku forges cannot be unmade.** Creation and persistence are his power. This is the show's core rule and the reason he matters — use it instead of inventing new ones.
2. Rishi's boon constrains who can defeat or catch Cheel; it does **not** give her power over
   Kuku's completed creations. In Episode 8 her wing-gust scattered an unfinished letter bridge.
   Rishi still owns the consequences of granting the boon, but the boon is not dark power.
3. The five children's bracelets (कड़े) let them take large youthful forms. They earn this; it is never automatic.
4. Any mechanism must be explainable to a five-year-old with everyday physics. EP9's gate works because **a door stuck half-open makes a draft**, and a draft **spirals things upward like water down a drain** — that is why the countdown is three orbits. Do not write a rule a child cannot repeat back.

## Hard lessons from EP9 (each one cost a rewrite)

**Write the picture, not the dialogue.** EP9's first cut had 107 of 143 shots showing only a talking face while every actual event happened in the audio. If a line describes an action, the shot must show the action. A character can speak *while* the event happens behind or around them — that satisfies both the lip-sync rule and the story.

**Silent-test every scene.** Read the scene with the dialogue covered. If you cannot follow what happened, the scene is not written yet.

**Never explain a rule only in words.** Show it once physically, then a character may name it. EP9 needed a shot of the gate's missing notch matched against the letter before anyone could say "the new letter must be big".

**Establish before you threaten.** The goat had to be befriended (copying the children's practice hops, napping on its favourite low cloud) *before* the draft took that cloud. A stranger in danger is not stakes.

**Payoffs need their own shot.** Celebration before guilt. Transformation shown, not announced. A countdown needs a visible dial.

**Dialogue length dictates pacing** — write lean. EP9's recorded speech reached 491s inside a 720s story, which left almost no room for picture. Aim for well under half the runtime in dialogue.

**Never truncate paid animation.** Beats must be at least as long as their clip; consecutive shots sharing one clip must continue it, not restart it. Episode length flexes to fit the picture — the runtime target is not worth chopping footage for.

## Production notes for whoever generates assets

- **Never let an image model draw Devanagari.** It produced a wrong ब on every attempt (mirrored, फ-like, invented). Either composite the glyph from the real codepoint, or attach a rendered image of the letter as a shape reference and verify every result.
- Attach character reference images and state the colour and the size class (small child form vs large youthful form) in every prompt; models drift to generic western dragons otherwise.
- Verify each generated still against the cast list before wiring it. EP9 caught a sixth purple dragon, a stray human boy, and repeated wrong-scale characters this way.
- Review renders: full-frame picture with the shot number in a **small corner chip**. Never a caption panel across the bottom.
