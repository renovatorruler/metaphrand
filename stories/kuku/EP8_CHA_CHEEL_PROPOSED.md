**Letter:** च — anchor: **चालाक चील**. Lived words: चील · चाल · चक्कर · चुप · चाँद. App drill adds: चम्मच · चिड़िया.

**The nemesis:** चालाक चील — a great cut-paper kite, elegant, patient, vain. Years of hard तपस्या on the high peak earned her a वरदान from the mountain ऋषि, granted as तपस्या demands regardless of the asker: **«कोई उड़ने वाला मुझे कभी न हरा पाए»** — no flyer shall ever defeat her. She chose those words believing flyers were her only rivals, then found the one valley where nobody flies and crowned herself its sky. Her word is चाल; she never chases, she arranges. Register: comic menace, never gory. She NEVER becomes an ally, and defeated ≠ destroyed — she flees vowing return.

## Beats

0. **COLD OPEN — the boon (60–75s, sepia storybook register, सूत्रधार narration).** Before the title card, an old story told in the old-story look: a young चील climbs to the high peak and SITS — snow piles on her wings, seasons wheel past, a small bird nests on her head and raises its chicks and leaves — तपस्या shown, never defined. The mountain ऋषि appears, grants as the law demands, and she speaks her wish plainly: «कोई उड़ने वाला मुझे कभी न हरा पाए।» Golden words wrap her. She rises, circles once — and her shadow slides over a green valley far below. Title card. The loophole is never winked at; the boon is stated straight and left for the audience to hold. (This register doubles as training: the same old-story look returns for दादी's flashback in beat 7.)

1. **A bright morning + the letter.** दादी introduces च; a shadow keeps crossing the courtyard, and तानसेन mimics a cry nobody recognises. लेडा — the eyes — is the only one who notices the shadow always circles the same way. Nobody listens to the smallest. Yet. (The audience, who saw the cold open, knows exactly whose shadow it is — and the kids on screen don't.)

2. **The चाल.** A trail of shiny paper gifts leads up the goat-path to the crag. फ्यूरिया — the runner — is first, of course; the trail is built for exactly that. At the top, a beautiful nest-bower, and behind them the plank over the gap is quietly gone.

3. **Trapped UP.** The चील lands on the bower rim at last, unhurried, and invokes what the audience already knows in one proud line: «मेरा वरदान भूल गए? कोई उड़ने वाला मुझे कभी न हरा पाए!» She wants कुकु's glowing letters for her collection: the brightest things in the valley. The kids are stranded in the one place wings would solve — and for the first time, the flying gap has COST them.

4. **The team fights — every job attempted, every job not enough.** This is the action block, plan-execute-adapt: लेडा reads the चील's circling pattern and calls the safe seconds; कैस्टर finds a crack too small for wings and squeezes down — it dead-ends above the drop; कुकु forges a bridge of letters — her wing-wind scatters it; कुकु forges «चुप» to kill her mocking echo — she smiles wider; फ्यूरिया runs the rim AGAINST her circle, splitting her attention, buying the others time. Brave, clever, coordinated — and still trapped, because none of them can do the one thing she can.

5. **तानसेन's gambit.** The voice — and the only flyer on the team. A parrot flying past a hawk is prey, and he goes anyway: he mimics the चील's OWN voice to snarl her circling, slips the rim, and carries word down the valley. कालू — the tracker — meets him, and leads दादी up the goat-path by scent, the slow way, unseen.

6. **The dive, and दादी's tail.** The चील ends the game: one long silent dive at the nest for the letters. दादी steps out of the rocks between her and the children — grounded, old, unhurried — and one full sweep of her tail meets the dive. The चील tumbles across the sky in a storm of feathers, shrieking «मेरी चाल! मेरा वरदान—» — and दादी names the loophole for every child who held the cold open in their head: «वरदान तो पूरा है, बेटा। मैं उड़ती ही नहीं।» The boon holds, unbroken: no FLYER defeated her. She flaps over the ridge vowing return.

7. **The begging — and the story.** Safe, the question explodes, earned: «उसके भी पंख, हमारे भी पंख! हम क्यों नहीं?» And दादी finally tells it, as a story, in flashback: a valley whose dragons flew; then the रास्ता; then the पुल; then the रथ that carried more than any dragon and never got tired. Nobody wicked, nothing stolen — wings set down one convenience at a time, until no morning needed them and no one remembered. **«हम उड़ना भूल गए।»** And the reserved line: **«रास्ता ऊपर नहीं जाता।»** Whether it can come back, she doesn't say.

8. **The lesson.** One law, two uses: the चील's power came from तपस्या — effort, repeated, earns what nothing else can. She spent hers on greed. «तुम अपनी किस पर ख़र्च करोगे?» Grow strong, practise, so no one's चाल can catch you — and today, remember: alone every one of you failed, together you brought the tail. They walk down by the road — the long way — and the kids look at the रथ differently now.

9. **Button.** Dusk on the ridge: a distant silhouette wheels once and is gone. तानसेन, quietly, in HER voice: «मेरी चाल…» — कालू growls at the sky.

## Canon compliance

Nobody flies (तानसेन the parrot always has — he is not a dragon and proves nothing about them, which the kids' begging can even cite: «तानसेन उड़ता है! चील उड़ती है! हम क्यों नहीं?»). The forgetting stays «भूल गए», never «नहीं सकते»; no return promised; the strength lesson never touches wings. The boon's wording guarantees the चील stays dangerous even after «उ से उड़ान» — flight can never defeat her, only wit and words can. She never allies. Register: papercraft folktale; Hindu mythological structure (तपस्या/वरदान/loophole) used with respect — the ऋषि grants as the law demands, and is no one's villain.
