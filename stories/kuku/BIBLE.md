# कुकु और अक्षर — World Bible

*A warm preschool series that teaches Hindi one letter at a time, in the register (not the plot) of* Sesame Street *and* Bluey *— gentle, playful, ritualized, safe. The teaching rides on story and character, never on flashcards.*

## Current phase override — Akshar Gurukul, binding from Episode 9

`AKSHAR_GURUKUL_CANON.md` governs the active five-child power-and-training phase. The children now
receive repeatable great-dragon forms and bracelet-form flight; Kuku, Fyuria, Vesper, Castor, and
Leda are equal trainees. Older references below to a trio, a villain-free world, a recurring chant,
or flight remaining unavailable document earlier development and do not override the current canon.

The enduring core is unchanged: story earns the letter, the letter's exact shape solves a physical
problem, and the child audience can copy the action in play.

## Part 0 — The register (read first, every session)

**The bar:** a child roughly aged 3–7, who speaks little or no Hindi, laughs at a small dragon,
wants to imitate a great one, and by the end can say the focus sound and its lived words. Language
stays accessible to the youngest; serialized power, choices, and consequences reward older viewers.
The letter is *earned by a story beat*, never announced cold.

**What this is NOT.** Not an app, a drill, or a worksheet read aloud. Action and a continuing
nemesis are allowed, but no frightening menace, cruelty, sarcasm, or adult wink over the kids'
heads. Nobody pauses an urgent story to monologue the lesson; the rule is learned by using it.

**The craft laws bind here too, but bend for the age:** heavy repetition and call-and-response — a defect in the adult stories — is **required pedagogy here.** The engine's echo/repeat instincts are deliberately overridden for dialogue (see Part VI).

## Part I — The Place

**अक्षर घाटी (Akshar Ghaati — "Letter Valley").** A soft green Himalayan valley of friendly little dragons. Warm light, round hills, a stream, Dadi Maya's stone house with a small oil lamp (दीया). Every dragon here breathes fire — it is how they light lamps, warm tea, toast the morning. It is ordinary and cozy, not fearsome. Into this ordinary world, one dragon breathes something no one has seen: **letters.**

## Part II — The engine (fire → letters)

The youngest dragon, **कुकु**, cannot make flame. When he breathes—especially when he is excited or
brave—a glowing **अक्षर** appears instead. A completed Kuku-forged letter becomes a stable physical
story object and persists; it does not evaporate when the scene ends. Each episode focuses on one
new sound or letter relationship. The machine is: **a story need makes Kuku forge the letter, its
exact shape changes the action, and the valley and child at home learn it by watching it work.**

## Part III — The child's journey (the real curriculum)

Under the fun, a real spine for the viewer: recognize the shape → make the sound → hear it starting familiar words → say it back → find it in the hero's own name. "Most-useful-first" letter order (`LETTER_MAP.md`) means the child is building *real, high-frequency words* (family, body, animals, food) from episode one, not abstract glyphs.

## Part IV — The cast (see `VOICE_CARDS.md` for the voice spec)

From Episode 9, the core trainee cohort is **five children**. An ensemble climax gives each present
child an independent choice; a spotlight episode may use fewer characters without demoting anyone:

- **कुकु** — hero, the heart + the forge; breathes persistent physical letters and must learn to
  share control of what he creates.
- **फ्यूरिया (Furia)** — the spark. First at everything (catchphrase «पहले मैं!»), kind but fiercely
  competitive with her brother; mothers anything small; dreams of a shiny red कार; writes each
  day's letter in her किताब; in action she is speed and first response. *(Mirrors the author's
  daughter.)*
- **वैस्पर (Vesper)** — the dreamer, Furia's younger brother. Kind, sweet, lost in imagination
  (must be called 4–5 times); drifts into English and is reminded «हिंदी में, वैस्पर!» (the show's
  in-character translation device); notices what everyone misses; **falls asleep before everyone,
  anywhere — every episode ends on where.** In action he sees hidden routes and patterns.
  *(Mirrors the author's son.)*
- **कैस्टर** — the young access specialist; climbs into cracks and, in great form, changes scale to
  reach or brace spaces nobody else can.
- **लेडा** — the youngest precision specialist; notices and names the exact detail everyone else
  overlooks, and expects that observation to change the plan.
- **कालू** — Kuku's ground companion, tracker, and alarm. He is important to missions but is not a
  bracelet trainee.
- **पापा** — Kuku's father; kind-but-firm; never a villain.
- **दादी माया** — grandmother and mentor; leads the Hindi lesson.
- **तानसेन** — a green parrot who repeats what he hears in the exact आवाज़ of whoever said it; joins the valley permanently in Ep6.
- **भोलू** — big gentle older dragon (recurring, optional).
- **सूत्रधार / Narrator** — brief offscreen Hindi storybook framing.
- **Friends-of-the-show:** अन्ना & माशा (the kids' real best friends) — referenced from Ep2 (माशा's
  letter → म), guest appearance later.
- **Future segment idea (from the family's real ritual of writing letters to the dragon):** Kuku's
  mailbox — episodes can open with a real letter from a viewer-kid.

## Part V — The spine and the underlayer

**Original spine (Episodes 1–8):** the littlest dragon cannot do what the big dragons do and
discovers that he can do something wonderful instead.

**Gurukul spine (Episode 9 onward):** five children can already become the magnificent dragons they
imagined. Each new अक्षर teaches them how to direct that power together without making any child
less important.

**Underlayer (never on the page — the Sod):** your difference is your gift; learning comes *out* of you when you dare to try. Stated once in `PITCH_v1.md`, never in a line of dialogue.

## Part VI — Craft laws (non-negotiable)

1. **PURE HINDI SCRIPT from the current production phase.** Hindi sluglines, Hindi action, Hindi
   parentheticals, and Devanagari dialogue. Vesper's single in-character English drift is the only
   recurring exception. Earlier English-slugline screenplays remain delivered history. Any English
   captions, dub, or accessibility gloss belong to a separate post-production track; they are not
   written into the Hindi screenplay or spoken by Dadi or the सूत्रधार.
2. **Preschool register.** Short present-tense sentences; ~300 most-common spoken-Hindi words;
   तुम among the little dragons; Dadi warm, never stern.
2a. **PROPER HINDI, complete sentences (author's law, 2026-07-21).** The kids LEARN syntax from
   this show. Every dialogue line is a complete, grammatically correct Hindi sentence — short and
   simple, never chopped staccato fragments (the «काला कुत्ता. मैंने बनाया.» fragment-append style
   is the megalist's Tier-3.6 fragment-drama tell AND bad pedagogy — banned). Model line:
   «पापा, पापा, देखो वो काला कुत्ता। आप एक कुत्ता मेरे लिए घर ले आओ ना।» Warm colloquial, not
   stiff formal. Exceptions: single-letter call-response («क!») and toddler babble already
   delivered through Episode 5. From Episode 7 onward Castor and Leda use short complete sentences;
   the retired name Cheeku is never used.
2b. **Pronoun register (author's law, revised 2026-07-22).** Children address elders (Papa, Dadi,
   any grown-up) with **आप**. Among the kids, and to कालू, use **तुम / तुम्हारा** — NOT the very-
   informal तू/तेरा (too rough for the show). Elders → children: **Papa addresses Kuku with तुम,
   never तू** (author's note 2026-07-21: «कालू तुम्हारा है», not «तेरा»); Dadi's affectionate तू
   stays. Kids among themselves and to the puppy use **तुम / तुम्हारा**. (आप + warm imperative
   «ले आओ ना» is the house register, per the author's example.)
3. **Repetition is required, not a smell.** Call-and-response, the recurring "कुकु का क" button, the same ritual each episode — all intentional. (The mechanical Craft gate is English-token-based and will not fight this on Hindi lines; the model-judged lift is steered by director's notes to *preserve* the simplicity — see below.)
4. **The letter is earned by a beat, never announced cold.** Story first, then the breath, then the lesson.
5. **Safe action.** A villain and urgent rescue may create real child-scale stakes, but never
   frightening cruelty, graphic danger, or shame. Failure changes the situation and invites a
   better choice; it does not humiliate a child.
6. **The Sod is never stated.** Dadi gives one brief, plot-earned नीति because that is the current
   production pattern, but it may name only the practical lesson the action proved. Nobody states
   the hidden underlayer, explains a child's psychology, or turns the ending into a lecture.
7. **Native-speaker check before any episode ships.** Register/spelling/idiom verified by a Hindi native.

## Part VI.6 — Character richness (author's law, 2026-07-22)

**Tics are seasoning, not the meal.** Fyuria is NOT «पहले मैं!» in every scene; Vesper is NOT
"always drifting off" in every scene. Fyuria's `Ф` comparison is reserved for a story that actually
shows that script confusion; it is not a routine tic. Other signatures (Fyuria's «पहले मैं!»,
Vesper's name-calls/English-drift/falling-asleep) may fire **at most once or twice per episode, at a beat
that earns it.** The rest of their lines carry the scene through real want, feeling, and their
underlying person (see VOICE_CARDS: Fyuria = big-hearted leader whose racing is generosity in a
hurry; Vesper = tender seer/inventor who sees clearest by wandering). A character who only ever
says their catchphrase is written wrong. Write the person; deploy the tic like a spice.

## Part VI.5 — The टोपी (hat) principle — limited disambiguation only

The horizontal headline (शिरोरेखा) can help distinguish a genuinely confusing pair. It is a visual
property, not a line of dialogue repeated in every episode.

**Why it's here:** a specific, real confusion — क reads like the Cyrillic **Ф** (the first letter
of Furia's name): both are "a round thing with a vertical line." The fix is not to explain the
foreign letter; it is to point at the hat.

**Current writing law:** no routine टोपी talk, no recurring hatless-to-hat transformation, and no
comparison with another script unless the episode is specifically resolving a demonstrated
confusion such as क versus Cyrillic Ф. Describe the actual letter shape in plain child-facing
language and verify it in the approved Devanagari font.

**Secondary cue (क vs Ф, optional / say-it-to-Furia):** Ф is a ball on a stick, the same on both
sides; क wears a hat and has a little leg — lopsided, like a tiny person, not a ball.

**General rule:** use the smallest cue that resolves the actual confusion. Do not turn that cue into
the series' transformation chant.

## Part VII — How prose is made (respect the engine)

For engine-generated prose, follow `studio/NATIVE_WORKERS.md`: the ReScript orchestrator emits the
exact worker job, one native Codex worker writes the declared response file, and the orchestrator
validates and accepts it. Director notes protect the children's register: simple complete Hindi,
visible action, no adult subtext, and no explanation replacing enactment. Letter teaching remains
governed by `LETTER_MAP.md` and the approved episode design.
