# MOON KING — plotline v1 (engine-generated; old plotline forgotten)

Brief seed: moonking-errgpv6. Fixed anchors kept; Sky King homage kept; old four-old-men/crate plot discarded.

## FIXED SPINE (unchangeable)
1. OPEN: a socialist president is elected.
2. INCITING TURN: the administration announces it will remove the six Apollo flags from the moon.
3. END: a group of Americans hold the most illegal Fourth of July cookout on the moon, defending the flag.

## THE ROLLED TROPES (and how each is used)
- Gaslighting (SUBVERTED): the regime tells the public the flags never mattered and the defenders are dangerous cranks nobody supports — and it FAILS; the "cranks" are vindicated.
- IfYou'reSoEvilEatThisKitten (STRAIGHT): to crew the removal mission you must first prove loyalty on camera — publicly denounce and personally lower a flag. The loyalty test.
- PoorMan'sSubstitute (INVERTED): the mission's crew are the leftovers — backups, ground techs, a washout — the cheap substitutes for real astronauts. They turn out to be the realer thing.
- MutualKill (STRAIGHT): the moon confrontation kills two men at once, together, at the flag. Real cost, real dread.
- RealSongThemeTune (STRAIGHT): "This Land Is Your Land" (Woody Guthrie) as the recurring tune — a real leftist folk song reclaimed by the flag-defenders, sung on the moon. Built-in irony.
- ArtisticLicenseGeography (STRAIGHT, translated — weak/production trope): the regime publishes false moon maps, renames Tranquility Base, lies about where the flags are; the defenders' edge is a man who knows the real geography.
- Prequel (AVERTED, weak/production trope): no origin-flashback; the lead's grief surfaces in present-tense action, never a backstory dump.

## THE LEAD (Sky King figure) + CAST
- VERN TICE, 50s. A launch-ground technician at the Cape — suits and crawlers, never an astronaut, the man who straps others in. Personal grief: his son, an astronaut, died on the last flag-planting mission years ago. Private, never collective. (Sky King: the personal register carries the grief.)
- The GROUP (the "substitutes"): the mission's throwaway crew — a grounded backup pilot, a young idealist welder, an old flight engineer who knows the real moon geography. Textured, specific, not archetypes. Some are here for reasons that have nothing to do with the flag (chaos/texture).
- ADMINISTRATOR CORWIN: the true-believer official running the removal. Not a cartoon — she thinks the flags are an obscenity and their removal is a moral duty, and she's smart and ruthless about it. The real villain.
- WRAY: a young star astronaut, Vern's former trainee, sent up to stop them. Believes in the new order. Half of the MutualKill.

## THE METAPHORICAL FLIP (never interpreted on screen)
Everyone reads taking the flags down as moving FORWARD — maturity, progress, growing past nationalism (the regime's whole frame, and half the country buys it). Shown, not told, the flip lands: taking the flag down is the most BACKWARD act available — iconoclasm, erasing the one time humanity did something purely forward-looking and did it together. The regime thinks it's the future. It's book-burning. The audience arrives at this by watching, never by being told.

## ACT-BY-ACT (beats marked up/down for variance)

ACT 1 — THE ELECTION AND THE ORDER
- [DOWN] Election night. The socialist president wins big on an apology-and-reset platform. For Vern's world, dread; for the country's mood, relief.
- [DOWN] The announcement: a mission will retrieve and retire the six Apollo flags as "symbols of a shameful past." Gaslighting begins — the flags "never meant anything," the outrage is "manufactured."
- [neutral] Vern, at the Cape, hears it. We see his grief without its cause named (withheld why): he keeps a folded flag in a drawer he doesn't open. Do not explain it.
- [DOWN] The loyalty test (EatThisKitten): the prestige astronauts assigned to the mission are made to denounce the flag on camera first. One refuses and is quietly ruined. The message: only true believers fly.

ACT 2A — THE SUBSTITUTES
- [UP, small] Because the real astronauts balked, the mission falls to the leftovers — the backups, the ground crew, the washout (PoorMan'sSubstitute, inverted). Vern engineers his way onto it.
- [UP] The crew bonds; the old engineer knows the real moon maps the regime is falsifying (ArtisticLicenseGeography). Texture, humor, competence.
- [DOWN] Midpoint gut-punch (OhCrap): mid-mission, the crew learns the regime's real plan isn't retrieval — it's to be filmed destroying the flags, live, as a global spectacle of the new order. Corwin is sending Wray up behind them to make sure it happens.

ACT 2B — THE DECISION
- [UP] The turn: the crew decides not to take the flag down but to DEFEND it — plant themselves at Tranquility. The July 4 cookout is born as an act of defiance, not a stunt.
- [DOWN] The regime's counter: the public is told the crew are insurrectionists who've gone rogue; the world is told nobody's with them (gaslighting escalates).
- [WITHHELD WHY PAYS OFF] We learn what the flag is to Vern: his son planted the last one, and died doing it, and the regime's cover-up of that mission is part of why the flags must vanish. His grief and his purpose fuse. (Personal register.)

ACT 3 — THE STANDOFF, THE KILL, THE COOKOUT
- [DOWN, hard] Wray arrives to enforce. The confrontation at the flag: Wray and one of Vern's crew tear each other's suits — both die there, together, at the base of the flag (MutualKill). Vern's former trainee and Vern's friend, gone in the same minute. The dread beat, the real cost. No tidy karma.
- [UP, collective] July 4. The cookout happens on the moon, broadcast live — grills, folding chairs, the flag standing. "This Land Is Your Land" sung over the loop.
- [UP, the subversion] Gaslighting collapses: the regime predicted outrage and isolation; instead the Earth ANSWERS — millions light their own grills and fly their own flags in defiance, coast to coast. The "cranks" were the country. (Sky King: collective register = optimism.)
- [SWERVE — the interesting ending, committed take] Vern came up carrying his son's death and never meant to come home; but he does — mercy arrives as a surprise (Sky King: he lives). The swerve's edge: the victory is real and it is paid for — the cookout is held over two fresh graves in the lunar dust, and the last shot is Vern setting an empty plate at the cookout for his son and for the two dead men, joy and grief in the same frame. Collective optimism, personal grief, never blended into a tidy bow.
