# V14.2 editor read — 2026-07-13

## PUNCH-LIST

1. **[ACT 2 — the AUCTION BARN build sequence, all six+ scenes] The caper never nearly fails, so rooting flatlines under fascination.** 
   WHY: Every test passes on the first try — the physical ("Same result as 1968. All four"), the suits ("they'll hold air by Sunday"), the crate seals ("Needle hasn't moved off it"), the trainer re-purchase, even the Pell inspection. Two "DAYS COMPRESSED" montages explicitly dissolve time, so there is no ticking sub-clock and no cost anywhere between the seizure and the reweigh. We *like* and are *fascinated by* four competent men; we never *root*, because rooting needs jeopardy they never touch.
   FIX: Break one thing on the page and make it cost. Have a suit fail its bubble test the night before load-out and force a choice — go three-man, or send Stitch up with a bad seam he hides (seed his cracked side / the cough). Put a hard date on the build (Frontier's crate-count deadline is "Friday" — make the build run late against it) so at least one Act-2 scene is a race, not a procedure.

2. **[EXT. DAWES FARM doorway → EXT. TRANQUILITY standoff] The mirror is thematic, not staged — the failed doorway stand is never *quoted*.** 
   WHY: In the barn Cricket "steps into the doorway and plants himself square in it, feet set, hands at his sides… 'You'll have to move me, Wade,'" and is walked "three steps to the side. Cricket lets him." On the Moon the hold is real but the payoff line goes to Gunny ("You want 'em? Come through us"), and Cricket's "You'll have to move me" is never echoed. The single most load-bearing arc in the film — Cricket fails to hold, then holds — is diffused across the wrong mouth with no matching posture or cadence.
   FIX: Give Cricket the standoff line and stage it identically. He steps forward, plants "feet set, hands at his sides," and says a variant of "You'll have to move me" to Brandt — then *doesn't* get moved. Let Gunny's spatula and "Come through us" be the second beat, not the first.

3. **[Legion sign-on / grandson's worksheet / fireworks refusal] Cricket's turn into the mission is muted, and his strongest motive never reaches him.** 
   WHY: He refuses twice ("Count me out… I've got a grandson who still thinks I hung the moon") then simply "comes back in" and signs with no voiced reason. Meanwhile the grandson's worksheet — "THE 4TH OF JULY COOKOUT AT GRANDPAS," the tradition marked for retirement — is delivered to *Danny*, who "doesn't call the boy back in," and Cricket is never shown learning it. The one lever built to flip the protagonist is planted and then never connects to his decision.
   FIX: Route the worksheet to Cricket. Danny brings it to the barn; Cricket reads the pencil line, and *that* is the beat that walks him back through the Legion door to sign — the refusal and the re-commitment now share one cause.

4. **[FIRST PLAINS BANK → Danny's line "County's squared"] The personal 60-day clock is set up beautifully, then defused offhand.** 
   WHY: The loan officer's scanner gambit ("That buys you about sixty days") is the film's most concrete private stake and its warmest scene. Then Danny disposes of it in a throwaway — "County's squared, by the way. Paid it Tuesday" — and the only clock the audience can feel personally evaporates before it ever squeezes anyone.
   FIX: Let the debt ride unpaid until the coda so the RANGER pay stub ($4,217.60) actually *clears* the $4,206 — cash the payoff you already planted. Cut or delay Danny's payment; if Danny pays, make it the thing that finally lets Cricket go, not a stakes-eraser.

5. **[FIRST METHODIST church bell] An Act-2 insult with no Act-3 payoff — and the Adams quote literally names it.** 
   WHY: The silenced steeple ("The clock reaches the hour and the bell stays quiet") is one of the sharpest off-space insults in the rage ladder. Yet Gunny reads Adams aloud on the Moon — "with shows, games, sports, guns, bells, bonfires and illuminations" — and no bell is ever rung. The one insult the climax verbally invokes is the one it fails to pay.
   FIX: When the shells break over the flags, cut to Broken Bow — someone climbs the steeple and cuts the canvas off the clapper; the bell rings under the fireworks. Pay "bells" with a bell on Adams's word.

6. **[ROY'S DINER — the ~5 counter scenes] Redundant mood-only beats hitting one note without escalation.** 
   WHY: The humming scene, the free-coffee ticket scenes, the TikTok-kid/courier scene, the burner scenes all play the same chord — "the town quietly holds." None turns (Mamet); they accrue rather than climb. The TikTok kid + burrito courier in particular is texture that stalls momentum and never returns.
   FIX: Collapse to two diner scenes that *escalate*: one early (quiet, humming, meter ticking) and one at the climax (the cook turns every burner up). Cut the TikTok kid or convert him into a payoff — he's filming the diner when the Moon cookout breaks and the room ignores his camera to look at the screen.

7. **[Cemetery / zinnia / empty-net static / Sky King] Solemnity floods the middle and stalls the comedy engine.** 
   WHY: This is a rage-and-release comedy, but a long central stretch goes elegiac — Peg's grave, the pressed zinnia in the kit bag, the barn where "the static runs on for a long time," the near-mythic Sky King parable. Individually lovely; together they drain the native register for an entire movement and make Act 2 read as grief drama, not comedy.
   FIX: Keep the zinnia and Sky King (they earn their weight) but intercut them with the varsity arrogance so the tone never fully lowers — e.g., break the dead-net silence with Gunny opening the fireworks argument again, not with more static. Move one grief beat later so it lands as release, not as a wallow.

8. **[Senate hearings / Hale posts / Vess corporate chess] The B-plot runs insider-legible and rooting-thin, and Hale is tangential to the four.** 
   WHY: The receiver/nationalization maneuvering ("a federal receiver until Unity Day. For stability") is boardroom chess a general audience skims, and Hale's drafts-folder arc ("My COO and the mother of my children just") connects to the old men only by borrowed rocket. His "Go. Fuck. Yourself." is gold, but he triumphs over a fight the four aren't in.
   FIX: Trim the Senate/receiver material to the single load-bearing fact (Pell's signatures certify the cargo) and let Vess's office scene carry the whole payoff. Tie Hale to the four concretely — one line that he *knew* what was in those crates and let the launch fly — so his defiance is complicity, not coincidence.

9. **[Moon standoff / cookout] Dutch's specification voice goes missing exactly where distinctness matters most.** 
   WHY: Cricket (checklist), Gunny (military-sacred), and Stitch (drawl) all get signature climax lines — "We came in the mail," "Come through us," "pack the fuck off." Dutch's specification voice, the sharpest of the four in Act 1 ("Three hundred. It's printed on the box"), has no signature beat in the standoff; he's handed the door-seal engineering only at the very end. The quartet thins to a trio at the peak.
   FIX: Give Dutch one spec-voice line in the standoff that only he could say — cite the exact statute/spec that makes them federal personnel, or read Brandt the archival tolerance he's about to violate by grabbing the pole. Let the specification win an argument on the Moon.

10. **["Earth answers with lights" used twice] The coda declares the emotion the climax already earned.** 
   WHY: The mass porch-light uprising ("light crawls across the black in slow waves") is the film's true emotional summit. The final coda then reuses the identical image — Cricket waves, "one city flickers… rolling west across the night" — so the last beat repeats rather than caps, and the Ranger coda stacks pay stub + park sign + ghost-file + wave until the ending is announcing its payoffs instead of landing one.
   FIX: Spend the Earth-answers image once. If the porch-light uprising keeps it, end the coda on something smaller and single — Cricket's glove up, no answer, then one lone light. Or hold the Earth-answer for the coda and make the uprising a *sound* cue (horns, the diner) so each beat owns a distinct payoff.
