# THE POKER SCENE (v1) — the seed scene of the pilot novel

## THE DECISIONS (made before writing, per Layer 0)

WORLD: present-day naval aviators, a Super Hornet squadron, home game at the protagonist's kitchen table on a Friday. ACT POSITION: end of Act One — the doorway of no return; this scene closes a door forever. AFTERMATH (governs the ending): he will not explode; he will begin, tonight, coldly, to plan the killing of his wife and his friend — so the scene must end in control, the rage turning inward, the mask staying on. THE SUSPICION'S CAUSE: two weeks ago, an old iPad still synced to his wife's deleted messages — a man who knows when Hank flies nights; someone inside the wire; someone at a table like this one. THE PLANT (established earlier in the novel, innocent when planted): Dana's Thursday breath-practice course — yoga nidra, a teacher named Renata, and the course phrase Dana has carried home all year: "inner stillness." THE MECHANISM: the baited error — Hank mangles the term and the wrong return day on purpose, and waits to see who corrects him. THE ACTIVITY AS METAPHOR: Tommy has bluffed Hank all night the way he's bluffed him all year; the moment Hank sees the affair, he can suddenly read every hand — because now he knows what Tommy's face does when it lies to him. WHAT CHANGES FOREVER: Hank stops being a husband at this table and becomes a man running a targeting problem.

## THE SCENE

Tommy took the third pot off him a little after ten, and showed the bluff. He didn't have to show it. He turned over the seven-deuce and spread his hands, and Griz banged the table so the chips jumped, and Hank pushed the cards away and said, deal.

They called Hank "Kodak" because he remembered everything. Fuel states from a hop two years ago. What the weather had been over Fallon on a Tuesday. It was squadron legend and a squadron joke, and it was worth exactly nothing at this table, because you can't remember a man into showing you his cards.

Deuce dealt. Griz talked. Griz always talked, about the det schedule, about his truck, about the new skipper's mustache, a big bearded warrant of a man who flew back seat and played cards the way he drove, loud and straight ahead and honest. Deuce folded early and watched everything with his chin in his hand. Tommy sat easy in the chair by the window, sleeves pushed up, stacking and restacking a short tower of blue chips, and being Tommy, so Griz kept handing him things and Deuce laughed at his jokes half a second early.

Dana's magnets were on the refrigerator behind Tommy's head. A calendar. A dentist card. A little laminated om symbol from the studio, which Hank had made a joke about in March and had stopped seeing by June.

Hank had not planned to do it tonight. For fourteen days the plan had been nothing at all, which was its own full-time job, standing watch on his own house and his own phone, flying twice a week and grease-penciling the sortie like a man with an intact life. But the iPad was in his flight bag in the truck, and the man in those messages knew what nights Hank flew, and there were only so many tables in the world where that man could be sitting.

So between hands, shuffling, not looking at anybody, he did it.

You're on your own for chow next Friday, he said. Dana's got her guru weekend up in Ojai. The sleep yoga thing. Needra, nadra. Whatever it is. Back Sunday.

Saturday, she was coming back Saturday. He squared the deck and dealt and did not look up, and the word sat out there among the chips.

Costs four hundred dollars to lie on a floor, Griz said. My wife saw it on the internet. Four hundred dollars, and you bring your own blanket.

That's the racket, Deuce said. Call.

Tommy didn't say anything. Tommy raised, and won that one too.

Two hands later, Tommy handed it to him. Tommy was raking a small pot, feeling good, three beers deep, and Griz was going on about stress, how his neck had been one solid knot since the workups started, and Tommy laughed and said:

That's 'cause you got no inner stillness, Griz. You want the nidra. Fixes the neck right up.

He had said it clean, the d soft, the same sound as the recordings Dana played in the car. And nobody in a ready room says inner stillness. Hank knew one voice that did, and it practiced on Thursday nights.

Nobody at the table heard anything. Griz said if lying on a floor fixed necks the Navy would issue floors. Deuce pushed his blinds in. Hank looked at his hole cards and the numbers on them, Then a thing he knew from the jet happened. The picture built. Every piece already in his possession climbed into formation and held there, wing-steady. March, when Tommy started saying he liked the drive up from Lemoore. The det in the spring Tommy came off early, bad back, three weeks on the beach while the squadron flew nights. Nights. The iPad. Saturday.

Hank folded the hand and got up and got two beers and set one by Tommy's elbow, and his hand was level doing it, and he noted that levelness from a small distance, a good instrument in bad air.

Then he sat down and started winning.

It was not luck and he didn't let it look like anything. But the man across the table had given him nothing all night, and now Hank couldn't stop reading him, because he finally knew what that face did when it lied to him. It was the same face that had been eating dinner in this kitchen for a year, asking was there anything Dana needed hauled up to the studio. Twice more Tommy pushed at pots with nothing behind the push, and both times Hank sat quiet and called him down to the felt and took it. On the second one Tommy laughed and said okay, okay, and there was a second where his eyes came up to make sure it was still Friday night, still Hank, and Hank smiled back with a mouth that worked perfectly.

Kodak runs hot late, Griz said. Every damn time.

They played till the case beer was gone. At the door Tommy put a hand on Hank's shoulder and said something about next week, and Hank agreed to it, whatever it was. He stood in the driveway while the trucks backed out, and waved.

Then he went in and washed the glasses and set them in the rack. He wiped the table down, racked the chips by color, and stood a while in front of the refrigerator with the dish towel over his shoulder.

The calendar hung under Dana's magnet. He moved the magnet and smoothed the page. Friday, poker, his own handwriting. Sunday, D home, her loop on the D.

Saturday, blank.

He had two hops next week, a fam flight Tuesday and a night trap Thursday. He stood there and laid it out the way he'd been taught to lay out any strike, time on target working backward, and the part of him that was screaming he put in the back seat, where it could watch.

He turned off the kitchen light at eleven forty. The om magnet caught the streetlamp through the window, and he looked at it a good while, learning it as a threat system: emitter, range, kill radius.

Then he went up to bed on the right side, his side, and lay with his eyes open, still as anything.
