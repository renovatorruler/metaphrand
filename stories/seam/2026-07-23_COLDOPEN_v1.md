# UNTITLED CIVIL WAR SHOW — cold open v1 (Sheridan register; reveal ladder per the author's spec)

---

EXT. RIVER TRENCH - NIGHT

Rain. A trench cut into bottomland mud, half flooded, shored with pallet wood and car doors. A soldier bails water with his helmet and pours it over the parapet like a man emptying a boat he has stopped believing in.

Somewhere above the cloud, a small motor whines. Nobody looks up anymore.

A machine gun team works the parapet. HUTCH, 30s, runs the gun. DEWEY, 20s, feeds it. Their uniforms are green-on-green, sleeves banded with green duct tape. Down the line, more green tape, more wet men.

Mortars land on the far bank in pairs. A radio crackles against the mud wall.

RADIO (V.O.)
Drop five zero. Fire for effect.

The next pair lands closer to the river. On the far bank something catches fire and burns small and stubborn in the rain.

HUTCH
(into the radio)
Good splash. You're on the wire now.

He settles back behind the gun. Waits. The night comes down to rain sounds and the river running high and black past the wire.

DEWEY
My cousin got out through Memphis. Says they got power all day down there. Air conditioning in the churches.

HUTCH
Churches always had air conditioning.

DEWEY
Not like this. He says it's cold enough to hang meat.

A flare goes up two positions north. Dies. Nobody reacts.

DEWEY (CONT'D)
What's the first thing you do?

HUTCH
When?

DEWEY
After.

Hutch thinks about it longer than the question deserves. Rain runs off the brim of his helmet in a little rope.

HUTCH
My old man's got a dock at Percy Priest he never finished. Decking's stacked in the carport, gone gray by now. I'm gonna finish his dock, and I'm gonna sit on the end of it with one beer, and I'm not gonna talk to anybody for about a month.

DEWEY
That's it? That's the whole plan?

HUTCH
I'm gonna drink the beer slow.

DEWEY
Man, I'm going straight down to Broadway. First honky-tonk still standing, I don't care which.

HUTCH
Nashville's gonna be full of people just like you. Line around the block to feel something.

The radio crackles. A different voice now, tired brass.

RADIO (V.O.)
Gun three, Gun three. Actual says send the Kentucky Special.

Dewey comes up off the mud grinning like a kid let out of school.

DEWEY
It's Christmas.

BEHIND THE TRENCH - CONTINUOUS

Four men wrestle two fifty-five gallon drums to the mouth of a flooded ditch. The ditch runs down the levee's face toward the far positions, carrying rainwater fast. Det cord loops the drums like ribbon on a present. A CORPORAL checks the current with a stick, then just his eyes.

CORPORAL
Current'll take 'em right down their door. Fuse 'em and let go.

They light and shove. The drums ride the ditch water away into the dark, spinning slow, and are gone.

A count. Ten. Twenty.

Downstream, the night turns orange. A whump rolls back up the levee, then the fire spreads low and wide, burning ON the water, running the flooded trench line like something alive and looking for the door.

From the green-tape line, whooping. Men beating on helmets. They cheer it the way you cheer a ballgame, and the fire keeps working.

HUTCH
(quiet, to the gun)
Merry Christmas.

EXT. FAR BANK TRENCH - CONTINUOUS

The other side. Same American gear, same rain, sleeves banded in brown tape. Men drag a burned man backward through the mud while another beats fire out of the pallet shoring with a wet blanket.

SGT. ODELL, 40s, built like a fireplug, stands in the light of his own burning trench with his jaw working.

ODELL
Kentucky Special. They rolled us the goddamn Kentucky Special.

SOLDIER
Barrel-aged, Sarge.

ODELL
Shut up.
(then, deciding)
Go wake up Beaumont. Tell him the Nashville boys wanna play. We're sending the Nasty.

BEHIND THE FAR TRENCH - MOMENTS LATER

A gray F-150 sits under a shredded tarp. The windshield is cased in welded rebar. The bed rides low on its springs, packed with drums and sandbags, wired tight.

BEAUMONT, 50s, no helmet, sleep still on him, walks the truck like a horse trader. He ties the wheel off with paracord. Wedges a brick to the pedal with a ratchet strap. Reaches in and drops it into drive with the tenderness of a man closing a casket.

The truck rolls out onto the old highway approach, gathering speed toward the span, one headlight burning, rain silver in the beam.

BEAUMONT
(watching it go)
Go on, girl.

EXT. RIVER TRENCH - CONTINUOUS

Dewey sees it first, the one headlight swinging onto the bridge, coming steady.

DEWEY
Truck. TRUCK ON THE SPAN.

The whole line opens up. Tracers pour into it, hood, cab, tires. Sparks jump off the rebar. The truck takes all of it and comes on, sandbags eating the fire, engine screaming somewhere under the weight.

HUTCH
Hit the block. HIT THE BLOCK.

A rocket team fires. The backblast lifts trench water into steam. The rocket clips the bed rail and tumbles off burning into the river.

The truck comes off the span onto the levee road.

HUTCH (CONT'D)
Down. DOWN.

WHITE.

Then sound coming back wrong. Mud raining into the trench in clods. A wall of the parapet gone, car doors flung like cards, the wire dragged flat for forty yards. Dewey against the trench wall with his mouth moving and no words arriving, or none that can be heard.

Hutch drags himself up the mud. His ears ring one long note. He looks over what used to be the parapet.

The rain has slacked. First gray light on the water. The far bank sits quiet, burned in one long stripe, and the river runs between the two lines the way it ran before anybody had an opinion about it.

The radio, half buried in mud, crackles. An open channel. A voice from the other side, Southern, easy, not old.

FAR BANK VOICE (V.O.)
How about that, Third. Y'all still with us?

Hutch stares at the radio. Doesn't touch it.

FAR BANK VOICE (V.O.) (CONT'D)
Hutchins. I know that's your gun. Heard you on the net all week.
(a beat)
You still owe Beaumont for that generator.

Hutch sits down in the mud with his back against the trench wall. Above him the little motor whines past again, headed somewhere with somebody's morning in it.

EXT. THE CONFLUENCE - CAIRO, ILLINOIS - DAWN

Wide and high for the first time. Two rivers meet at a drowned point of land, brown water folding into brown water. Trenchworks stitch both banks. The dead town behind the levee. The dropped span.

In the shallows, a green interstate highway sign stands hip-deep in floodwater, shot through and through:

CAIRO 1 — PADUCAH 44 — NASHVILLE 174

Hold on it. The river does not care.

SMASH TO BLACK.
