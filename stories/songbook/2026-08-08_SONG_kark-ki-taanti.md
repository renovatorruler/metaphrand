# «कर्क की तांती» — the cancer song (standalone, general)

Drafted 2026-08-08 from the author's direction: a general cancer song through the Tejaji lens — a follower trying to save someone from a snakebite that is actually कर्क. Not written for any particular listener's ears (the living-elegy constraints of rule 8 deliberately NOT applied, per the author).

**The linguistic ground.** Cancer in Hindi is कर्क रोग — the crab disease, same etymology as the Latin. The song never says "cancer"; it says कर्क and means both animal and disease at once, to every listener, for free.

**Situation.** A devotee arrives at the देवरा with the तांती, doing everything the snakebite-god's law requires — and can't find the bite. No fang marks, no wound; the bleeding is on the inside. He names the animal: not a snake — a crab. A snake strikes and flees; this thing grips and stays, and never walks straight. His quarrel: your name binds snakes — who binds this one? Even the doctors are doing your work (poison against poison) without knowing you. The turn is the myth remembered honestly: **Tejaji didn't survive his bite either — what he got, and all he ever asked, was time enough to keep his word.** So the prayer collapses from "cure him" to the one thing this deity actually grants: he has one promise pending — give him that much time. And the तांती is retied to what it always bound: not the venom, the word.

**Laws check.** Drama: the singer wants TIME from the god, argued line by line. One governing figure: the wrong animal / the jurisdiction gap. One turn, discovered late. Epigram budget: the टेक («बोल पूरो करण जितरो टेम») and the final line, nothing else quotable on purpose. तू/थूँ to the deity is bhakti-legitimate (law 5). No inventory — the zoology is contrast in argument, not description.

## NOTES (v3)

- **The fang-marks payoff (author, 2026-08-09):** the hospital verse now answers the song's opening demand. The devotee asked «दाँत रा निशान बता» in the first verse and found none; the answer arrives late — the only tooth marks on the body are the needle's, set daily by the बड़ा वैद, raising the venom slowly. The chemo metaphor never says chemo: the needles are fangs, the drip is venom, and the physicians do the snake-god's own work «थने पूछ्या बिना» — without asking him. Question planted in Segment 1, answered in Segment 2: the extend structure makes the payoff land a full movement later, which is the point of extending.


- **The v2 synthesis (author-directed, 2026-08-09):** two elements folded into the original spine. (1) The लीलण homage — the voice shifts once, to the sick man himself, honoring the partner/caregiver who carried him: Tejaji's Lilan was a mare, his is this person. Her line is his description of her, not an aphorism: she never chose the road, and she never stumbled. (2) The भादवा ending — the pending word is DISCOVERED to be the fair-vow itself: «अगले भादवे फेर आऊँला», sworn each year at the मेला. Keeping it mints next year's vow — a self-renewing promise, which is why the ask («let him reach Bhadva») can be granted again and again. The तांती is retied annually; the final whisper carries the vow.
- लीलण is grammatically feminine (the mare), so the caregiver rides in the feminine (या / आवेली) regardless of the person's gender — the metaphor's gender, not the person's. Swap to यो/आवेलो if the author wants a male caregiver named instead.
- The patient stays unnamed; the quarrel verse is the उपालंभ (complaint-to-god) genre, native to bhakti folk — the devotee is allowed to argue.
- Epigram budget note: the टेक («बोल पूरो करण जितरो टेम»), the vow («अगले भादवे फेर आऊँला»), and the renewal line («हर साल पूरो होवे — हर साल फेर बंधे») — the last two function as one unit (the turn and its consequence), which keeps the song at the lawful one-टेक-one-turn. If the author's ear says it's one aphorism too many, the renewal line is the one to sacrifice.
- Not yet checked by a native ear. Flag: ले'र / उठा'र / पकड़'र contractions; आवाँला / आवेली / बाँधेलो / देवेलो / आऊँला future forms; मिलाँगा vs मिलाँला; अर (and); पिछाणे.

## V2 RECORDING — rhyme brief (author, 2026-08-16; NOT applied to the v1 master)

The song's governing word, कर्क, currently rhymes with nothing. Rajasthani/Hindi offers a
tight -र्क family that is entirely untapped: **अर्क · फर्क · तर्क · नर्क**. This brief is for a
future re-recording; the v1 master and everything timed to it (seam map, slot structure,
lip-sync windows, subtitle cues, the assembled cut) stay untouched.

**अर्क — the strongest, and the least obvious.** An extract or distillate; in Ayurvedic and
Unani usage specifically a medicinal distillate. Belongs in the physicians verse, where the
needle's fang sets in daily and the venom is raised slowly. What hangs in the drip bag *is*
an अर्क. The rhyme and the object are the same word.

**फर्क — carries the naming verse's thesis.** That verse already argues the difference: a
snake strikes and flees, this one grips and stays, it never walks straight. फर्क means
difference. Placing it where the argument already lives makes sound and sense arrive together.

**तर्क — suits the quarrel.** The song is an उपालंभ, a formal complaint to the deity, and तर्क
is precisely what the devotee mounts when he asks who binds this animal's name.

**नर्क — hold in reserve.** Powerful, but the song's discipline is dryness; it never wails.
Hell is the one rhyme that would announce itself and risk the melodrama the song has avoided.

**साँप stays; do not swap to सर्प.** सर्प shares कर्क's र्-conjunct architecture and does chime
where साँप's open vowels do not — the author's ear on this is right. But साँप is the folk word
and this is a village bard in Malwi, where सर्प reads bookish. Take the chiming from the rhyme
family instead. Keep **नाग** reserved for the deity's snake in «ले, नाग जी», which keeps the two
songs distinct.

Also carried forward to v2: the honorific question (तेजा vs तेजाजी — see the native-ear flags
above), still unresolved and worth settling before a re-record.
