# «रघुवीर की तान» — one voice, then three, then five

**STATUS: SUPERSEDED / AUTHOR-REJECTED DRAFT v1 — TOO STRUCTURAL, FORCED, AND WITHOUT A LIVE COMIC SITUATION. DO NOT RENDER.**

The replacement direction is [«नाम हैं या गीत?»](2026-08-02_SONG_naam-hain-ya-geet.md). This file remains only as craft history: equal name weighting was the right safeguard, but the one–three–five device turned the family into a chart rather than a song.

## Dramatic design

An old field bard addresses his own instrument and asks who first raised the family's refrain. The string answers «रघुवीर», then gives three names, then five, always in generational and age order. The singer's pressure-bearing question is not which man best embodies his name, but which voice is the life of the song. The turn refuses a ranking: every voice retains its own name and sound; that plurality is what makes the inherited song possible.

The cleverness is structural—one founder, three sons, five grandsons—rather than nine forced etymologies. «कुबेर» is treated only as a proper name. There is no wealth, money, treasure, prosperity, abundance, joke, reversal, or consolation attached to it. «रणछोड़» and «रणजीत» are likewise kept intact and are not contrasted as retreat versus victory. There is no Gita passage or spoken coda.

## PERSONA / TAKE STRATEGY

- Make the first diagnostic take without a Persona so the conversational question-and-answer and equal name spacing can establish themselves.
- Then test the saved «उठ रे जुझार» Malwa Bard Persona only if it preserves warmth and restraint. Reject it if it makes the names martial, devotional, mournful, or hierarchically climactic.
- Generate at least three seeds. Choose the take in which every proper name is intelligible and receives equal weight, not the cleanest pitch.
- Reject any take that turns «कुबेर» into a grand or comic flourish, or that crescendos from «रणछोड़» toward «रणजीत» or from «कपिल» toward «विक्रांत».
