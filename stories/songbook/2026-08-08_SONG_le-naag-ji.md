# «ले, नाग जी» — the Tejaji track (माटी माँगे, AMAL-coupled)

Filed into the songbook 2026-08-08 from the author's paste. The album doc still listed Tejaji under NEXT; this is the draft, recovered and now on paper. Referred to by its closing whisper — «तांती बाँध ले».

**Situation.** Tejaji has kept his word to the snake and come back to be bitten, having first freed the cows. His body is already ruined — every part of it spent on some other promise — so the snake cannot find clean skin to bite. The song is the negotiation that follows: a dying man auditing his own body, part by part, until he finds the one piece still whole, which is the tongue — the organ he made the promise with. He offers it. The clock is the real antagonist: if he bleeds out before the snake bites, the word is broken by accident.

**Why it works (the album's own law).** The singer wants something from an addressee — he wants the snake to *hurry up and take him*. Not one line is inventory. The governing figure is single and it argues: a body itemised as debts already paid, and the last unmortgaged thing given away on purpose. The turn is «पण जीभ बाकी है» — the discovery that what survived is exactly what was promised with. One टेक («बोल्यो हो तो आयो हूँ»), one earned turn, no second aphorism.

## LYRICS (paste as-is)

```
[Intro: lone ravanhatta, dry morchang pulse, no drums]

[Verse: arriving on his knees, out of breath]
आयो हूँ रे नाग... बोल्यो हो तो आयो हूँ।
गायाँ छुड़ाई — अब थारी बारी।

[Verse: the snake, dry and flat]
कठे मारूँ थने? सारो देह लोहू।
साफ जगह बता — जठे दाँत धरूँ।

[Verse: giving his body away, piece by piece]
यो हाथ चोराँ को — वे ले ग्या।
यो सीनो कटार को — वा ले गी।
यो पीठ रस्ता की — वो ले ग्यो।

[Verse: the offer]
पण जीभ बाकी है। जीभ साफ है।
जिण सूँ बोल्यो हो — वा ही तो बची।
ले, नाग जी — दाँत जीभ पे धर।

[Verse: urgent, the clock]
जल्दी कर... लोहू ढलतो जावे।
दूजी मौत पहले आ गी — तो बोल झूठो पड़ जावे।

[The vow returns, quieter, meaning finish it]
बोल्यो हो तो आयो हूँ।

[Instrumental: one bar, the ravanhatta alone]

[Final whisper, barely audible]
तांती बाँध ले... और बोल मत तोड़।

[Silence]
```

## GLOSS (never rendered)

I have come, O snake — I gave my word, so I have come. I freed the cows; now it is your turn. / (The snake, flat:) Where am I to bite you? Your whole body is blood. Show me a clean place to set my teeth. / This hand belonged to the thieves — they took it. This chest to the dagger — she took it. This back to the road — it took it. / But the tongue is left. ## NOTES

- **The तांती in the last line does double work.** It is the literal protective thread devotees tie in Tejaji worship — the snakebite-protector's own emblem — and, spoken by the dying man to the listener, it converts the myth into an instruction: wear the thread, keep the word. The address turns outward on the final line, the same move «उठ रे जुझार» makes.
- **Consistent with MALWA.md §5**, which fixes the metaphor: the tongue is the word; integrity is not what survives the damage but the thing spent last, on purpose; and the keeping *is* the dying, not a route to a reward.
- **Not yet checked by a native ear.** The Malvi/Marwari forms to flag: कठे · जठे · सूँ · ले ग्या/ले गी/ले ग्यो gender agreement across the three debts, and ढलतो जावे.
