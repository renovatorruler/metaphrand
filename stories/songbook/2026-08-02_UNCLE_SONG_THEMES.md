# The uncle song — requirements and theme exploration

**STATUS: THEME HISTORY / ALTERNATIVE BANK (2026-08-02).** «King and Rain» was rendered and rejected semantically. The active lyric is now [«एक आँगन में दो राजा» — the king, the fort, and काल](2026-08-02_SONG_ek-aangan-do-raja.md). The other themes below remain alternatives.

## What the song must do

This is a song **for** the author's living uncle, not a song that narrates or invents his life. He is the feared patriarch of the family, not a benevolent-protector figure. The song must not falsify him into a saint, and it must not confuse fear with affection or respect.

The listener will hear the dedication, so:

- no illness, death announcement, farewell, departure, waiting, or plea for more time;
- no disguised goodbye furniture: setting sun, departing bird, river crossing, extinguishing lamp, falling leaf, final harvest, or inherited flame;
- no invented biography, clan, habits, virtues, or family memories;
- no automatic claims of shelter, generosity, tenderness, or moral leadership;
- present-tense or present-habitual action;
- one governing image that argues, rather than a list of compliments;
- one memorable refrain and, at most, one earned turn;
- dignity without dishonesty: the song may recognize magnitude, authority, will, or force of presence without praising fear itself;
- the nirgun reference supplies the **method** (plain image, direct question, spiritual turn, austere delivery), not a borrowed image of death or departure.

## Superseded direction — King and Rain

### Core proposition

Two sovereign forces occupy one courtyard. The human king commands the court; rain crosses every human boundary without asking. Rain does not humiliate the king, and the king does not conquer rain. The deeper question is where any person's authority ends.

The dignified turn: a ruler secure in his own domain does not need the whole sky to obey him. Making room for the one thing that does not fear him is strength, not defeat.

### Dramatic design

1. **Singer:** an unnamed nirgun bard at the edge of a court.
2. **Addressee:** first the court/king, then the rain itself; the address must remain clear in the finished lyric.
3. **Want:** the singer asks who has authority to admit, stop, or command the rain.
4. **Resistance:** court language assumes everything must arrive with permission, a seal, or an order.
5. **Turn:** there are two kinds of sovereignty in the courtyard, and neither is made smaller by the other.

### Tone boundaries

- Formidable, dry, and faintly amused; never sentimental.
- The rain is an equal, not an executioner, omen, cleanser of sin, or symbol of tears.
- The king is not mocked, converted, redeemed, or suddenly made gentle.
- Avoid king-versus-beggar equations; they turn the dedication into a public correction.
- Avoid a narrative in which the king performs an invented gracious act.

### Hook test — working, not approved

The first hook should revolve around this question:

> **दरबार में दो राजा — किसका कहाँ तक राज?**  
> *Two kings in one court — how far does each one's rule extend?*

This is the conceptual टेक, not yet a finished refrain. It has the required pressure, contains no plea, and allows the verses to keep testing the boundary between command and the uncommanded.

Possible title territory: **«दो राजा»**, **«कहाँ तक राज»**, or **«राजा और बरखा»**. None is locked.

#### PERSONA / TAKE STRATEGY

- First make one take **without** a Persona so the open-field distance and fragile performance can establish themselves.
- Make a second take with the saved «उठ रे जुझार» Malwa Bard Persona only if it preserves the distant untrained voice; reject it if it pulls the song toward martial force or a clean close vocal.
- Make at least three seeds. Keep the take with the most believable field, pauses, and questions—not the take with the most accurate pitch.
- A few wind fluctuations, bow scrapes, uneven entrances, and slight overload are desired evidence of the physical performance. Do not clean them away.

### What this proof is testing

- **«एक आँगन में दो राजा»** gives the song one visible situation rather than a list of ideas.
- **«किसका कहाँ तक राज?»** is the pressure-bearing टेक: a question, not a compliment or plea.
- **«एक के आगे शीश झुकें»** acknowledges commanded deference without naming it love or respect.
- **«एक भिगोए ताज»** lets rain touch authority without taking it away.
- The turn explicitly refuses a contest for the crown. Rain asks about the boundary of power; it does not announce the king's defeat.

### Known risks before expansion

- «एक भिगोए ताज» may still feel too much like a public correction; the author's reaction decides it.
- The two administrative words, «चौकी» and «अर्ज़ी», may make the language feel constructed rather than inevitable.
- Verse 1 uses the closed-fort test, which is dramatically clear but may be more राज-दरबार folk tale than nirgun bhajan.
- Do not add more philosophical conclusions. The draft already spends its one epigram on «किसका कहाँ तक राज?» and its turn on rain not wanting the crown.

#### What changed and why

- The opening now names both participants immediately: a king in court and rain arriving uncalled.
- The guard's failed challenge makes “permission” a visible event instead of an abstract idea.
- The refrain locates the two powers physically—king on the throne, rain on the roof—before asking about the reach of rule.
- The king now asks what the rain wants, and the rain answers. The listener no longer has to invent the thesis.
- The last two lines make the song's proposition explicit: the king's crown and court are real, but the earth does not belong to one person.
- This clarity also exposes the creative truth: if that final proposition feels wrong as a dedication, then «King and Rain» is the wrong theme. Hiding the proposition again would only recreate v0.2's failure.

## Alternative themes — retained for later exploration

### Mountain and Road

The mountain gives no order, yet every road alters itself around it. This recognizes immovability and force of presence without claiming kindness. Risk: it can celebrate stubbornness or turn static.

### Crown and Mirror

The crown demands recognition of rank; the mirror can only return a human face. This is intimate and nirgun-sharp. Risk: it can feel like a public rebuke or a lesson in humility.

### Fort and Sparrow

Everyone approaches the fort carefully; a sparrow lands on its wall without knowing it should be afraid. This introduces tenderness without falsely making the fort tender. Risk: it can become whimsical or sentimental.

### The Uncommanded Note

An order can move a room, but it cannot order music to touch the heart. The song itself becomes the one visitor that does not come from fear. Risk: the metaphor is abstract and may become self-congratulatory.

### Shield and Sword

The sword receives visible glory; the shield's work is what never reaches those behind it. Retained only if protection is established as a true family fact. Risk: it currently assigns an unverified virtue and can turn illness into battle imagery.

### Tree at Noon / Well and Thirst / Roof and Storm

These shelter-and-generosity themes remain available only if the author supplies a true basis for them. They are not current candidates because they would presently romanticize the listener.
