# ECHO AND THE BROKEN BONDS

## Eight-frame playable scene brief — lowest-budget internal validation

**Status:** Ready for storyboard production after owner approval.

**Authority:** This is a derivative production brief. The current series premise,
character bible, six-episode arc, and Episode 1 remain binding. If this brief
conflicts with any of those files, the story package wins and the conflict must be
reported rather than silently repaired.

**Project boundary:** This is a standalone project. Do not import characters,
terminology, lore, powers, visual identities, or mechanics from any other project.

**Current authorization:** Write and review this brief only. Do not build the
prototype, generate artwork, generate paid audio, host a new service, or conduct
child testing until the owner explicitly requests that next action.

---

## The decision this prototype is meant to support

We are not trying to prove that seven-year-olds have learned lambda calculus.
That requires later work and is outside this phase.

This prototype has one smaller job:

> Show whether the opening hook, silent story clarity, and first transition from
> cartoon rescue into player action feel worth developing.

This eight-frame slice cannot honestly validate Episode 1 or the season as a
story. It stops before Echo's defining choice to destroy Blaze's captain score in
order to reach Tavi. Because story is the project's first priority, Stage 1 also
includes a four-panel rough **story-payoff coda** described below. The coda costs
almost nothing, receives no polished art, and prevents the team from mistaking a
good opening interaction for a proven episode.

After watching and touching the scene, an adult reviewer should be able to say:

1. Echo wanted a place on the team but hid instead of asking.
2. Knot altered a complete plan before Blaze ran it.
3. Echo had to reveal himself to stop the emergency.
4. The glowing Bond showed ownership; a matching symbol did not.
5. The Linkkeeper supplied a whole pack to the plan, and Blaze physically carried
   the result into the rescue.
6. Reaching the brake solved the immediate problem and caused the next one.

If those six facts are not visible, more polish does not solve the problem.

## What we are making

A **70–90 second landscape playable storyboard** for a browser or tablet.

It is closer to a moving comic than a finished cartoon:

- eight illustrated story states;
- restrained pans, pushes, parallax, light, steam, and camera shake;
- one interaction in two legal parts: reveal the Bond, then Deploy one complete
  pack;
- a few story sounds and at most eight short story lines, plus two optional
  assistance lines;
- no lip-syncing, character locomotion system, 3D world, menus, points, lives,
  collectibles, level map, or game economy.

The first production round is cheaper still: eight rough monochrome panels, four
rough story-payoff coda panels, and one polished reference frame. No other
polished asset should be produced until those pass.

Frames 5 and 6 each need a small **before / player action / after** state strip.
Those are vector-overlay variants of the same painted composition, not additional
illustrated scenes. This keeps the formal change inspectable without increasing
the background-art budget.

## What it should feel like

Echo watches four heroes receive the place he wants while he is literally absent
from view. A hidden hand then changes one connection inside the launch machinery.
Blaze runs the altered plan and a tram carrying Tavi tears away from its guidance
rail. Echo tries warning Blaze while hidden; it does not work. He throws himself
onto Blaze's public mark and becomes visible in front of everyone.

The player sees what only Echo can reveal: one gold Bond running past an attractive
false destination to the east brake. The player follows the real relationship,
places Blaze's whole Speed Pack beside the plan, and Deploys it. Blaze becomes the
answer in the world—he rockets to the brake and catches the tram. Then two bolts
shear from the still-connected middle coupling and the rear car swings wider.
Blaze looks back at Echo: “Next problem.”

There is no lesson screen. The rescue is the explanation.

## The concrete visual bar

### The Mini Force rescue-frame comparison

Before producing polished art, create a six-cell comparison sheet:

- Top row: three screenshots from one official *Mini Force* rescue sequence the
  family already knows—one emotional setup, one civilian-in-danger shot, and one
  decisive hero-action shot.
- Bottom row: Frames 1, 4, and 5 from this brief at exactly the same display size.

If no family-favorite sequence has been named, use the licensed
[*Mini Force* S01:E01 — “New Heroes / New Threat”](https://tubitv.com/tv-shows/200164631/s01-e01-new-heroes-new-threat)
as the provisional source. The reference is for internal comparison only. It
does not authorize copying characters, costumes, vehicles, shots, or franchise
design language.

This comparison plays the same role that real *Call of Duty* screenshots played
in *Claude of Duty*: it prevents the team from congratulating itself on an image
that is technically complete but emotionally dead. The relevant standard is not
equal rendering expense. It is whether a frozen image instantly reads as a real
action-rescue story rather than an educational diagram.

### How every visual round is judged

Show each image for two seconds at 320 × 180 pixels, with captions and controls
hidden. A round passes only if all of these are true:

1. **One focus:** The eye lands immediately on Echo, Tavi, or Blaze—not on the
   interface or scenery.
2. **One readable danger:** A reviewer can point to what will happen if nobody
   acts.
3. **One readable intention:** Body direction and staging show what the hero is
   trying to do.
4. **Emotional face and posture:** Echo's fear of being seen, Tavi's danger, and
   Blaze's urgency survive at thumbnail size.
5. **Broadcast-frame test:** Beside the *Mini Force* reference, the frame belongs
   to the same broad category—an exciting children's rescue show—not a worksheet,
   dashboard, or toy advertisement.
6. **Originality:** Nothing looks traced, recolored, or derived from the reference
   franchise.

The mechanic has a separate non-negotiable gate. Art scores cannot compensate
for a false transformation.

## The eight frames

The times are targets, not animation obligations. At either interaction, the
physical danger feels urgent but the state holds safely until the player acts.
There is no failure countdown.

### Frame 1 — The place Echo does not get

**Target:** 0:00–0:10

**What we see:** Four bright formation marks wait inside a clean boundary. An
empty fifth socket sits just outside their rhythm. A loose green chameleon mark
floats toward it in an invisible hand. Nearby, an ordinary tool tightens Blaze's
loose launch clamp while the same unseen wrist holds the green mark. Blaze
approaches; both hand and mark retreat into camouflage. The council chime rings;
the boundary seals; the fifth socket dims. The invisible hand lowers the green
mark.

**What this must communicate without words:** Echo wanted to stand there. He
helped while hidden, waited to be invited, and has been left outside.

**Cheap movement:** Slow push toward the green mark; one boundary-closing glow;
the mark drops a few inches.

**Sound:** One soft tool click, then the council chime. No narration.

**Pass condition:** A muted viewer can point to the excluded place and the unseen
person holding its mark.

### Frame 2 — The crossed plan

**Target:** 0:10–0:17

**What we see:** Inside a narrow conduit, only Knot's gloved hand and Threader are
visible. The fixed guidance lead remains connected to the live tram bus. Knot
moves one already-existing braided Bond from the demonstration Role Spot to the
pre-existing tram override, caps the abandoned demonstration spot, and closes the
new connection with an angular violet stitch. One of six spools goes dark. A
violet tail remains caught beneath a screw.

**Formal event:** A non-computational, pre-evaluation replacement. No plan runs
and no lambda-calculus arrow appears.

**Cheap movement:** One lateral slide of the Bond endpoint, one cap settling, one
violet pulse, one spool dimming.

**Sound:** Thread tightening: *click—skritch*.

**Pass condition:** The old and new destinations are both visible, the fixed
guidance lead never moves, and Knot is clearly finished before Blaze Deploys.

### Frame 3 — Blaze runs the altered plan

**Target:** 0:17–0:28

**What we see:** Blaze's complete Speed Pack sits to the right of the public
launch Power Script. Before he strikes Deploy, Echo illuminates the existing
violet-seamed route into the tram. The gold light catches only the outline of
Echo's still-camouflaged hand making a stop gesture. Blaze looks toward the
unidentified empty space, then back to the falling council chime, and dismisses
the interruption. No term changes. Blaze strikes Deploy. The shell clears and the
whole pack appears at the violet-stitched override—not at the capped demonstration
role. The fixed guidance core remains connected, but the priority override
commands maximum drive and opens the isolation fuse. The tram surges. Its guidance
lights fail from front to rear. In the swinging rear car, Tavi's small wings are
folded uselessly as she grabs the emergency bell.

**Formal event:** One legal beta reduction of Knot's complete altered script. The
bad event is the literal result of that valid replacement, not punishment for a
wrong answer.

**Minimal line:** Echo, still unseen: “Blaze, wait. Your route goes into the
tram.”

**Sound:** Deploy thump, rising rail scream, Tavi's *ting-ting—pause—ting-ting*.

**Pass condition:** A viewer sees that the plan caused the tram surge and that
Tavi—not a score or machine—is the reason to care.

### Frame 4 — Echo steps into view

**Target:** 0:28–0:39

**What we see:** Blaze launches toward the emergency. Echo dives onto Blaze's red
public mark and blocks him. Green color returns through Echo from his feet upward.
Blaze stops with one wing almost brushing Echo's face. Behind them, the tram is
still swinging and Tavi is visible gripping her bell through the rear-car window;
above them, council observers and cameras turn toward Echo.

**Minimal lines:**

- Blaze: “Move.”
- Echo: “Your speed went into the tram.”
- Blaze, seeing the distant switch: “Seven seconds. Show me the brake.”

**Cheap movement:** Echo's color reveal, Blaze's abrupt stop, a short rack focus
from their faces to Tavi's tram.

**Pass condition:** With sound off, Echo has visibly risked public exposure to
contradict the person whose approval he wants.

### Frame 5 — Follow the Bond

**Target:** 0:39–0:56, held until the player acts

**What we see before interaction:** Link Vision does not replace the world. Gate
Plaza desaturates slightly while Echo, Blaze, the tram, and the approaching east
switch remain visible. A small emergency Power Script appears as a physical
projection in the scene. One raised, smooth gold Bond runs from its Role Gate,
passes a nearer bright socket carrying the same runner rune, and ends at a
differently shaped east-brake Role Spot. The lure remains attractive and closer.

**Player action:** Place the two-point Lens on the Bond's two real endpoints and
trace the already-existing path past the matching lure.

**What the action is not:** It does not draw, move, repair, or create a Bond. It
only reveals and confirms the ownership already present.

**Visible response:** The true brake spot gives one restrained pulse. The lure
does not disappear, darken, shake, or receive a red X.

**Minimal line:** Echo: “I can keep the Bond lit. Hold its two ends.”

**Assistance if the reviewer waits:** First, Echo repeats the immediate objective:
“We need the east brake.” Second, the raised texture of the real Bond pulses. The
prototype never chooses an endpoint.

**Pass condition:** A reviewer can point to the source, true endpoint, and lure,
and can explain that the string—not the matching badge or proximity—determines
the owned place.

### Frame 6 — Put the whole pack to the right

**Target:** 0:56–1:08, held until the player acts

**What we see before interaction:** The unchanged one-use Power Script is on the
left. A fresh, visibly closed Blaze Speed Pack is on the right. Its boundary makes
clear that it is one complete object. This is not the first Speed Pack imprint,
which remains trapped in the tram override after the corrupted launch. The two
must never look like one object travelling from the tram back to the console.

**Player action:** Move the complete Speed Pack to the right-hand input position,
forming one ready Team-Up, then press Deploy once.

**Visible after-state:** The outer activation shell clears. The entire Speed Pack
replaces exactly the single Role Spot owned by the Bond. The nearer same-rune lure
stays empty. Nothing congratulates or scores the player.

**Formal event:** Exactly one beta reduction:

```text
TeamUp(
  Power(
    b_brake,
    brake_runner,
    TeamUp(
      Bound(b_brake),
      Const(EAST_RAIL_BRAKE)
    )
  ),
  Const(BLAZE_SPEED_PACK)
)
→β
TeamUp(
  Const(BLAZE_SPEED_PACK),
  Const(EAST_RAIL_BRAKE)
)
```

**Minimal line:** Echo: “Plan left. Whole pack right.”

**Pass condition:** The player supplies the argument beside the plan and Deploys;
the player does not manually drag Blaze's pack into the inner destination.

### Frame 7 — The answer becomes action

**Target:** 1:08–1:18

**What we see:** The camera leaves the close mechanical view and follows Blaze
rocketing along the illuminated route. His movement follows the geometry the
player just revealed. He banks around the guide rail, lands feet-first on the
east-brake lever, and pulls with his whole body. The completed result remains
available as a small physical projection in the scene; it is not wiped away.

**Cheap movement:** A fast pan across a still background, three Blaze poses, a
brief impact shake, and sparks at the brake.

**Sound:** Wind rise, talons on metal, brake groan. No dialogue required.

**Pass condition:** A viewer can see that the player's construction gave Blaze a
route he physically enacted. Echo, Linkkeeper, and Blaze each performed a
different necessary job.

### Frame 8 — Not a victory screen

**Target:** 1:18–1:28

**What we see:** The front car catches the curve. Force travels backward through
the connected tram. A pipe bursts into white steam. Two bolts shear from the
middle coupling. The rear car with Tavi swings farther out. Lulu reacts before
the sound reaches the others. Blaze holds the brake and looks back at Echo.

**Minimal lines:**

- Lulu: “Rear joint. Two bolts.”
- Blaze: “Next problem.”

**End state:** Hold on Echo looking from Blaze to the swinging rear car. No stars,
“correct” banner, score, reward chest, or lesson summary. Cut to the title:
**ECHO AND THE BROKEN BONDS**.

**Pass condition:** The brake has produced a real, persistent rescue result, but
the audience wants to continue because Tavi is not yet safe.

## Required four-panel story-payoff coda

These are rough storyboard panels, not extra polished scenes and not part of the
formal I1/I2 validation. They exist because the playable slice stops before the
episode's defining choice.

1. **Blaze is right:** The shell PAIR braces the folding middle coupling just in
   time. Echo concedes Blaze's urgent judgment. Tavi remains hidden in steam.
2. **The real choice:** Echo and Blaze both grip the rehearsal reserve cable. On
   one side, the glowing council lamps and Blaze's captain marker are visibly
   connected to it. On the other, Tavi's silhouette and triangular beacon show
   through the window of the dipping rear car. Echo's two eyelines hold both
   sides, and one finger points toward Tavi. Her bell may sound, but the choice
   remains legible without it.
3. **Echo chooses Tavi:** Echo pulls the cable from the trial recorder and powers
   the Navigation Kit. The public lamps and captain marker go dark. Two complete
   kits clamp Tavi's rear car one side at a time.
4. **A rescue with a cost:** Tavi steps out safely and gives Echo her safety line.
   Blaze touches his captain marker; it stays dark. Echo remains visible. The
   loose green formation mark is still not his.

The coda passes if a silent reviewer can say, in ordinary language, “Echo saved
Tavi even though it cost Blaze the thing Blaze wanted—and may have cost Echo the
invitation he wanted.” It does not claim to validate every intervening mechanic.

## The silent story check

Play the eight frames without dialogue, subtitles, or narration. A reviewer gets
one watch and then puts these six pictures in causal order:

1. Echo's green mark remains outside.
2. Knot moves the Bond before launch.
3. Tavi's tram surges.
4. Echo becomes visible to stop Blaze.
5. The player follows the real Bond and supplies Blaze's pack.
6. Blaze catches the front car; two middle-coupling bolts shear and the rear car
   swings wider.

The review is not a test of the reviewer. Confusion identifies a weak image. If
two or more pictures cannot be ordered or explained, revise the storyboard
before adding polish.

## Formal fidelity gate

The storyboard fails immediately if any of these occur:

- Knot changes a running term.
- Knot moves or disconnects the fixed guidance lead.
- Knot's sampler judges correctness or alters the rescue.
- Link Vision creates, chooses, repairs, or reties a Bond.
- The matching rune or nearest socket determines ownership.
- The first Speed Pack leaves the tram override or is reused at the brake.
- The player drags the pack directly into the inner Role Spot.
- The pack is split into components rather than substituted whole.
- More than one Deploy or more than one beta reduction occurs at the east brake.
- The lure vanishes, becomes “wrong,” or is punished.
- The computed result disappears when the camera returns to the physical rescue.
- A quiz message, grade, or reward claims that the player has learned the rule.

This gate is pass/fail. It cannot be averaged against attractive art.

## Smallest asset list

The clickable animatic should reuse pieces rather than commission eight complete
animated shots:

1. One layered Gate Plaza and sky-rail background.
2. One conduit close-up.
3. One three-car tram in three states: gliding, surging, and brake-caught/rear
   swinging.
4. Tavi seated and Tavi gripping the bell.
5. Echo hidden-hand/green-mark elements, one public reveal pose, and one Link
   Vision pose.
6. Blaze launch, stop, flight, landing, and brake poses.
7. Knot's gloved hand, Threader, six tiny spools, cap, and violet stitch—no full
   antagonist design.
8. Council silhouettes and camera shapes; no detailed crowd.
9. One reusable vector/SVG grammar for boundary, Role Gate, Role Spot, smooth
   gold Bond, same-rune lure, violet stitch, complete pack, input rail, and Deploy
   shell.
10. Steam, sparks, light pulses, and camera shake produced in code rather than
    baked into every picture.

The mechanic overlay must be vector-built and independently editable. Do not
bake Bonds, Role Spots, or pack contents into generated background art.

## Sound and voice budget

The first storyboard round is silent except for written sound notes. The second
can use inexpensive temporary sound effects. Paid character dialogue is deferred
until the visuals pass.

The accepted neutral American River voice remains the project narration default,
but this brief does not cast River as Echo, Blaze, Lulu, or another character.
Before any paid ElevenLabs generation, report the resolved voice name, voice ID,
accent, model, exact text, and estimated billable character count as required by
the project instructions. Do not reuse or rerender the existing British narration.

## Production stages and stop rules

The dollar figures below are incremental external cash ceilings assuming existing
tools and internal labor. They are not estimates for hiring an illustrator,
animator, or game studio.

### Stage 0 — This brief

- **Incremental cash:** $0.
- **Deliverable:** Approved scope and eight-frame shot list.
- **Stop if:** The chosen scene is not the right thing to see first.

### Stage 1 — Rough board plus one finished look frame

- **Incremental cash target:** $0–$50 using existing tools.
- **Deliverable:** Eight monochrome panels, one polished Frame 5, the
  four-panel rough story-payoff coda, the six-cell *Mini Force* comparison sheet,
  and the screenshot scorecard.
- **Stop if:** Echo's emotion, Tavi's danger, or the story-to-mechanic transition
  fails. Do not generate the remaining finished frames.

### Stage 2 — Clickable motion storyboard

- **Total external-credit ceiling:** $100–$300.
- **Effort target:** Two to four focused working days after Stage 1 approval.
- **Deliverable:** One 70–90 second private browser scene with interactions I1 and
  I2, simple motion, temporary sound, and no production game systems.
- **Implementation constraint:** If implemented in the Metaphrand repository,
  follow repository law and use ReScript for active studio implementation. Use
  only native Codex workers unless the owner authorizes another provider.
- **Hosting:** A private Tailscale route may be added only after the owner asks to
  build and host the prototype.
- **Stop if:** The interaction feels like a school exercise, the visual benchmark
  fails, or formal fidelity fails.

Do not begin a full episode, full character turnaround package, game engine,
progression system, or general-purpose lambda-calculus editor at either stage.
