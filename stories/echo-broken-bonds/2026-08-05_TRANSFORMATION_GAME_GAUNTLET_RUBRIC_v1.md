# Echo and the Broken Bonds — frozen paper-gauntlet rubric

**Version 1.1, frozen after the Round 1 calibration failure.** This exact
reference set applies without alteration to Round 2 and every later round. Round
1 used the same criteria but only a provisional Mini Force description; because
Round 1 failed, it does not count toward the two-consecutive-pass stop condition.

## Scope and verdict

This gauntlet tests one playable story sequence: Episode 1's runaway-tram rescue,
including Echo's exclusion, the corrupted launch, the east-brake rescue, the
failing rear coupling, Tavi's rescue, and the emotional consequence.

This is a paper review. It involves no software build, generated artwork, audio,
or child testing. A pass means only: **ready for a low-cost playable prototype**.
It does not mean that children like it or that it teaches lambda calculus.

The four completed story-package files dated 2026-08-03 are binding. Formal
mechanics and existing character powers may not be changed for convenience.
Echo and the Broken Bonds is standalone. Do not import any other project's canon.

## The fixed comparison bar

No single official sequence inspected supplied all three moments honestly, so the
bar uses two exact clips from the verified MiniforceTV channel. This is a frozen
composite reference, not a vague appeal to the franchise.

### Reference A — vulnerability, activation, and scale reveal

Official source: [MINIFORCE Super Dino Power Ep.01: The Ultimate Dino
Power!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1-b0HsZJfWE)

1. **05:16:** tiny animal-form Volt is battered and nearly buried by flying
   rubbish.
2. **07:20–07:25:** Volt activates a handheld dinosaur device; it launches and
   enlarges into a mechanical dinosaur.
3. **07:31:** the completed large form is revealed with small Volt riding it.

Judge Echo against this clip for vulnerability, anticipation, legible activation,
and dramatic change of scale. Do not treat the later first attack as decisive; it
is not.

### Reference B — problem-specific decisive payoff

Official source: [The Vacuum That Ate Everything! — Episode
3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAxghmk9mRE&t=584s)

At **09:44–10:14**, the transformed hero uses the vacuum creature's defining
behaviour against it: wet concrete enters the suction line, visibly jams the
creature, overloads it, and ends the danger. The causal chain reads with the sound
off: **specialized form → problem-specific action → visible consequence → danger
gone.**

Judge Echo against this clip for specificity and decisive causal payoff. A generic
blast, costume reveal, or unrelated finishing move fails even if it looks large.

Together, the frozen bar asks for three corresponding Echo moments:

1. a small, vulnerable hero beneath an overwhelming physical danger;
2. a transformation whose cause, assembly, and change of scale can be followed
   visually; and
3. the transformed result performing the decisive, problem-specific action.

This comparison licenses no copied names, designs, costumes, vehicles, dialogue,
shots, or choreography. It is a bar for anticipation, scale, clarity, and payoff.

For paper rounds, the candidate supplies three sound-off shot descriptions. Judge
the dramatic construction rather than finished production polish. A shot fails if
its essential meaning requires narration or explanatory dialogue.

## Required synchronized accounts

The candidate must provide:

1. **Human story:** an exciting, jargon-free telling of the playable rescue.
2. **Player causality:** what the child physically does and what immediately
   changes because of each action.
3. **Three-shot proof:** vulnerability, transformation, decisive rescue.
4. **Formal proof:** before/action/after plus the exact matching lambda trace for
   every consequential power use.

All four must describe the same causal events.

## Hard gates

The round fails if any hard gate fails.

### Story drama

- Echo has one concrete want in each scene.
- An active opponent or physical obstacle blocks it.
- Failure has a visible cost.
- A clock makes action necessary now.
- The scene turns; Echo does not get a frictionless win.
- Echo's final rescue choice changes a relationship and costs him something.

### Genuine game causality

- If the player does nothing, the rescue fails.
- The player causes every major reversal rather than merely confirming it.
- Reveal/inspection and Deploy/evaluation remain distinct actions.
- The mathematical interaction occurs inside the endangered world, not in a quiz
  screen detached from the story.
- Every player action has an immediate, persistent, visible consequence.
- No reading is required for the first decisions.

### Transformation excitement and clarity

- The pre-transformation hero is visibly small relative to the danger.
- Transformation creates a strong change in silhouette, movement, and capability,
  not merely a glow or costume recolor.
- The form is the literal, problem-specific realization of the evaluated result;
  it grants no unencoded power.
- The child/player visibly causes the transformation.
- The later rescue escalates rather than simply repeating the first.
- The three-shot proof reads with the sound off.

### Age-appropriate desk proxy

- The immediate problem is physical, visible, and concrete.
- Only one new causal rule is foregrounded at a time.
- The child need not understand abstract identity, employment, or social-role
  theory to understand the danger or choice.
- Knot's permanent-role threat is embodied as a **Forever Form** that prevents a
  hero from changing into what a later rescue needs; it is never explained as a
  lesson about jobs.
- Failure is safe, literal, reversible in play, and never a shaming wrong-answer
  screen.

This proxy cannot establish real child comprehension or appeal.

### Lambda and canon fidelity

- Every consequential transformation has a complete exact lambda trace.
- Visible before/action/after state matches the trace with zero mismatches.
- Source arguments clear; no unexplained third copy remains.
- Sentient heroes are never duplicated.
- Zero-use does not transform, erase, reject, or injure the unused physical hero.
- Repeated-use copies only complete non-sentient packs, blueprints, or constructs.
- Persistent structures such as PAIR remain visibly present when required.
- Nested deployments occur one at a time when the binding mechanics require it.
- Cosmetic transformation cannot introduce different behavior that the term and
  canonical power do not encode.
- Link Vision, camouflage, hearing, far sight, alpha-renaming, and signature
  sampling stay non-computational.
- Knot edits only a complete well-formed script before activation, never mid-step.
- Transformation never grants membership, friendship, rank, or a permanent role.
- No binding character power, season event, or Episode 1 consequence is changed.

### Originality

- The candidate borrows only broad transformation-team excitement grammar.
- It copies no protected franchise expression.
- Every signature object, ritual, form, and image grows from this project's Bond
  laws, characters, setting, and rescue problem.

## Reviewer protocol

Each reviewer works independently and must:

1. return PASS or FAIL for every assigned gate;
2. quote or precisely identify the candidate beat supporting the verdict;
3. identify the single largest reason not to prototype the candidate yet;
4. distinguish a structural defect from optional polish;
5. never award educational efficacy or child appeal without evidence; and
6. avoid rewriting the candidate wholesale.

Praise is not evidence. Numeric scores are not used.

## Stop condition

A candidate earns **PAPER GAUNTLET PASSED — READY FOR LOW-COST PROTOTYPE** only
after:

- every hard gate passes;
- at least two independent fresh critics pass every story/excitement judgment;
- the formal auditor reports zero semantic or canon mismatches;
- remaining comments are execution polish rather than structural changes; and
- two consecutive fresh rounds pass without reopening a solved structural defect.
