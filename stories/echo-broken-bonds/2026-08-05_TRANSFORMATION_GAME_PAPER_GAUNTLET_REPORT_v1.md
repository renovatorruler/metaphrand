## The plain-English result

This can be a game.

The child is Echo's unseen Linkkeeper. Echo sees the hidden relationships in a
dangerous machine; the child decides how those relationships can legally run;
the Guardians and their rescue gear then perform the result in the world.

In Episode 1, a runaway sky-tram is carrying Tavi over open air. The child traces
the true route past a tempting false socket, places Blaze's complete Speed Pack
where the route actually uses it, and triggers a transformation. A compact pack
opens into a rail-running frame much larger than Blaze. Blaze still has to fly it
to the east brake and pull with his own body.

That success saves the front of the tram but tears two bolts from the rear
coupling. The child then discovers three different consequences of substitution:

- a plan can use an incoming pack once;
- a plan can legally use an incoming pack zero times, leaving Bolt physically
  safe and free; and
- a plan can use one complete non-sentient blueprint more than once, producing
  two real brace machines while the source clears.

The climax makes the repeated-use idea bigger. Echo gives up the power feeding
Blaze's captain trial so the team can reach Tavi. The child's Deploy copies one
complete Navigation Kit into two complete kits. Either inner plan can run first.
The first becomes a drone and catches one rail; the car's swing shortens, but it
twists around that single hold. The game stops there and shows the consequence.
Only a fresh second action transforms the other kit, catches the opposite rail,
and lets the team open Tavi's door.

The lesson is never delivered as a quiz about lambda calculus. It is the law of
the rescue world: an input goes everywhere its Bond owns, nowhere it does not,
and a complete copied thing remains complete in every occurrence.

## The concrete comparison bar

The bar played the same role that real Call of Duty screenshots played in Matt
Shumer's Claude of Duty project: it replaced the vague instruction “make it
exciting like Mini Force” with visible, falsifiable moments.

No single inspected official sequence honestly supplied every needed moment, so
the gauntlet froze a two-clip composite:

1. Official MiniforceTV, [MINIFORCE Super Dino Power Ep.01: The Ultimate Dino
   Power!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1-b0HsZJfWE): small, vulnerable Volt at
   05:16; visible device activation and enlargement at 07:20–07:25; completed
   large-form reveal with small Volt still legible at 07:31.
2. Official MiniforceTV, [The Vacuum That Ate Everything! — Episode
   3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAxghmk9mRE&t=584s): at 09:44–10:14, the
   transformed hero uses the vacuum creature's own suction to pull in wet
   concrete, visibly jam itself, overload, and end the danger.

Every paper round therefore had to supply sound-off evidence for:

- a small hero under overwhelming physical danger;
- a child-caused transformation whose activation, assembly, and scale change are
  visually legible; and
- the transformed result performing a problem-specific action with a visible
  consequence, not a generic blast or costume reveal.

This is useful because a reviewer can point to a missing picture. “The form looks
large but does nothing specific” fails. “The child presses after the answer has
already been given” fails. “The first clamp can be skipped before its consequence
appears” fails. Taste alone cannot wave those defects through.

The final treatment clears the bar on paper: Blaze is tiny beneath three tram
cars; the child's Deploy opens a pack into a frame three times his height; the
frame takes Blaze along the traced route to the real east brake; and the later
two-drone transformation solves the exact torsion problem created by one clamp.

## Frozen success criteria

A round failed if any one of these gates failed:

- **Story:** Echo wants something concrete, an obstacle and clock oppose him,
  success turns into a harder problem, and the final rescue costs him or changes
  a relationship.
- **Game:** the rescue cannot continue without the player; Reveal and Deploy are
  separate; actions happen inside the endangered world; each committed action
  immediately and persistently changes that world; early decisions require no
  reading.
- **Transformation:** the hero starts visibly small; the child causes a large
  silhouette/capability change; the exact evaluated result performs the exact
  rescue action; the later rescue escalates.
- **Age desk proxy:** danger and choices are concrete; one causal rule is
  foregrounded at a time; attempts are safe, literal, reversible, and never
  shaming; no abstract lesson about jobs or permanent identity is required.
- **Lambda/canon:** every before/action/after matches its trace; sources clear;
  sentient heroes are never copied; zero-use does not erase or reject a hero;
  repeated-use copies complete non-sentient objects; nested steps and persistent
  structures remain exact; no transformation invents a power.
- **Originality:** only broad transformation-team grammar may be borrowed. All
  objects, rituals, powers, and rescue images must grow from this project's Bond
  laws and canon.

A final pass required two consecutive fresh rounds, two independent subjective
passes in each round, and a zero-mismatch formal audit in each round. No numeric
average could hide a failed gate.

## What the final design fixed

The strongest change is not more explosions. It is that spectacle and meaning are
now the same event.

When the child supplies one pack to one owned spot, one large rescue form appears.
When the body owns no spot, no form appears around Bolt and he remains available.
When a complete non-sentient kit is owned twice, two complete kits appear. When
only one nested plan has run, exactly one clamp exists and the car twists around
it. Nothing automatically supplies the missing second result.

The transformation is therefore not a reward pasted onto the lesson. It is the
visible physical result of the lambda-calculus operation.

The story also keeps its human heart. Echo begins with a green formation mark he
is too frightened to place openly. At the decisive moment he chooses Tavi over
Blaze's captain score, in public. Blaze does not instantly forgive him. He asks
Echo to stand where he can be seen next time. At the end, Echo still has no fifth
place—but he carries the green mark openly.

## Honest limit and first prototype question

This paper pass proves only that the treatment is coherent enough to prototype.
It does not prove that a five- or seven-year-old will enjoy it, understand it,
learn lambda calculus, or transfer the idea elsewhere. No children were tested.

Every final critic identified the same first prototype question:

> After the first drone catches one rail, does the player understand that one
> clamp shortened the swing but created a twist that needs the opposite clamp—or
> does the pause merely feel like an animation before an obvious Continue button?

That is now the most useful thing to learn from a future low-cost prototype. It
cannot be settled honestly by another rewrite.
