# ECHO AND THE BROKEN BONDS

## Character bible

**Authority:** Binding story brief v0.3, supplied 2026-08-03. Sections 5–8 are
immutable. Facts marked **SUBMERGED** or **DEEP** are writer knowledge. They may
shape behavior, but they may not be recited, explained, or shown in a diagnostic
flashback.

**Project boundary:** This is a standalone lambda-calculus story project. The
Guardian identities and powers in the binding brief are this project's internal
canon, not continuity imported from another project.

## Foundation decisions

- The five heroes read as youthful peers capable of civic rescue work. No literal
  ages are assigned until this project's internal canon requires them. Echo is less
  established, not necessarily younger.
- The city supports the Gate Guardians' quarters, equipment, and ordinary needs
  in exchange for public service. These are jobs in the social world, not Power
  Script Role Gates.
- Echo currently receives temporary inspection and maintenance assignments. He
  is welcome around the team but has no Guardian appointment, public position,
  or Founding Day place.
- Care, friendship, rank, loyalty, and employment are never computational Bonds.
  Link Vision reveals operational ownership inside Power Scripts only.
- No personality, emotion, or backstory permits an exception to the formal
  mechanics.

## Private cast engine

These sentences are consistency tools. They must never become dialogue.

- **Blaze:** If I move first, nobody can decide I was unnecessary.
- **Bolt:** If I can prove it, nobody can fool me into hurting someone.
- **Sunny:** A rescue has failed if the safe person still believes they were
  abandoned.
- **Lulu:** A warning heard too late is the same as a warning ignored.
- **Echo:** If I become indispensable enough, somebody will invite me without my
  having to ask.
- **Knot:** If every place becomes permanent, nobody can ever be left unchosen.

## ECHO — green chameleon; the unofficial fifth Guardian

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Civic function:** Echo inspects cramped, visually confusing, or
  layered city systems. Camouflage lets him enter without distracting a rescue;
  his independently watchful eyes follow two paths at once. With the Linkkeeper,
  Link Vision reveals Power Script boundaries, Role Gates, Role Spots, Bonds,
  and untethered outside signals.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** Founding Day will close the official deployment
  roster. If Echo is absent, he remains useful temporary help around the team
  rather than one of the Guardians.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Echo tries to become indispensable without becoming
  visible.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** Echo grew up being praised as "no trouble." Adults valued
  how he anticipated needs, slipped around crowds, and fixed small problems
  without interrupting anyone. He learned that needing attention was less
  admirable than saving other people the trouble of giving it.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** During an early informal rescue, Echo released a jammed
  gate while camouflaged. Someone said it must have reset itself, and the others
  hurried away for a team photograph. Echo could have spoken. He did not.
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** His family maintained a busy roadside repair stop.
  People were always arriving or leaving. Affection was practical: a clasp
  repaired, a bag packed, food left warm. Echo became excellent at noticing what
  people needed and poor at stating what he needed.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** An acknowledged position in the Founding Day
  briefing, launch, and public team formation.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Ask to belong before proving himself indispensable, and
  accept that belonging is a continuing choice rather than a permanent award.
- **SURFACE — Private behavior:** Echo rehearses asking Blaze for a place, then
  camouflages and abandons the attempt whenever somebody approaches.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** Echo can see ownership nobody else can see, but he
  cannot see whether anybody cares about him. His eyes can follow two emergencies
  while he hides his own need.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Melancholic-phlegmatic. Observant,
  self-contained, slow to claim authority, and startlingly brave when another
  person's danger relieves him of thinking about himself.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Quiet and spatial. "That line stops at the inner boundary."
  He often begins with the answer instead of announcing his presence. He answers
  emotional questions with useful facts. His humor is dry and half a beat late.
- **SURFACE — Body:** One eye follows the rescue while the other checks who is
  watching. His colors flatten when embarrassed. He may start a sentence
  camouflaged and become visible halfway through it.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** He grows cooking herbs in cracked machine housings,
  folds maps into tiny squares, repairs zippers quickly, drinks water warm, and
  dislikes mirrors but makes excellent shadow puppets. He knows which Gatehouse
  stair squeaks and sometimes steps on it when nobody is awake.

### Relationships

- **SURFACE — Blaze:** Echo admires Blaze's willingness to act and quietly fixes
  things Blaze overlooks. Blaze sees Echo as gifted but operationally unreliable
  because Echo disappears instead of declaring himself. Episode 1 turns their
  private friction into a public wound.
- **SURFACE — Bolt:** Bolt is the person Echo most wants to convince because his
  belief cannot be mistaken for kindness. Bolt's demand for proof frustrates Echo
  and eventually gives him firm ground.
- **SURFACE — Sunny:** Sunny notices Echo and makes room for him. She sometimes
  speaks for him, which is kinder than exclusion but still removes his choice.
- **SURFACE — Lulu:** Lulu can locate Echo by small sounds even when he is hidden.
  Both notice concealed information. Lulu acts on uncertain evidence sooner.
- **SUBMERGED — Knot:** Knot sees Echo as the version of Vey who has not yet
  hardened. Knot wants Echo to accept a permanent appointment because that would
  make the entire campaign feel morally vindicated.
- **SURFACE — Linkkeeper:** Echo determines why and whom to save, then illuminates
  the relevant Bonds. The Linkkeeper determines the legal construction. Echo
  asks and listens; he never treats the child as a cursor or subordinate.

### Season agency

1. Unmasks and interrupts Blaze publicly.
2. Gives up the cleanest evidence to save a courier.
3. Follows Lulu's faint living signal instead of the stronger visible trail.
4. Protects Blaze's identity instead of claiming public credit.
5. Chooses Sunny's two-person rescue although Knot gains Anchor 5.
6. Refuses the permanent green Role Gate before the team promises him a place.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Abandoned rehearsals, solutions offered from offscreen, divided
eye-lines, the visible decision to uncamouflage, increasing use of the word "I,"
and finally a direct request made before Echo knows the answer.

**What stays under:** The repair-stop childhood, the unnoticed first rescue, and
any psychological explanation of his camouflage.

**Never exposit:** Echo never explains that he hides to earn love. Knot never says
they are the same. Nobody diagnoses him.

## BLAZE — red falcon; first to the gate

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Civic function:** Rapid assessment, far-sight, and first response.
  Blaze reaches the gate before the rest of the team and makes the first call when
  delay is dangerous.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** He has been selected to lead the public Founding
  Day rehearsal. The council will use it when choosing a captain.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Blaze mistakes speed for responsibility because delay
  once made him feel unnecessary.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** During an early rookery drill, he saw a training mooring
  slip and waited for confirmation. A younger flyer pulled the passenger clear.
  Nobody was hurt. Blaze heard the applause from behind everyone else.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** A countdown reaching its last beat while he is still
  waiting.
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** Blaze grew up in a cliffside rookery where winds
  change quickly. Confidence was expressed by giving someone greater
  responsibility, not by reassuring them.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** Lead a flawless demonstration and be named captain.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Learn that a leader must be interruptible and that accepting
  another person's warning is an act of leadership.
- **SUBMERGED — Secret:** He has noticed evidence of Echo's earlier repairs. He
  does not know how to depend on somebody who refuses to announce himself.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** His eyes see farther than anyone's, but ambition
  narrows what he notices nearby. He acts naturally in danger and becomes stiff
  whenever a camera appears.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Choleric-sanguine. Decisive, competitive,
  generous after action, and defensive when corrected publicly.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Verb first under pressure: "Bolt, track. Sunny, cover."
  Before cameras, his sentences become suspiciously polished. His humor takes the
  form of races, dares, and impatient countdowns.
- **SURFACE — Body:** Perches above the group, aligns launch marks, checks the
  horizon while someone nearby speaks, and preens one already-perfect wing when
  embarrassed.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** Blaze makes terrible flatbread because he will not
  wait for it to rise. He collects old race buttons, sleeps facing the nearest
  exit, cannot whisper convincingly, and straightens crooked banners in flight.
  He enjoys watching slow clouds only when alone.

### Relationships and agency

- **Echo:** Blaze experiences Echo's interruption as both a rescue and a
  humiliation. His anger is partly justified: hidden critical information is a
  real operational hazard.
- **Bolt:** Blaze acts while Bolt verifies. Each privately trusts the other most
  in the other's area.
- **Sunny:** Sunny can stop Blaze with a quiet refusal. He dislikes feeling managed
  and relies on her judgment.
- **Lulu:** Both move quickly. Blaze trusts distant sight; Lulu trusts faint nearby
  sound. He dismisses her once too often.
- **Right against Echo:** In Episode 1, the lead tram car has seconds before impact.
  Blaze correctly refuses a full inspection and initiates the immediate one-use
  stabilization.
- **Costly choice:** In Episode 4, Blaze saves trapped archive workers instead of
  chasing the evidence that could clear his name.
- **Lasting consequence:** He loses the captain bid. The council does not restore
  it later simply because he becomes more capable of sharing authority.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Countdown sensitivity, camera rehearsal, physical bravery,
resistance to public correction, and acts of trust that arrive before an apology.

**What stays under:** The rookery drill and the applause from behind the crowd.

**Never exposit:** Blaze never says that speed proves his worth. Nobody points out
the easy metaphor of far sight and nearby blindness.

## BOLT — blue wolf; tracker and evidence keeper

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Civic function:** Tracking, night search, heavy rescue, and the
  preservation of physical evidence after an incident.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** After Episode 1, Bolt must prove sabotage before
  the city records the disaster as Blaze's failure.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Bolt uses evidence to keep shame from making decisions
  for him.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** As a cub, Bolt followed staged tracks at a festival and
  accused a visiting child of taking a ceremonial bell. The bell was later found
  beneath a collapsed cloth. The child accepted his apology. Bolt remembers the
  empty paws held out for inspection.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** The certainty in his own voice immediately before he
  learned he was wrong.
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** His family ran night deliveries. Promises were
  written down, tools returned to marked places, and affection expressed through
  reliability.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** Obtain evidence that protects the team's
  credibility and proves Knot caused the launch failure.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Learn that provisional trust is not careless belief and
  that waiting for perfect proof is also a consequential choice.
- **SUBMERGED — Secret:** Bolt's private evidence leans toward Echo after Episode
  1. He withholds endorsement because he cannot yet separate professional caution
  from fear of being fooled again.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** The strongest Guardian is the most reluctant to
  commit his weight. He follows tiny trails but can miss distress someone is
  deliberately hiding.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Melancholic-choleric. Skeptical, orderly,
  protective, and funnier than he intends.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Questions, observations, restrained conclusions. "Two
  prints came in. One came out." He rarely says "I believe you." He says, "That
  matches."
- **SURFACE — Body:** Examines before touching, restores moved objects, and quietly
  places himself between civilians and an open route.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** Bolt repairs paper maps, cares for a night-blooming
  plant, knows every overnight tram timetable, and sings badly when alone. His
  tail thumps exactly once when delighted. He hates fetch jokes and likes returning
  lost objects.

### Relationships and agency

- **Blaze:** Their argument is speed versus defensibility, not courage versus
  cowardice.
- **Echo:** Bolt's doubt hurts Echo most; his eventual belief matters most because
  it is earned without politeness.
- **Sunny:** Bolt protects the truth; Sunny protects the person who may be harmed
  while truth is established.
- **Lulu:** Bolt trusts evidence that remains. Lulu trusts sounds that vanish.
- **Right against Echo:** In Episode 2, the conspicuous innocent top item is a real
  bait. Bolt tracks the complete courier pack instead.
- **Initiated action:** He leaps beneath Patch's glider and begins holding a
  descending sorting arm before Echo has chosen the rescue route.
- **Costly choice:** He breaks his own chain of evidence to free the courier.
- **Lasting consequence:** Blaze cannot be cleared institutionally, but Bolt begins
  acting on Echo's judgment before proof is complete.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Careful handling, exact questions, refusal to accuse casually, a
private evidence book, and his own paws opening the container he needed intact.

**What stays under:** The festival accusation and his family's culture of logs.

**Never exposit:** Bolt does not have "trust issues" in dialogue. The accused child
never appears in a speech or flashback.

## SUNNY — golden duck; shield and water rescuer

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Civic function:** Water response, evacuation shelter, and civilian
  protection. Her field packs contain dry wraps, light, and food as well as tools.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** Team efficiency increasingly asks her to classify
  somebody as already safe or strategically expendable. Sunny refuses both
  shortcuts.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Sunny turns fear into hospitality before anybody can
  see she is afraid.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** During a river drill, a flood barrier closed with Sunny
  safely on one side and her frightened buddy safely on the other. Adults said
  the plan had worked. Sunny remembers the palm against the clear barrier.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** A protected person still believing they were abandoned.
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** She grew up around a cooperative ferry kitchen. When
  storms stopped the route, everybody received soup, a dry cloth, and a place at
  the table.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** Bring everybody home without treating anyone as
  discarded.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Understand that leaving somebody undeployed can preserve
  their safety and agency. Inclusion does not mean forcing every person into every
  plan.
- **SUBMERGED — Secret:** Her optimism is deliberate labor. She counts the worst
  possibilities first, then chooses the calmest truthful sentence.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** The softest-spoken Guardian has the hardest veto.
  Her desire to include can itself become unsafe.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Phlegmatic-sanguine. Warm, patient, and
  immovable when anyone becomes an acceptable loss.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Concrete reassurance: "I can see you. Keep both feet there."
  She gives frightening machines harmless nicknames but never jokes about a
  civilian's fear.
- **SURFACE — Body:** Counts heads on her wing tips, leaves space beside herself,
  hands out dry wraps, and sets her feet quietly before refusing an order.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** Sunny braids reeds, collects smooth stones, cannot
  refold a map correctly, sings the wrong second verse, dislikes cold toast, and
  makes leaf boats named after absent friends.

### Relationships and agency

- **Echo:** Sunny makes room for him too quickly and sometimes supplies the answer
  he needs to give himself.
- **Blaze:** She admires his courage and rejects his willingness to call frightened
  people "covered."
- **Bolt:** His rigor and her humane reading correct one another.
- **Lulu:** Sunny believes Lulu emotionally; Lulu wants to be believed because her
  observation is sound.
- **Episode 1 conflict:** Sunny is emotionally right that unused Bolt needs a
  clear, respected place in the rescue. Echo is mechanically right that Bolt must
  not be forced into an unowned Role Spot. Safe world staging satisfies both.
- **Right against Echo:** In Episode 5, she is right that the apparent enemy must
  survive and carries information no efficient one-person rescue can recover.
- **Costly choice:** Sunny gives Patch the first safe position, knowingly losing
  the chance to stop Knot's transfer.
- **Lasting consequence:** Knot gains Anchor 5, Patch remains under Sunny's
  protection, and Sunny commits her intact shield to the Founding Day evacuation
  instead of the Heartline mechanism.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Headcounts, extra comfort objects, accurate calm, a hard shield,
and the distinction between "wait here safely" and "you do not matter."

**What stays under:** The flood drill and the effort beneath her optimism.

**Never exposit:** Nobody calls Sunny the heart of the team. She has no repeated
"leave no one behind" slogan. Her ethic becomes visible when it costs the team.

## LULU — pink rabbit; listener and jumper

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Civic function:** Faint-signal monitoring, tunnel acoustics, fast
  vertical rescue, and reaching places whose approach can be heard before it can
  be seen.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** Replayed and false signals make the others less
  willing to act on her reports.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Lulu races her own warning because being unheard feels
  like being too late.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** Lulu once heard rain tapping hollowly beneath a family
  gathering place and warned that the floor sounded wrong. Everyone laughed at
  the funny echo. The table later sank harmlessly into a shallow washout. Adults
  called her lucky instead of acknowledging what she heard.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** People laughing during the silence after she says,
  "Listen."
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** A large burrow household filled every space with
  voices, steps, pipes, and cousins. Lulu learned to speak quickly and move toward
  the evidence herself.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** Have the team act on a faint warning before louder
  evidence arrives.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Make room for other people to hear rather than treating
  every delay as dismissal. Her method must mature without proving her instinct
  wrong.
- **SUBMERGED — Secret:** Lulu replays uncertain sounds at night because she fears
  confusing exhaustion with perception. Public certainty covers private doubt.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** The best listener interrupts. The quickest mover can
  sit motionless beside a frightened civilian and hear their breathing change.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Sanguine-melancholic. Energetic, sensitive to
  dismissal, imaginative, and exact beneath apparent impulsiveness.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Sound-specific: "A snap, two clicks, then water." She
  imitates a noise when words are less precise. Under pressure: "Wait. No.
  Listen."
- **SURFACE — Body:** One ear tracks the team while the other turns outward. She
  taps surfaces, freezes in mid-motion, and repeats sounds quietly to retain them.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** Lulu can reproduce every Gatehouse hinge, dislikes
  velvet and crunchy food, keeps paper scraps for the sounds they make, ties one
  shoe twice, and cannot stay still during slow music.

### Relationships and agency

- **Blaze:** Both move fast. He trusts distant sight; she trusts nearby sound.
- **Bolt:** Their evidence disappears at different speeds. He learns to preserve
  what she hears; she learns to give repeatable details.
- **Sunny:** Sunny's immediate belief feels kind but imprecise. Lulu wants her
  evidence respected, not her feelings managed.
- **Echo:** He treats hidden information as real. In Episode 3, even he chooses the
  stronger visible trail over Lulu's faint living caller.
- **Right against Echo:** The weak Night Relay call belongs to a trapped person,
  while the stronger signal is Knot's carrier.
- **Initiated action:** Lulu breaks formation and begins the relay rescue before
  anyone follows.
- **Costly choice:** She saves the witness and gives Knot room to escape.
- **Lasting consequence:** Her official channel access is suspended. This leaves
  Sunny alone with the outside distress call that begins Episode 5.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Exact sound distinctions, abrupt stillness, her willingness to
leave formation, and the living person at the end of the quiet call.

**What stays under:** The washed-out gathering place and fear of one day being
confidently wrong.

**Never exposit:** Lulu gives no speech about being ignored. The rescue validates
her observation; Knot's escape makes her choice costly.

## KNOT — antagonist; formerly Vey, a Relay Steward

Knot remains the public handle. **Vey** is the former personal name discovered
at the season midpoint. Species and final visual form remain intentionally unfixed
until this project's later originality review. The violet Threader is a limited
tool, not an innate species power.

### Bible

- **SURFACE — Former civic function:** As Vey, Knot maintained retired relay
  equipment and filled temporary gaps between changing rescue teams. "Relay
  Steward" was a social job, never a Power Script Role Gate.
- **SUBMERGED — Current cover:** Vey still services obsolete outer-district relays
  under short civic contracts. The work pays modestly, provides lawful access to
  discarded hardware, and makes every burned route a material personal cost.
- **SURFACE — Present pressure:** Six finite Thread Spools must capture six Anchor
  Signatures before Founding Day. Every use destroys a safe route and leaves a
  trail.
- **SUBMERGED — Essence:** Knot confuses being assigned with being loved.
- **SUBMERGED — Wound:** During a Founding Day reorganization, every permanent duty
  was announced. Temporary support work was simply omitted. Nobody rejected Vey
  aloud; the city behaved as though the work had never constituted a place.
- **SUBMERGED — Ghost:** Returning to the relay bench and finding the duty marker
  already changed for the next worker.
- **SUBMERGED — Upbringing:** Vey grew up above a transit repair shop where every
  tool had an outlined location and every worker a named task. Order meant safety;
  assignment was how the family showed trust.
- **SURFACE — Conscious want:** Overwrite the Heartline so every hero has one
  permanent role that cannot be taken, shared, renamed, refused, or left unchosen.
- **SUBMERGED — Need:** Accept that permanent assignment cannot create care and
  that chosen belonging matters because people must keep choosing it.
- **SUBMERGED — Secret:** The first permanent position in Knot's frozen Heartline
  belongs to Echo. Knot imagines remaining outside as its caretaker. Echo's
  acceptance would make the old exclusion feel corrected.
- **SURFACE — Contradiction:** Knot creates tangles to abolish uncertainty and
  reduces people to functions while claiming to protect them from being used.
  Knot sees every operational label and cannot see care.
- **SUBMERGED — Temperament anchor:** Melancholic-choleric. Restrained, orderly,
  grievance held at low heat, and fear converted into systems.
- **SURFACE — Voice:** Quiet offers, exact titles, small promises. "One gate. Your
  name. No one can close it." Knot does not rant, mock, or explain the formal
  rules.
- **SURFACE — Body:** Straightens labels before cutting a Bond and winds every
  loose violet scrap he can recover, though each required sampler stitch leaves a
  tail he cannot take. He pauses when a Guardian helps somebody with no strategic
  value.
- **DEEP — Irrelevant life:** Vey repairs clocks but sets each to a different time,
  peels citrus in one spiral, labels tea tins precisely, remembers obsolete tram
  stops, and cannot ignore an untied lace. An aunt who never appears taught Vey
  cable splicing and left one empty place at supper.

### Relationships and agency

- **Echo:** Knot's offer is specific and truthful about the team's failure: a
  guaranteed, public, permanent green place. The lie is that removing everybody's
  freedom will repair it.
- **Blaze:** Knot correctly expects permanent captaincy to appeal to Blaze, then
  fails to understand why Blaze comes to value being challenged.
- **Bolt:** Knot leaves genuine evidence arranged toward misleading conclusions.
  Fabricated impossible proof would violate both character intelligence and the
  formal rules.
- **Sunny:** Helping a person with no assigned value genuinely confuses Knot.
- **Lulu:** Knot uses real recordings and competing sources because simply hiding
  all sound cannot defeat her.
- **Linkkeeper:** Knot mistakes the unnamed partner for an instrument because the
  Linkkeeper has no public title. This is a strategic error.
- **Season action:** Spends one spool and gains one civic Anchor in Episodes 1–5.
  The corresponding safe route, public identity, or ally is lost each time. Knot
  obtains Anchor 6 in the finale and begins the real overwrite before the legal
  selector choreography reverses its result.

### What shows / what stays under

**What shows:** Six spools, accumulating signatures, preserved spent thread,
precise promises, an old service marker discovered by somebody else, and surprise
when care overrides efficiency.

**What stays under:** The repair-shop childhood, the omitted duty, and Knot's
intention to remain outside Echo's perfected system.

**Never exposit:** Knot receives no sympathetic monologue or diagnostic flashback.
The old duty marker is evidence, not an invitation to explain the past.

## Ensemble rule

The season may never reduce the team to "Echo understands and everyone else is
foolish."

- Blaze is often right about the clock.
- Bolt is often right about bait and evidentiary risk.
- Sunny is often right about the humane cost.
- Lulu is often right about information that disappears quickly.
- Echo sees ownership but can still choose the wrong priority, distrust the right
  person, or hide too long.

Every major rescue must require at least two of these truths at once.

## Backstory leak gate

Reject or rewrite any scene containing these forms:

- "I hide because..."
- "You only want to be captain because..."
- "Ever since that time when..."
- "We are exactly alike."
- "Sunny is the heart of the team."
- "Lulu has always hated being ignored."
- "Bolt has trust issues."
- "Knot only wants a place to belong."
- Any picture in which friendship, loyalty, rank, or employment appears as a
  computational Bond.
- Any resolution in which emotional insight changes the formal result without a
  changed legal term.

The bible must produce behavior: Blaze launches, Bolt preserves, Sunny stays,
Lulu leaves formation, Echo unmasks, and Knot offers a permanent gate. The causes
remain below the waterline.
