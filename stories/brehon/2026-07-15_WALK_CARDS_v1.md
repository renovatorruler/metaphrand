# Brehon walkthrough — the scene cards (before the script; the script obeys these)

Ground truth for the film, then one card per beat. The narration may not
contradict a card; a beat with no want/wall/turn does not get narrated, it gets
cut. UX doctrine applies: screen = scene, teach at consequence, held cards.

## Ground truth

- **Argument:** Brehon never judges opinions — it enforces rules a community
  wrote for its own discourse. Online rule-enforcement today is broken at BOTH
  ends of the incentive: the person whose post is removed has every reason to
  argue forever (arguing is free), and the moderator has no reason to spend
  one more minute (judging is unpaid). Make losing cost a little and make
  judging paid work, and rules get real verdicts, with reasons, on the record.
  **Antithesis (its best case):** charging money to dispute silences the poor
  and chills speech; the internet's freedom IS its zero price.
- **The two ordinary-world exhibits (real, verified):** the Wikipedia *Star
  Trek Into Darkness* war — 40,000+ words, Dec 2012–Jan 2013, over one capital
  letter; nobody could lose, so nobody could stop. And the r/Art implosion
  (late Nov): a mod banned artist Hayden Clay over a self-promotion rule in
  minutes; the fallout took protests, reversed bans, and the removal of every
  moderator — the only appeal court was the mob.
- **Contested object:** one post in a movie game — the author wants it to
  stand; a reader wants it fixed. Both cannot have it: the ruling is one
  sentence and it goes one way.
- **Promise (the opening contract):** THE ANTAGONIST IS THE PROMISE (user
  law): the cold open dramatizes the ungoverned argument itself — the Star
  Trek Into Darkness war told as a story. A crazy antagonist, fully shown, IS
  the promise; the audience supplies "something must answer this" on their
  own. No solution-teasing in the open.
- **NO MONEY AMOUNTS IN NARRATION (user law):** prices change; the video must
  not become a record of old pricing. Narration says "puts up a bond," "the
  loser pays," "paid for the work." Exact figures live on the SCREENS, where
  the product prints them at the point of decision — the camera may show
  them; the voice never speaks them. "Free" is allowed (a design fact, not a
  price).
- **The lineage (user law — legitimacy):** the system is derived from ancient
  Irish brehon law (~7th c. tracts to the 17th c.): sureties/pledges = the
  bonds, aigne = the advocates, chosen arbitrators = the adjudicator,
  loser-pays restitution, precedent, no police, no prisons. Two touches only,
  teach-at-consequence: a one-line name gloss at B1 ("a brehon is what early
  Ireland called a judge"), the full pedigree at B12 AFTER the viewer has
  watched the machinery work ("they called it brehon law; the app moves it
  online"). Never a history lecture.
- **Viewer transformation (the mirror):** the same court record is shown
  twice. At the open it is illegible — a strange artifact. At the close the
  viewer reads it fluently. The distance between those two readings is the
  product pitch.
- **Held cards:** the adjudicator (held until the deadlock needs him); the
  final-offer mechanism (held until the adjudicator is cornered by it); the
  money (a familiar FEATURE of real process — bonds, costs — never the
  headline; chain names cut entirely per the evergreen law).

## The cards

**B0 · Cold open — the antagonist story (pre-roll cards)**
want: the viewer wants to know how one letter eats two months · wall: no
mechanism exists to end it — nobody can lose, nobody is paid to judge · turn:
told as a STORY (setup: the missing colon; escalation: 40,000 words; the
punch: so nobody quit) then the mirror fault (r/Art: a referee with no
process, taken down by the mob) · the promise is the antagonist itself ·
forward: the audience supplies the question — what could possibly govern
this?

**B1 · The record (casedetail AB12CD)**
want: the viewer wants to know what they are looking at · wall: the record is
dense — verdict, holding, citations — and they cannot read it yet · turn: one
legible fact lands: a community's OWN rule got enforced, with reasons, on the
record, and losing cost the loser real money (no amount spoken) — the rules
are yours, the court is your peers · cost: stop watching and the machine
stays a mystery · forward: how does a movie game grow a court?

**B2 · Sign-in (one breath)**
want: in · wall: none, on purpose — the door asks almost nothing · turn:
instead of a tour, the app hands you a case · forward: what case?

**B3 · The rule (onboarding, rule phase)**
want: the viewer wants the game's terms · wall: rules usually mean fine print ·
turn: here rules are three numbered lines at the top, and rule two is short
enough to hold: never use the movie's title · teach-at-consequence: threads
write their own law · forward: someone is about to test it.

**B4 · Jaws (attempt phase)**
want: the viewer wants to know if it broke · wall: the word "jaws" sits in the
sentence as plain English — the rule is genuinely ambiguous · turn: a past
brehon ruled it BENT, and that one sentence is now precedent · teach:
precedent exists and is citable · forward: fine — but the next one is yours.

**B5 · Titanic (rate phase)**
want: the viewer is asked to rule · wall: no precedent covers it; the title is
doing real work in the sentence · turn: the film stops and waits — bent or
broken? · cost: choose wrong and learn the court disagrees with you · forward:
what did the real brehon say?

**B6 · The reveal (reveal phase)**
want: vindication · wall: the ruling is already sealed either way · turn: the
brehon said BROKEN — and behind the verdict a paper trail shows: demand,
refusal, bonds, ruling · teach: verdicts are the end of a PROCESS, not a mod's
whim · forward: that trail is where real money moves.

**B7 · Name + the gesture (mark, wand)**
want: to exist here · wall: a name is a liability if losing shames you · turn:
rulings cite you by name AND losing never lowers standing · then the everyday
verb: tap = bends, hold = snaps; votes are interest, not law · forward: enough
practice — a live thread.

**B8 · The live thread (pizza)**
want: see the real thing · wall: it looks like any feed until you know how to
read it · turn: laws on top, attempts below, each carrying votes and a fate —
and one post down the page has a problem · forward: what does a reader DO
about it?

**B9 · The demand (challenge, $0)**
want: the reader wants the post fixed · wall: the author likes his post ·
turn: the first move is free — fix, take down, or refuse — and the full price
list is printed at the point of decision (exact prices printed on screen, never spoken) · teach: most
disputes end here, at zero · forward: this author refuses.

**B10 · The bond (surety)**
want: to make the refusal cost something · wall: a real bond into escrow — the
doorway of no return; posting it means you can now lose · turn: both sides
post; the money sits where neither side (nor the company) can move it except
by the rules · teach (one sentence + one fact): held by code; USDC on Solana
for anyone who checks · forward: who decides?

**B11 · Deliberation (transcript QF83RN)**
want: each side wants the other to fold · wall: advocates argue on the record,
under chess clocks, paid for the work — and these two deadlock · turn: the
parties must now seat an adjudicator both sides accept (the held card lands) ·
forward: how does one person end it fairly?

**B12 · The ruling (casedetail SB63KH)**
want: a fair end · wall: the adjudicator cannot split the difference — he must
take one side's final offer word for word · turn: the mechanism makes both
offers honest; loser pays and the payment is wages — nobody gets rich off
a verdict · teach: the incentive design, at the moment it bites · forward: what
remains afterward?

**B13 · The record (casedetail AB12CD — the frame returns)**
want: closure · wall: none left — that is the point · turn: the SAME record
from the cold open, now fully legible to the viewer: verdict, holding, rule,
post, citations · the transformation is theirs · close flat, forward-looking:
the next case is somebody's post tonight.
