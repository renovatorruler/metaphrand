The lawyer mailed me the key with a letter that said the estate had entered probate and the property would be conveyed to the purchaser following the auction. That's how those people talk. Conveyed. The house I grew up in, conveyed.

Drove in on a Tuesday. Off day. Didn't tell Denise at the cafe why. Told her my back.

The town looked like shit. I'll say that plain because there's no other way to say it that isn't a lie. Half of Main boarded. The Sonic gone. The church still open (they're always still open) and the pawn shop still open, and that tells you the whole story of a town right there, who's still buying and who's still selling.

Dad's street was quiet. Not peaceful quiet. Fridge-unplugged quiet.

The key worked. Went in.

Smell got me first. Pipe smoke and WD-40. Peppermints. He kept them in every drawer in the house, don't ask me why, I asked him once and he said in case. In case of what. In case, he said. That was the whole answer and I stood in his front room at fifty years old smelling peppermint and thinking, in case of this, you old son of a bitch. In case of exactly this.

His glasses were on the chair arm. TV Guide open on the floor, some Wednesday. In the kitchen one plate, one cup, one fork in the rack.

I washed them. Don't know why. Washed them and put them away and then sat down at his table for a while and didn't do a goddamn thing.

Nine days to clear the place. Buyer wants the lot, not the house. Realtor kept saying the town needs the tax base, needs the tax base, like a man flipping the same egg over and over because he forgot there's an order up. She's not wrong. She's not right either. She's just paid.

Started in the bathroom because nothing in a bathroom can hurt you. Razors, aspirin. His comb. Kept the comb. (Couldn't tell you why. Could not tell you why.)

Six boxes by dark. Didn't open the desk. The desk has a locked bottom drawer and the desk can wait, and I know waiting isn't the same as deciding, I ran a flat-top thirty years, I know what it looks like when a man keeps moving tickets to the back of the wheel.

Sat on the porch after. No lights on the street but two, and the bugs going at the one across the way like it owed them money.

There were chimney swifts working the dark over the Methodist church. Little cigar-shaped birds, you'd miss them if you didn't know to look. They can't perch like a regular bird. Can't sit a wire, can't stand a branch. It's cling to the inside of a chimney or fly, that's the whole life, and they eat and drink and some of them sleep on the wing. My father lived in this house sixty-one years and I left it the week I turned eighteen and never once came back for longer than a weekend, and I sat there watching the swifts go around and around, and I thought, well. There's two ways of doing it. Wasn't sure anymore which of us had done which.

Slept in my old room. Ceiling stain shaped like Galveston. Still there. Roof fixed twenty years ago, stain still there, because he'd never paint over a thing that had stopped being a problem. Not a sentimental man. A man who kept score fair, is all.

House ticked most of the night, boards letting go of the day's heat. I knew every sound. Hadn't heard them in thirty years and I knew every one, same as you'd know a griddle by its pops, blind, which burner's dirty, which one runs hot.

That's not nothing, knowing a house that way.

It's not enough either. Tomorrow, the garage.
