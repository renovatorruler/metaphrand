# PILOT — the squatter chapter through the metaphor stack (layers 1–3, for approval BEFORE prose)

Setting relocated to the author's country: Anaconda, Montana — the dead smelter's town. The son remains a cook (the character survives; the author changed). Narrator: first person — Dale's rule permits it, this is a damaged man.

## LAYER 1 — THE MEANING SHEET (what this chapter exists to say)

M1. The official record of the house is false — paper lies about what's lived. ("Vacant.")
M2. The father had a whole life the son had no place in, and it was a giving life — the undocumented mercy.
M3. The woman's claim is real in every register except the legal one. The informal ledger is a true ledger.
M4. Neither party is wrong, and the clock doesn't care — conveyance grinds regardless of right.
M5. The son is the only person in the chapter whose ledger is in default — he arrives as a collector and discovers he's the debtor. (The chapter's deepest metaphrand.)
M6. Competence is love's dialect — these people say what they can't say by fixing, cleaning, cooking. (Dale's WORK obsession.)
M7. The town is the house at scale: the company left, the work left, the monument stayed. Even the dirt carries the company's debt.

## LAYER 2 — STRUCTURE (drama card with meanings positioned)

Positions: M7 frames arrival (and returns once in the sale mechanics). M1 at the threshold. M2 in the interior. M3 at the confrontation. M6 carries the turn. M5 the exit beat. M4 closes it unresolved — Saturday.

## LAYER 3 — THE METAPHIER PASS (each meaning's concrete vehicle, chosen before any sentence)

M1 → the purple bike with training wheels in the yard, four lines after the lawyer's word "vacant" (spoken once, plainly — no ironic echo).
M2 → three vehicles: the smell of Community Coffee (the red bag, brewed too strong) in a stranger's kitchen; the nickname "Buck" in the child's mouth; the bike wrench still lying on the porch rail — a fixing left mid-use.
M3 → the spiral notebook: two years of entries in the father's hand, checkmarks like his shift sheets; the rent NEVER RAISED — the mercy carried by the unchanged number; the line "call if trouble" under her name.
M4 → the date "the twelfth" spoken by two different mouths, lawyer and realtor, in institution-Latinate; every human in the chapter speaks plain. The register split IS the meaning.
M5 → plain arithmetic, stated once: she knew him two years; the son hadn't called in three. Plus the washing of the one plate, one cup, one fork — a debt paid too small and too late, without the narrator explaining why he does it.
M6 → the fixed bike chain (reported by the child); Darla's night job at the plant, named without pity; the son's trade in his hands (he reads the kitchen the way he reads a griddle). OPTION at render: on leaving, he uses the porch wrench on the bike's loose training-wheel bolt — or picks it up and puts it down. Dale decides against sweetness at the keyboard.
M7 → the smelter stack visible from the porch, used ONCE and load-bearing: the realtor's disclosure that the yard soil carries arsenic — the buyer's price discounts the dirt. The company's debt outlived the company. No scenery admiration (furniture dogma).
