# FREE ROSS — RESEARCH CASEBOOK v1 (110 cases)

Sub-project: series research corpus. Category 1 of 3 (cases; category 2 = Ross-prowess situations, category 3 = poker playbook, separate file). Grounded in stories/ancapistan/ARENA.md: all criminal matters are civil; restitution not punishment; agency-to-agency recovery; Texas Hold'em fact-staking; sealed-by-default; work-program; Cainland only for refusal. Taxonomy letters from the classification pass (A violence, B property, C fraud, D vice, E public order, F justice itself, G protectors, H standing, I consent, J mass harm, K cross-border, L organized/inchoate).

## A — VIOLENCE AGAINST THE PERSON

1. THE PRICE OF A FATHER — A hit-and-run kills a crane operator; his three kids' agency must literally price a life, and the driver is a beloved school-bus driver whose community starts crowdfunding her award before the hearing ends. [homicide award; agency-to-agency]
2. FIRST SWING — A bar fight leaves a welder half-blind and the whole case is one stakeable fact, who swung first; the only witness is a barback who owes money to both men. [single-fact staking]
3. THE PATIENT MAN — A stalker never touches her, so there is no damage to claim; her agency instead prices a restraining contract onto his premiums until his own insurer has to decide if he is worth keeping. [inchoate harm priced; premium pressure]
4. THE MISSING HAND — An agency's star fact-staker is kidnapped three days before the biggest showdown of the season, and the ransom demand is not money, it is a fold. [violence aimed at the system]
5. THE WIFE WHO WON'T — A pharmacist's assistant spots a slow poisoning in a refill pattern, but the victim refuses to claim against her husband; the question of who else has standing to save her becomes the case. [standing; the victim as obstacle]
6. SCHOOLYARD — Two boys hospitalize each other at a covenant school and both families discover the school's own contract makes it liable for supervision; the families end up on the same side against the fine print. [community contract]
7. NOBODY'S VICTIMS — A predator works the uninsured poor of a fresh-immigrant community because nobody stands behind them; a charity claims-fund adopts the victims' claims and hires the best hand in Dallas to play them. [claim assignment as rescue]
8. THE OLD MAN'S DOOR — A seventy-year-old beats a burglar nearly to death with a stove poker; the burglar's agency claims excess, and the case prices exactly where defense ends. [proportionality]
9. AFTER THE BELL — Two feuding men settle it in a sanctioned boxing match; one dies days later of an aneurysm, and the fight was clean but the dead man's corner hid a doctor's letter. [consent crossover]
10. OUT-OF-NETWORK — Road rage between an insured trucker and a drifter with Cainland plates; there is no agency to claim against, and the episode shows what recourse looks like at the network's edge. [the uninsured wrongdoer]
11. CLEAN HANDS — A respectable feed-lot owner hires a Cainland ghost to kill his partner; the triggerman is beyond reach, so the case must establish that buying a death is the death. [conspiracy without the killer]
12. THE MIDWIFE — An infant death becomes a staking war over cause, and the autopsy sits sealed with a discreet-witness firm that has never once been proven wrong; the defense forces showdown against a perfect record. [discreet-witness reputation]
13. BASELINE — A wife's injuries live inside her husband's sealed household categories, until an investigator notices the one thing he recently unsealed, his finances, and asks what a man shows in order to hide. [sealing-deviation thread]
14. THE SHAKEN SCIENCE — A daycare death turns on whether the diagnosis itself is real science; two expert firms stake their entire reputational capital on opposite answers. [expertise as stake]
15. RULES OF ENGAGEMENT — Private security wounds a would-be mass shooter mid-act, and his family claims excessive force; the agency defends its engagement rules in open showdown and wins by showing its own bodycam worst-case. [protectors accountable; system working]

## B — PROPERTY

16. THREE GOOD FAITHS — A stolen vintage pickup passes through three innocent buyers; title has to be traced backward and the loss shared forward, and every owner loved the truck. [good-faith purchaser chain]
17. THE FEED STORE FIRE — An arson investigator and the owner's own agency converge on the truth that the owner's son lit it, and the owner would rather eat the loss than file; Ross reads a man trying not to win. [claimant as defendant's father]
18. ELEVEN FEET — Two ranch surveys disagree by eleven feet moved over forty years; adverse possession staked map against map between neighbors who share a well. [documents against documents]
19. DRIFT — A crop duster's spray costs an organic farm its certification; the damage is not the crop, it is the premium market that no longer believes the label. [reputation as the damaged asset]
20. THE VAULT JOB — A safecracker hits a discreet-witness firm and steals sealed evidence from forty live disputes; every one of those cases is now poisoned, and the thief's real client is one of the forty. [property crime aimed at justice]
21. THE BOOKKEEPER — Twenty years of embezzlement surfaces at the bookkeeper's funeral; the claim runs against an estate whose heirs knew nothing and spent everything. [claims against the dead]
22. THE BRAND REGISTRY — A rustling ring works by forging entries in the private brand registry; the registry's own bond has to pay, and its bondholder hunts the ring to save itself. [infrastructure fraud]
23. THE WRONG AIRPLANE — A repo agent lifts the wrong Cessna off a strip; conversion, embarrassment, and an agency paying fast to keep a story small. [cold-open scale]
24. FRESH INK — Squatters in a dead man's house produce an unrecorded lease; the heirs' hand stakes everything on the age of the ink. [physical-evidence staking]
25. THE MURAL — A tagger's overnight mural triples a warehouse's resale value; the owner claims vandalism while the market calls it a gift, and the arbitrator must price an unwanted improvement. [valuation comedy]

## C — FRAUD AND DECEPTION

26. SIXTY-ONE NECKS — One chiropractor signs 61 whiplash files across nine agencies; the agencies must cooperate without colluding, and the line between the two is the episode. [inter-agency sting]
27. TWO OF TWO HUNDRED — A discreet-witness firm's fabled 197-of-200 record contains two bought validations; proving it detonates every seal the firm ever sold, and half the town needs those seals to hold. [the nuclear reveal]
28. THE MEDIUM — A widow keeps gifting her psychic; her family has no standing while she is competent, so they must prove the one thing she will not forgive them for proving. [fraud vs freedom; competence]
29. PAPER BONDS — Counterfeit surety bonds turn up under a small community's entire docket; every settled case for a year was secured by nothing. [systemic counterfeit]
30. THE THROWN STAKE — A star hand mis-prices one stake on purpose; no one would ever have known except a gambler who models staking patterns for sport flags the outlier. [market forensics]
31. THE BORROWED MAN — A Cainland exile has lived ten years under a dead insured man's name; every contract he signed is voidable, and the community must decide which of a good man's years were real. [identity; equity vs rule]
32. CLAIMS ON CLAIMS — A fund that securitizes bundled claims collapses as a Ponzi; the victims' only asset is their claim against the fund, which the fund also owns. [meta-finance]
33. HAIL CREW — A roofing crew seeds a town with storm damage and vanishes with deposits; the community contract's collective-fraud clause activates for the first time and nobody is sure how it works. [contract first-impression]
34. THE RESURRECTED — A man over-insures his life, fakes his death, and resurfaces in the Union; his agency paid his widow, who may or may not have known, and extradition runs on contract, not treaty. [cross-border fraud]
35. PROVENANCE — A gold bar with plausible Fort Knox provenance hits a Dallas auction; the authentication fight brushes the founding mystery and everyone in the room knows not to push too far. [canon texture; hold the card]
36. THE LONGEVITY CLINIC — A clinic sells premium discounts on doctored biomarkers; the detectives are actuaries who notice its clients die exactly on schedule. [actuarial detection]
37. THE AUTHENTICATOR — An art authenticator stakes his career on a painting he himself forged thirty years ago; calling it fake now means confessing, calling it real means compounding. [the self-trapped liar]
38. COVERAGE VOWS — A network-access marriage, wed for coverage the way people once wed for green cards; the agency wants its premiums back and the couple discovers mid-case they mind. [fraud gone sincere]
39. RESTORATION — A video studio sells "restored" alibi footage to desperate defendants; deepfakes against the showdown mechanism, and the arms race between forgery and forced reveal. [tech vs procedure]
40. NOTHING MISSING — A tornado relief fund's books are perfect because they are fake; the auditor's tell is that real books are never clean. [the too-perfect record]

## D — VICE

41. THE RIGGED ROOM — A private poker room fleeces its regulars with marked decks, and the mark who catches it is an off-duty staking hand who knows exactly what a rigged game feels like. [irony; real cards vs the system's cards]
42. THE LIST — A brothel's client ledger becomes relevant to one custody fight, and forty unrelated men pay forfeit-with-seal in cascade to stay out of one couple's war. [privacy valve at scale]
43. THE DRY TOWN'S SON — A dry covenant community's bootlegger is the founder's own boy, and the family's lawyers offer to simply buy the temperance clause out of the contract. [covenant amendment as plot]
44. PURITY — A cut batch kills three users, and the dealer is convicted by the purity-testing clause his own liability coverage required him to sign. [the market regulating vice]
45. THE LOCKOUT — A gambling addict's family enforces his own self-exclusion contract against the casino that let him back in; the casino's defense is that he came in disguise, which is also the family's evidence of how sick he is. [self-binding contracts]
46. THE LINE SIGN — A billboard legal from the highway and obscene under the covenant it faces stands exactly on the property line; pure texture comedy about where law lives. [jurisdiction comedy]

## E — PUBLIC ORDER (what remains of it)

47. RENTED OUTRAGE — Protesters blockade a slaughterhouse road; no one can arrest them, so the road's owner prices the blockade by the hour and the movement crowdfunds the toll until one side's money runs out. [protest as rental]
48. THE PARADE — An armed militia parades through a nervous town; no law touches the guns, so the covenant's never-tested menace clause has to define fear as nuisance. [fear priced]

## F — CRIMES AGAINST JUSTICE ITSELF

49. SIX PERCENT — An arbitrator's rulings drift six percent toward one agency over five years; the pattern is real, the proof is statistical, and staking on "the judge is bought" risks everything if the math is wrong. [staking on corruption]
50. THE RISING PRICE OF FEAR — A longshoreman witness keeps raising the price of his sealed testimony, and someone is bidding his fear up through perfectly legal offers; intimidation without a single threat. [bloodless tampering]
51. THE FIRST LIE AT SHOWDOWN — The county's first proven perjury at open reveal; the liar's money is gone in a day, but every past case he ever touched begins reopening, and the docket drowns. [recoverability cascade]
52. THE EXPENSIVE LESSON — A party forces showdown with no hope of winning just to see the other side's sealed evidence; espionage by procedure, priced by forfeit, and the counter-move that closes the exploit. [angle shooting]
53. THE NUISANCE MILL — A firm files thin claims priced just below the cost of defending them; the community contract patches the arbitrage in real time, and we watch case law grow like scar tissue. [system evolving]
54. UNESTABLISHED — An arbitrator rules "I don't know" in a child's death and the town turns on her; her defense of the honest verdict, at the cost of her practice, is the episode. [the three-outcomes canon]
55. THE JANITOR'S KEY — A broken chain of custody flips a killer's fact to unestablished; the victim's agency must choose between accepting the bounded error or burning years reopening it, and the janitor has to live there too. [error bounds, honestly shown]
56. THE RANDOM DRAW — Someone bribes the randomizer that assigns arbitrators; not a judge, the dice themselves. [corrupting the meta-layer]
57. THE DEFECTOR — A hand quits mid-case carrying the client's whole strategy in his head and signs across the aisle; no law was broken, and the market's answer to him is the story. [professional ethics priced]
58. THE BORDER VAN — A debtor bolts for the Union line with the work-program van behind him; their side sees a refugee from debt slavery, our side sees a man skipping out on a widow, and both are looking at the same man. [flight from award]
59. INSIDE THE SEAL — A man commits his wrong entirely inside the categories everyone seals by default, knowing sealing signals nothing; the read that catches him is that his baseline tightened the week before. [sealed-by-default gamed]
60. THE SHREDDER — An agency destroys its own client's inculpatory files to save the case; when your protector is your spoliator, the duty question has no clean answer. [agency self-dealing]

## G — CRIMES BY THE PROTECTORS

61. THE SURCHARGE — A security agency's "neighborhood risk surcharge" is a protection racket in actuarial clothes; a rival underprices it honestly and the racket dies by spreadsheet, on screen, without a sermon. [market as police]
62. TALENT WASTED — A work-program debtor with a machinist's hands is kept mopping floors because slow repayment means long fees; the matching clause gets enforced by a claimant who wants her money faster. [work-program abuse]
63. THE TECHNICALITY — A surety company denies a paraplegic on a filing technicality and wins on the letter; then we watch its book of business walk out the door in real time, which is the verdict that matters. [reputation as enforcement]
64. DOUBLE COVERAGE — Two agencies' field teams draw on each other over a client who quietly bought both policies; the deconfliction accord gets read aloud for the first time in anyone's career. [inter-agency protocol]
65. HOUSE MONEY — A star hand is placing side bets on his own cases through cutouts; nothing he stakes is false, and the question is whether a true stake with a private wager behind it is already the crime. [conflict of interest]
66. THE BODYGUARD'S CLAIM — A bodyguard takes a bullet and then claims against his own client for ordering him into an uncovered risk; loyalty priced against contract. [protector as claimant]
67. FEAR SELLS — An agency's ads invent a crime wave to sell coverage; commercial fraud where the product is dread. [marketing fear]
68. THE HIDDEN CHILD — A repossession agent finds a child hidden in the house and breaks protocol to protect her, breaching the contract he was sent to enforce; the arbitrator must weigh the breach against the reason. [discretion vs contract]
69. PRIORITY DOCKET — An arbitration firm sells queue-jumping subscriptions; is faster justice for some slower justice for the rest, argued as a market question with real numbers. [justice as product]
70. THE MASTERCLASS — A retired hand's seminar is a how-to-bluff-arbitration school for guilty defendants; teaching sharp play is legal, and the market's counter-adaptation is the ending. [knowledge as weapon]

## H — THE STANDING PROBLEM

71. THE THIRD VOICE — A comatose man's daughters hold opposite instructions, and his coverage contract names a tiebreaker nobody expected, his AA sponsor. [voice clauses]
72. THE KENNEL — A dog mauls a mailman, the owner died broke, and the kennel's covenant answers for the dog; meanwhile the mailman's agency wants the animal destroyed and the dead man's daughter fights for it. [nested standing]
73. THE ONLY WITNESS — An eight-year-old's testimony is the only evidence there is, and the system has no child-witness protocol until this case writes one, live. [soft case law forming]
74. FORTY YEARS OF HANDWRITING — A caregiver holds a deed signed by a woman sliding into dementia; competence is the stakeable fact, and the tell lives in four decades of her grocery lists. [competence staking]
75. NINE MONTHS — A contaminated well injures a child in utero; the claim matures at birth, so the defendant has nine months to settle with a claimant who does not exist yet. [the future claimant]
76. THE HERMIT'S CLAIM — A murdered hermit leaves no heirs and no agency, so his claim goes to auction and the town buys it together; justice funded by subscription because nobody could stand the alternative. [the orphaned claim]
77. THE RIGHT TO ROT — An old man is dying of self-neglect and no one has wronged him; the community's welfare clause collides with a free man's right to refuse rescue. [the hard liberty case]
78. WHO DID THE MOTHERING — A birth mother returns for the child her neighbors have raised informally for six years; there is no state paperwork, only the community's memory of who showed up. [custody by covenant]
79. THE BLOC — A guardianship company votes 400 wards' claims as one portfolio; a ward recovers her wits and wants her claim back, and finds it has been traded. [fiduciary capitalism]
80. THE SEALED FATHER — Heirs discover their father once paid forfeit-with-seal, and the seal survives him unless every heir consents to open it; three siblings, three answers, one memory of a good man. [the seal as inheritance]

## I — CONSENTED HARM

81. THE CORNER MAN — A sanctioned bare-knuckle death where the waiver covered the fight but the corner hid a doctor's warning; consent was real and the information was false. [informed consent]
82. THE LAST LUCID DAY — A pharmacist honors a dying man's assisted-death contract; the estranged son claims the contract lapsed when lucidity did, and the case must find the hour consent ended. [consent's expiry]
83. THE CLOWN'S PRICE — A rodeo-stunt community has priced its own broken bones internally for decades, until a newcomer's family refuses the culture's number. [community price vs outside price]
84. ONE FACT SHORT — An injury inside a sealed private arrangement; neither party will unseal, both want coverage, and the underwriter needs exactly one fact nobody will sell. [privacy vs adjudication]
85. THE DIVE — A boxer consents to lose, the bettors never consented to anything; where fraud begins inside consented sport. [consent's perimeter]
86. THE FAST — An adult starves to death in a strict religious covenant with airtight consent papers; the outside grandmother loses, the contract holds, and the episode's honest freight is that the system worked. [the cost of liberty, uncushioned]
87. THE REVERSAL — A man's consented, notarized body modification collides with a new employer-covenant's appearance clause; everyone consented to everything and someone still has to pay. [stacked consents colliding]
88. AFFRAY INSURANCE — A firm revives formal dueling as an insured product and the first death arrives on schedule; the arbitrator must decide whether the product defrauded both duelists' agencies by existing. [selling consent to violence]

## J — MASS HARMS

89. ONE BOLT — A covenant rodeo grandstand collapses; sixty claims, four defendants up one supply chain, and the severance of who-pays-whom plays like a poker table with side pots. [multi-party pots]
90. THE VALLEY'S WELLS — A fertilizer plant's slow leak salts a valley; one aggregated community claim against two hundred individually priced settlements, and the neighbors must choose to hang together. [aggregation politics]
91. BATCH 40 — A bad private-vaccine batch, no FDA anywhere; escrowed-recall clauses and batch insurance catch it faster than a regulator would have, and we show the clock to prove it. [market recall]
92. DOWNWIND — A Cainland-side smelter's dust drifts across the line; the polluter is beyond the network, so the claim aims at every distributor inside it who profits from him. [reaching the unreachable]
93. THE DRAWDOWN — A dam owner saves his lakeside homes by flooding downstream farms; priced necessity, one community's worth against another's, no villain available. [tragic valuation]
94. THE COLD NIGHT — One defective space-heater brand kills across five communities the same freeze; the pattern is caught by a claims clerk crossing files, not a coroner. [actuarial detective]
95. THE QUARANTINE — A private hospital chain locks down a district against an outbreak, breaching a thousand movement contracts at once; force majeure finally means something. [necessity vs contract]
96. THE LEAK — An agency's breach spills 9,000 clients' sealed categories; the harm is privacy itself, and the case must price a secret that was successfully kept until it wasn't. [pricing privacy]

## K — CROSS-BORDER

97. THE MARSHAL — A Union marshal crosses the line to snatch a fugitive the Free States consider innocent; on one bank it's law enforcement, on the other it's kidnapping, and the river doesn't care. [two cosmologies]
98. NO SUCH CRIME — The Union wants a hacker extradited for something Ancapistan doesn't recognize as a wrong; the treaty-contract's silence on the point is the entire case. [conflict of laws]
99. TWO DEEDS, ONE ROAD — Two transport agencies bought the same cartel-route easement from different sellers; the corridor war is fought entirely in staking rounds while trucks idle. [the cartel route]
100. THE CASE FILES — A Union social worker defects carrying files that prove Union abuses; the Union claims the files as state property, and the people inside the files claim their own stories. [who owns a record]
101. THE MUSEUM'S BAR — A UN commission demands a "stolen federal" gold bar back; Ancapistan's answer is an open title-staking invitation the Union cannot accept without recognizing the system, the signature pattern played on the world stage. [Part VI canon]
102. THE STRADDLE FARM — A farm sits across the line, north field Union, south field covenant; the owner's divorce splits along the property line and each spouse shops the friendlier law. [jurisdiction comedy]
103. MUTUAL AID — A Cainland raid hits a border community and the defense pacts activate perfectly; the episode is the invoice afterward, who owes whom for the night everyone showed up. [defense economics, warm]
104. THE ACQUISITION — A Union insurer quietly buys an Ancapistan agency to mine its sealed records for prosecutions back home; the inverse firewall becomes a plot engine and the clients' seals hold or don't. [Part II canon]

## L — ORGANIZED AND INCHOATE

105. THE ACCIDENT FACTORY — A crew manufactures collisions against deep-pocket defendants; the covenant's pattern-of-wrongs clause is the RICO that isn't one. [organized fraud]
106. THE UNDELIVERED NOTE — A blackmail demand is found in a dead drop before it was ever sent; no demand, no harm, a loaded intention in an envelope, and the first attempt-pricing debate. [inchoate claims]
107. THE APPLICATION — A Cainland gang incorporates as a security agency and applies for network recognition with a clean-looking book; the accreditation hearing is the immune system of the whole world, televised. [legitimacy for sale]
108. SEVEN ARBITRATORS — One holding company quietly acquires every arbitrator in a county; antitrust with no state, answered by communities chartering their own bench overnight. [capture and counter]
109. THE LEDGER — A dead hitman's price ledger surfaces with four completed jobs; four families claim, and every buyer's name sits inside a dead man's forfeit-with-seal. [F/H crossover]
110. THE YOKE BOLTS — A saboteur files the serials off a small plane's control bolts and is caught before anyone flies; damage zero, intended harm total, and the system must invent risk-restitution or admit a gap. [the unsolvable attempt]

## NOTES FOR SELECTION

Every episode should be pickable as a triple: crime x mechanism-that-gets-its-aria x the lie someone tells themselves for Ross to read. Categories F and G prove the thesis (the system policing itself); A/H/I carry the heart; J/K/L scale the world. The Porch Light was H-adjacent (a minor inside a father's claim) with the mechanism being stake-refusal as confession.
