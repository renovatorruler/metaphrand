# FREE ROSS — "THE LEAVING MAN" (episode design v1; the anti-read case; audience-view first)

Presented audience-first per the author's instruction; construction notes at the bottom. Mount #1 (the read against a reader) with mount #2's clock folded in. Gates run before presentation.

## THE EPISODE AS WATCHED

COLD OPEN. A grain elevator at dawn; Otis Brewer, seventy, dead at the base of his own rig; the county calls it a fall. After the ambulance leaves, his business partner DELL sits in Otis's truck and PUTS something into the glovebox. Doesn't take. Puts. The audience knows what is happening (a death, called an accident) and holds one splinter it cannot place. TITLES.

ACT ONE — THE JOB. The widow's agency hires Ross for a routine matter: accidental death pays double, the underwriters float suicide, establish accident. The show lets Ross be great: the trivia assembles under his own rules — the dog given to the grandkid, every carried account paid off at the feed store, the deer lease let go, the good rifle given to the neighbor boy. Three-plus witnesses, plain meaning thin, one shape: a man saying goodbye. The audience reads it WITH him; the series trained them to trust exactly this convergence. Act out: Ross honestly reports that the pattern reads like leaving.

ACT TWO — THE CRASH. "Suicide" detonates in the widow's kitchen (the church, the burial, forty years re-narrated); the agency moves to settle cheap; and the small settlement has an unwatched beneficiary — Dell holds a buy-sell option at book value against a hidden letter of intent at four times the price. The audience arrives a half-beat early (the glovebox). Then the floor: Ross's read did not fail; it worked AS DESIGNED. Someone built a trail of goodbyes for a reader, and there is one famous reader in Dallas. His gift is the murder weapon. All-is-lost: Ross alone in his truck. The line: "The best lie I ever read was the one built for me to read."

ACT THREE — THE RECEIPTS. The counter-rule as detection: a real trait has HISTORY — it has cost its owner for decades (Merrell's grammar had thirty years of witnesses). These goodbyes are two weeks old, and the county remembers the opposite man: a keeper, his name burned into his tool handles. A pattern with no receipts is a script. Who held the pen: every act routes through Dell's suggestions (the accounts "for the sale," the dog "for the boy's birthday" — a birthday four months off). The read points; documents take over: twelve months of the dog's heartworm medicine pre-paid the same week; next spring's seed order placed; the deer-lease fall deposit PAID — and the cancellation slip signed per procura in Dell's hand. The dead man was buying his future in small money everywhere; every past-tense edit carries the partner's fingerprints. The splinter seats: the glovebox was staging. Thesis: you can forge a pattern; you cannot forge its receipts.

ACT FOUR — THE TABLE. The staking war against the best raise schedule money buys, on a clock (the LOI expires in days; each round burns the widow's estate). Ross's side plays short-stack: one fact, maximum surety, early showdown — "Otis Brewer was buying his future the week he died" — vet invoice, seed order, lease deposit on the felt. The suicide theory dies at market price. The staging finding opens the darker matter: the harness — Otis died unclipped, against thirty years of the company's own safety-award photos, always clipped. Dell knew the acts; he did not know the man; every forgery is off by one habit. The murder claim files with teeth, and the award it points to runs past everything Dell owns — a debt his agency will collect out of him for the rest of his life.

TAG. Two images. The office wall of thirty anniversary gimme caps, every one reading BREWER — including the year Dell ran the operation alone (the Sod carrier; never spoken). And among Dell's papers: a handwritten page of notes on HOW ROSS READS — in handwriting that is not Dell's. Answered: this case. Opened: who is writing the manual. (Season thread toward the composed-case finale.)

## CONSTRUCTION NOTES (backstage; audience never sees)

HIDDEN TRUTH: Dell killed Otis (the unclipped "fall") ahead of a 4x sale to exercise the buy-sell cheap, and staged two weeks of "leaving" acts by suggestion — real events, performed by innocent Otis, authored by Dell — aimed specifically at the widow's own famous reader. FRACTAL LAW: forgery-vs-receipts at every level — staged goodbyes vs pre-paid future; Dell's whole life as the un-named partner (caps) vs the name he kills to buy; even the table play is bought raise schedule vs short-stack truth. ABILITY CANON COMPLIANCE: Ross wrong on screen while correct by his rules (the trap exploits the method, not a lapse); the counter is the RECEIPTS TEST (new drawer entry: history is the one thing a staged pattern cannot fake); the read only ever points — documents and the harness carry the case; no lie-detection register anywhere; Sod (Dell erased from the sign for thirty years) never stated. DRAMA GATES: want (the widow's husband's name and future back; Ross's method vindicated), wall (perfect staging + hired hand + clock), turns (false read breaks the widow; receipts epiphany; showdown), antagonist at climax (Dell and his hand at the table), stakes never retreat (estate burns per round; then murder; then coverage withdrawal), all-is-lost at the truck. CURIOSITY LAW: the audience always knows WHAT (a death, a settlement, a trap); the glovebox splinter is next/why, not fog. CLOCK: the letter of intent's expiry. CHARACTER ECONOMY: Dell (a builder of patterns) does not duplicate Enya (a believer in procedure); the tag's unknown hand seeds the season antagonist without casting them.
