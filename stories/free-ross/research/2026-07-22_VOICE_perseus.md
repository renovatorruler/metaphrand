# WRITING PROFILE: PERSEUS (from the author's attached sample, 2026-07-22)

Source: the author's own draft novel excerpt (Paducah, TX siege thread + Ross/Mable/Enya Union-era thread). This profile names the FEATURES of that writing so it can be reproduced. Typos in the sample are not the style; the style is everything below.

## STRUCTURE

- Multiple storylines interleaved with **** section breaks; a location slug at the top of sections ("Paducah, TX").
- Each section is a complete dramatic unit that ends on a hook; some sections end with an explicit serial note: "NEXT: ..." (forward promise, working-draft style — keep it, it is part of the form).
- POV hops between sections, third person limited. Women get POV sections too (Bethany nursing the baby, recognizing the horse by her own hand-stitched saddle).

## SCENES

- DIALOGUE CARRIES THE SCENES. Long runs of back-and-forth with plain "X said" attribution or small action beats ("Able spat on the ground"). Exposition and worldbuilding arrive inside talk.
- READS HAPPEN OUT LOUD: one character explains an observation to another, House-style (Enya reading the turkey-breast sandwich: the bodega woman is practically his mother). The narrator does not interpret; the characters do. This matches the un-interpreted-metaphor law: interpretation lives in dialogue, never in narration.
- PROCEDURE PORN: mechanisms shown step by step because the writer enjoys them — the mannequin decoy (leather straps through the joints, lead shot in the boots, steel rod up the spine), the horse clicker (two clicks walk, one click stop), the lamp-relay signals, radio codes (BOLO, 10-7, Unit 23). In our arena stories this slot is filled by the staking procedure (the chalkboard, the posted stakes, bonds).
- PLANTED OBJECTS PAY OFF: the thermos cap at the murder scene; the hand-stitched saddle; the coffee-can dimes. Small, physical, quiet at planting, load-bearing later.
- SMALL HUMAN COMEDY: petty pranks (the thermostat knob pulled so it does nothing; hot coffee poured into her cup-holder), running jokes ("themed security agencies"; the agency name invented on the spot), married-couple texture.

## PROSE

- Plain, workmanlike, comfortable; varied sentence lengths; casual grammar allowed. Not terse, not literary. Simple words.
- Direct psychology is allowed and plain: "A chill ran down Mable's spine." Occasional direct quoted thought: "Where the fuck is Ross?", she thought.
- Brief atmospheric openers: the tin sign chattering in desert air; alley cats fighting over butcher scraps; "In 3 more nights, it would be the new moon."
- Mild profanity, R-lite.
- Concrete numbers everywhere: calibers, dollars, ages, street names, times.

## CANON THE SAMPLE ESTABLISHES (respect it)

- Union-era prequel thread: Ross was a city cop; Mable is his WIFE, chasing the Morning Killer on her own; Enya was his ride-along partner who reported him (the by-the-book vs instinct fight; Officer Mike story); the bodega woman practically raised him.
- The agency NAME is born when Mable, cornered, improvises "Free States... Ross Detective Agency" and Jason Hinkle mishears it as "Free Ross Detective Agency."
- Union propaganda in-world: the Free States are believed "ruled by warlords and cartels"; Dallas private security agencies quietly operate inside the Union (Jason Hinkle, hired by concerned citizens because the FBI is failing).
- Agencies have SUBSCRIBERS; themed agency names are a known thing.
- Paducah thread: Sheriff Mason Kane, brother Able (Jabal Kane), Barnett's salon, Billy, Bethany and the kids in the courthouse, the Rattlers held in the jailhouse armory, the water-tower killings, Cooper Ridge. Blood-for-blood customary law on the frontier vs mediation talk.

## CALIBRATION FROM THE SIDE-BY-SIDE AUDIT (2026-07-22; both texts through SlopScore + manual megalist)

The numbers (per 1k words): the AUTHOR'S sample scores HIGHER on the lexicon lists than my imitation (4.7 vs 3.0 flagged words — spine-chills, jaw-clench, gaze, growled...) but near-ZERO on structural patterns (0 verbless fragments in 6k words, 1.7/1k participial tails, no cadence closers, no narration triples — his one triple is in dialogue where it belongs, sections end on plot never on cadence). My imitation was the inverse: cleaner vocabulary, dirtier structure (3 verbless inventory fragments — the oldest tic's fifth recurrence; 3.0/1k tails; "letting the house say what it had to say"; a folk simile; an echo-dialogue pair; an and-and triple; three landing-sentences). AND: my page was suspiciously UNIFORM — 0.0 -ly adverbs vs his 11.9/1k, 100% "said" vs his occasional growled/muttered, zero clichés vs his casual scatter. Machine regularity is itself a fingerprint.

THE SETTINGS, THEREFORE: (1) LOOSEN THE SURFACE — adverbs at a human rate (~8-12/1k), an occasional colorful dialogue tag (about 1 per 8 saids), 2-3 stock body-beats per chapter allowed (a chill, a clenched jaw) used casually, unevenly. (2) TIGHTEN THE STRUCTURE — verbless fragments NEVER (the inventory drip died five times now, kill on sight); participial tails rationed under ~2/1k; no sentence built to LAND (no inverted-object closers, no chiasmus buttons, no laughed-and-quit-laughing turns); no echo-dialogue; no narration triples; sections end on plot. Perfection reads as machine; looseness must be real looseness, not decorated tidiness.
