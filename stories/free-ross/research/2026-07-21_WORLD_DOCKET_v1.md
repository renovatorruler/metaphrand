# FREE ROSS RESEARCH — THE WORLD DOCKET v1

The world's real conflicts as they exist on the Ancapistan docket. Companion to 2026-07-21_LAW_EXPORT_v1.md (that doc = the mechanisms; this doc = the actual disputes). Author guardrail, binding: the system does not dissolve hard conflicts — Israel-Palestine stays as unresolvable and complicated as in the real world. The design work per entry is locating exactly where staking works and exactly where it breaks. Part VI rule applies: shown, never explained.

## HONESTY DOCTRINE (governs every entry)

1. The system resolves disputes between parties who both want an enforceable deal; it cannot resolve identity and sacred-ground conflicts, and does not pretend to.
2. Superpowers bend the system: exclusion threats weaken against parties too big to exclude, and the honest version shows partial compliance priced, not justice triumphant.
3. Some actors are sovereign Cainlands (states that refuse the network entirely); the system reaches them only through cutouts.
4. The dark side is real and shown without apology: the same machinery that makes peace enforceable makes cartels stable. It works well, including for bad people — that is the cost of it working at all.

## I. ACTIVE INTERSTATE WARS

1. RUSSIA-UKRAINE (canon's named example). The UN performance, then Dallas. What routes: ceasefire lines escrowed — breach forfeits staked billions, so the line holds better than any UN resolution ever did; prisoner and body exchanges run as escrowed swaps; grain and gas corridors flow THROUGH the war on clean paper, both sides' escrow paid by the same pipeline. What breaks: territory — sovereignty cannot be staked as a fact, so the front line is precisely the place where the system ends; the map is decided by the one instrument Dallas doesn't sell. The world-native deterrent that emerges: security as a surety product — a consortium whose payout on invasion is priced to make the next invasion a bad trade.
2. ISRAEL-PALESTINE (the canonical failure, by author directive). What routes, and thrives: prisoner exchanges, incident wergild flowing both directions through agencies, water and orchard cases adjudicated one farm at a time, ceasefires that hold weeks longer than UN ones because breach costs escrowed money. What breaks, mechanized: THE SCHISM OF RECORDS — each side maintains its own standing factual record, serviced by its own discreet-witness ecosystem, and neither recognizes an established fact from the other's. The system runs on shared facts; here there are two complete, internally consistent, mutually unacceptable fact-worlds, so every micro-dispute resolves and the macro-dispute cannot. No price exists for the sacred: Jerusalem has no ask. Both delegations keep permanent Dallas offices; their lawyers lunch together; the principals have never met. (The author's worked example stays: the PM performs against Turkey and Spain at the UN, then flies to Dallas for that fight — the recognition-and-blockade docket, which IS resolvable, unlike the one at home.)
3. ARMENIA-AZERBAIJAN. The enclave was a community-contract problem the hard way: standing on the land itself. What routes: the displaced population's property claims bundled into a generational class claim attached to pipeline revenues — restitution siphoned from oil flows at the valve; corridor disputes (Lachin, Zangezur) as purchasable easements with sureties, because corridors are the thing the system does best. What stays bitter: the award pays out and the homes are still gone; return prices out at numbers nobody will stake.
4. INDIA-PAKISTAN. The water already half-lives in arbitration in our world; here the Indus is a Dallas escrow with flow-rate staking and drought triggers. The other docket: attribution wars after attacks — "the handlers sat in Muridke" as a stakeable fact, fought through metadata rounds for years; the fact war IS the war, and establishment-against moves markets where armies can't move lines.
5. CHINA-TAIWAN. Never staked, by mutual terror of the answer: forcing the fact would trigger the payout apocalypse. Instead the semiconductor supply chain functions as mutual surety — the fabs are the hostage both sides posted. The tell the whole world watches: Strait war-risk premiums. When the underwriters move, ministries follow; the insurance market is the real early-warning system, and there are people whose job is reading it.

## II. FROZEN CONFLICTS AND RECOGNITION

6. THE KOREAS. Permanent escrow: the armistice as a surety structure nobody can afford to close out; family reunions run as micro-exchanges with proof-of-life staking. North Korea is the world's one sovereign Cainland — a state that refuses the network entirely — reachable only through cutout entities in Dandong and Vladivostok, every deal double-papered.
7. KOSOVO-SERBIA; WESTERN SAHARA; THE UNRECOGNIZED. Recognition in the real order is network accreditation: a "state" exists when its paper clears — when its entities can buy coverage. Kosovo's actual independence day, in-world, is the day its first bond settled on clean paper; the UN vote was the Eurovision entry. The accreditation hearing for a would-be state plays exactly like the casebook's gang-goes-legit case, at planetary scale.
8. CYPRUS; THE AEGEAN. Shelf delimitation as title staking, hydrocarbon revenues escrowed pending the fact war; forty years of frozen conflict quietly monetized into the world's most boring, most stable escrow account — peace as an annuity everyone would rather collect than resolve.

## III. WATER AND RESOURCES

9. THE NILE (Ethiopia-Egypt, the dam). Fill schedules as contract terms with sureties; drought triggers priced; downstream flow as a staked monthly fact read off gauges both sides paid to install. What breaks: national pride is not a flow rate — the dam's SYMBOL stays untouchable even after its water is fully contracted.
10. SOUTH CHINA SEA. Catch tonnage and shoal alteration as staked facts; the real-world arbitration award reappears in-world as an award that partially binds because shippers need insurance — and the honest version prices partial compliance against a party too big to exclude, doctrine point 2 on full display.
11. CONFLICT MINERALS (DRC-Rwanda). Title-washing of cobalt and coltan as the crime; provenance staking as the counter; a bar of metal carries a biography of paper, and the fight is over which biography is fiction.

## IV. CIVIL WARS AND COLLAPSED STATES

12. SYRIA. Every militia incorporated; checkpoints as toll easements; the captagon trade carried on clean paper the network is not proud of (doctrine point 4). The deep vein: reconstruction claims held by millions of refugees, bundled, rated, and traded — a diaspora's justice as a bond market, waiting decades for an attachable asset.
13. LIBYA. Two governments, one oil escrow — and the escrow agent is the kingmaker: whichever government the agent recognizes IS the state that matters. An episode-sized truth: the country is run by a signature block in Dallas.
14. AFGHANISTAN. The frozen reserves as a claims war (creditors, victims, the state's paper). The native affinity: hawala was always an informal claims-clearing network — it plugs into the system like it was waiting for it, and the hawaladar is the one man in Kabul who already understood Dallas.
15. HAITI. The gang question as an accreditation question at national scale: does the network recognize any paper out of Port-au-Prince, and whose? A collapsed state as a Cainland with a coastline.

## V. CARTEL WARS (author-named docket)

16. SINALOA SUCCESSION. The founder's capture turns the firm into a probate case: factions as contesting heirs of an incorporated entity, the plaza system as franchise territories with route easements, the succession war fought as staking rounds punctuated by rule-priced engagements (canon: violence conducted under agreed rules, priced rather than pretended away).
17. SINALOA-CJNG. The territorial war as a franchise dispute between two incorporated firms; the system's dark achievement: measurably lower body counts, because turf now has a price and a forum — and nobody in the Union may say so out loud.
18. THE FENTANYL LIABILITY. The in-world razor: product that draws too much heat breaches the cartels' own covenants — self-regulation via premiums, enforced better than any DEA ever enforced anything. And the unresolved horror underneath: Union overdose families hold claims no Union court can land, and the question of whether a Dallas agency will take a claim AGAINST an incorporated cartel is a season-weight case, not an episode.
19. POST-FARC COLOMBIA / ECUADOR. The demobilization as an insured transition: amnesty escrowed, dissidents as breach parties whose violations forfeit the guarantors' stakes — peace processes with margin calls.

## VI. MONEY AND SOVEREIGN DEBT

20. THE ETERNAL RESTRUCTURER (Argentina-pattern). Defaults become staged adjustments on clean paper, holdouts price out, and the sovereign STILL finds a way — some national talents transcend legal systems; played for comedy, the one docket where the system loses gracefully and everyone expects it.
21. OPEC DISCIPLINE. The cartel that never had an enforcement arm gets one: quotas as staked facts, overproduction forfeits sureties, tanker-loading data as the evidence war — OPEC finally works, which is not the same as the world being better for it (doctrine point 4).
22. TRADE-WAR THEATER (US-China pattern). Tariffs and tribunals above; quiet cross-licensing on clean paper below; the fish-out-of-water beat is the Union trade negotiator discovering her own side settled the real terms in Dallas before her plane landed.

## VII. ASYMMETRIC, TERROR, PIRACY

23. THE LONG CLAIM (mass-attack pattern). Victims' claims against financiers chase money through the network for decades — a claims trust as an institution with a building and a staff, outliving its claimants, the modern wergild running at civilizational patience.
24. HOSTAGE DIPLOMACY (Iran-pattern). The ransom market formalized: prisoner-for-escrow swaps, proof-of-life as a staked fact, national hostages cleared through the same K&R desks that clear executives — and the state that takes hostages finds its OWN paper repriced every time, the cost it pretends not to pay.
25. PIRACY (Somalia, Gulf of Guinea). The real world already runs ransoms through insurers; the show says it out loud: pirate firms with track records, repeat-player reputation suppressing violence, a pirate captain protecting his rating like a restaurateur — the funniest honest thing in the docket.

## VIII. INSTITUTIONAL SCANDALS (the embarrassment docket)

26. SPORT. The transfer market's real clearinghouse; doping as a staked fact with lab reputations priced like discreet-witness firms; governance bribery settled on clean paper while the federation performs reform at a podium.
27. THE OPIOID-FORTUNE PATTERN. The family that engineered a liability shield through friendly courts discovers the network does not launder reputation: their paper gets marked, no agency will write them, and the reputational death no court delivered arrives by underwriting — the system's quiet ferocity, no gavel involved.
28. THE ASSASSINATION ABROAD (diaspora-pattern: the Canada-India row shape). A killing on Union-adjacent soil becomes an attribution staking war between two states' proxies, fought entirely in metadata rounds; the dead man's family holds the claim, and two governments bid to control it — standing as geopolitics.

## FREE ROSS SCALE (how the world docket touches a Dallas storefront)

The season engine: world-docket matter arrives at street level by accident of diaspora — a Karabakh property claim surfaces because the claimant's cousin welds in Arlington; a cartel probate needs one neutral small agency for one sealed fact; a reconstruction-bond holder dies intestate in Fort Worth and Ross's client inherits a share of Aleppo. The storefront brushes the sovereign docket the way a House case brushes a rare disease: rarely, fatally, and always through an ordinary door.
