## The thesis (what the story is)
- Axis is COURAGE vs COWARDICE, not honesty vs dishonesty. The belt respects courage; honesty is garlanded only when backed by strength.
- Honesty without courage IS cowardice (the molester-who-never-acts analogy): a man clean only because he lacked the nerve to be dirty has chosen nothing; it becomes honesty only when dishonesty is a live option he declines.
- Cultural indictment: Harishchandra, worshipped as truth incarnate, is the moral coward — served the WATCHED duty (the master's fee), derelicted the higher ones (wife, dead son), let the weakest pay the bill. The show turns this lens on modern India.

## Ratan (protagonist)
- A coward who hides behind honesty. Clean 25 years, dismissed by all, never bought (a transfer order was always enough). The living Harishchandra.
- One gift: he NOTICES what others miss.
- Arc: forced past the wall he always stopped at → becomes brave → commits the saka → becomes the NEW JHUJHAR OF THE FAMILY, the bravest of them.

## The father (LOCKED 2026-07-17 — the anti-Harishchandra pivot; see FATHER PIVOT ✅ in the redesign doc)
- A DISHONEST cop by the culture's measure — took the trade's money — and moral by the deep one: he did the hard, dirty, NECESSARY things so the weak around him were fed and shielded. The anti-Harishchandra: burned his purity so his people wouldn't. Sacrificed his good name where Harishchandra sacrificed his wife.
- THE THREE SLOTS (never blur them): RANA breaks rules for himself = truly evil = FEARED. THE FATHER broke rules for his people = necessary good = LOVED. RATAN acted for no one = neither = INVISIBLE. Ratan CANNOT tell his father from Rana — that flattening IS his cowardice; the story un-flattens it by calling the clean man a coward.
- The father is the most offensive thing Ratan can imagine (worse than Rana): living proof that cleanliness earns nothing and stained service earns everything.
- Not a saint, not a Rana-clone: his dirt points OUTWARD, in service; he paid (his name, his legs) and won't apologize. Holds the Harishchandra critique in his own mouth (Rana echoes only the cynical version).
- Speaks the closing line over the body ("you destroyed yourself, and not even for anything") — the anti-Harishchandra accusing his son of dying like a Harishchandra; the ballad answers; both held true.
- Wheelchair stays; cause gritted up (a bullet from the trade, not a clean hero's wound). Glory engine re-aimed: the look tracks sacrifice-for-others, not honesty. The bribe "perhaps" reframed: Ratan taking money = his first step BACK toward his father; the father's pride = vindication.
