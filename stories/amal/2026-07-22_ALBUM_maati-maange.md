# AMAL — concept album «माटी माँगे» (The Earth Demands)

Origin: the झूझार song (the Bhat's invocation, sung via Suno, came out excellent). Goal: a concept album of songs in that exact register.

## THE FRAME (how one song becomes an album)
The bhopa/phad tradition (Pabuji ki phad): one blind bard sings a hero's whole epic across a night on a ravanhatta, unrolling a painted scroll. That IS a concept album — one bard, one instrument, one saga, in movements. The album = the AMAL saga sung as a bhopa epic. झूझार opens the scroll.

## THEME + TITLE
«माटी माँगे» — the earth still demands (from the song's last whisper «माटी अब भी माँगे»). Blood, courage vs cowardice, kartavya, a soil owed justice it never gets. Elegiac, unresolved.

## ORIGINAL ROSTER (HISTORICAL — superseded by the 2026-07-26 rebuild below)

1. उठ रे जुझार — INVOCATION (exists; canonical sung text below). Summons the warrior-blood; commands the coward's veins.
2. THE FATHER — bard to Ratan: his father did the dishonest thing that had to be done, and was loved for it (anti-Harishchandra).
3. THE PRICED GIRL — lament to the sold girl; «किण रो मोल?» whose price.
4. RANA — the fear; the bard's warning about the feared man.
5. THE COWARD — indictment of Ratan: honesty without courage is only cowardice.
6. KARTAVYA — the dharma argument sung; where Harishchandra failed the higher duty.
7. THE SAKA — last stand, death in Mahakal's city, «मौत सूँ कुण डरे»; a vow.
8. माटी अब भी माँगे — CLOSE; the refrain as finale, unresolved fade.

## PRODUCTION TEMPLATE (sonic unity + controlled variation)
LOCKED core (every track): lone ravanhatta + low tambura drone; raw outdoor field-recording of a blind bhopa; weathered, wavering, untrained, distant-mic; evening wind + crickets; no drums; mournful; unresolved fade.
MASTER STYLE PROMPT (reuse verbatim as base): "Rajasthani-Malwa folk elegy, sparse cinematic instrumental, lone mournful ravanhatta over low tambura drone, vocal is a raw outdoor field recording — old blind village bard singing in open air, weathered wavering untrained voice cracked with age, distant from the microphone, faint evening wind and crickets, bhopa epic-singer style, documentary authenticity, no drums, mournful, unresolved fading ending"
MASTER EXCLUDE: "studio vocals, polished vocals, professional classical singer, trained voice, pitch-perfect vocals, autotune, close-mic vocals, clean vocal production, Bollywood, film orchestra, pop, EDM, drums, female vocals, youthful voice, rap"
VARY per track (ONE lever, so kin not clones): add a single folk color (morchang pulse / kamaicha or sarangi shadow / algoza flute); shift tempo (dirge for the saka, a hair faster for the indictment); drop voice lower for death, crack higher for grief.
LYRIC DISCIPLINE: Marwari-Malwi dialect; the सुण रे/उठ रे grammar; run the Mamet gate (who wants what from whom) before "done"; no inventory/descriptive lyrics.

Gloss (reference only, never rendered): rise, O Jhujhar — your horse whinnies; the black soil calls you — why do you not come? If the head falls, let it fall — do not stop the hand; in Mahakal's city, who fears death? Ask your own veins whose blood this is — that same blood, that same blade — now what will YOU do? Whisper: listen — the earth still demands.

Design notes: every sung line acts (address → command → question, no inventory); the address TURNS on the last sung line — from the dead warrior to the living listener («अब थूँ काई करे?») — the roster's "commands the coward's veins" delivered in one pivot; «मौत सूँ कुण डरे» plants the saka line; the final whisper is the album title's origin, now verified on paper. ## ROSTER REBUILT (author, 2026-07-26) — the album argues DUTY: kept at an unbearable price vs worn as a costume
Subjects come from the SOIL, not from the plot (author's correction: the Jhujhar song works because it is a real generic folk song; tracks written as narrative errands would sound bespoke). Named so far: TEJAJI (kept his word to the snake; offered his tongue — the organ he kept it with), AHILYABAI (Malwa's justice-queen at Maheshwar; the legend of judging her own son — folk legend, not documented history, and the legend is what the soil sings; summoned back to a bazaar with rigged scales), FAKE KARTAVYA (duty as costume — «बोल दे», drafted below). Jhujhar (track 1) stands. Old abstract tracks (the father / the coward / kartavya-as-topic) are OFF.
Structural consequence: adding Tejaji and Ahilyabai widens the frame past one phad bhopa's single epic — the album is the songs OF this soil; "one blind bard, one instrument" becomes one voice among a few, not a law.

## TRACK — «बोल दे» (You tell her) — fake kartavya — DRAFT v1, native-ear verdict pending
Situation: night, the lamp low, the girl asleep in the next room. A father persuades his wife. What he wants is not her agreement — he wants HER to be the one to tell the daughter. Nothing he says is untrue. The album's only track sung by a liar; the sweetest sound on the record carries the worst content.

Gloss (never rendered): Turn the lamp down — the girl's asleep. Wipe your eyes, you'll wake her. Your own father did this, remember; you cried that day, and today this house is yours. The patwari is asking — the papers aren't complete. If the licence goes out of our hands, whose is the field? We'll bring her at Teej. Every Teej. I'll send the cart; you watch the road. But YOU tell her — she won't hear it from me. With a mother's hand on her head, a daughter doesn't cry. Wipe the tears beforehand — they'll be watching. And... put the good oil in her hair. This is dharma. What else?
