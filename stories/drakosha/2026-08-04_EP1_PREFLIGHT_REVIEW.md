## How this review process saves the author time

Future drafts should not reach the author until they pass all of these checks:

1. **Physical blocking:** establish where every character starts and ends, what cover exists, and what path is exposed.
2. **Scale and load:** compare every human object with the supplied character designs; verify who can lift, carry, reach, enter, or see it.
3. **Prop continuity:** track every important object, letter word, pouch, pencil, candle, opening, and door from beat to beat.
4. **Knowledge continuity:** confirm that a character knows only what that character saw, heard, or was told.
5. **Sound-off test:** prove that the action can be storyboarded and understood without dialogue.
6. **Radio test:** prove that the essential story can also be followed without the picture.
7. **Russian and bilingual test:** check natural Russian, playable actor directions, and faithful English immediately below it.
8. **Independent challenge pass:** have a second reader try to break the geography, mechanics, and causality before delivery.

The author should receive one consolidated packet with:

- only material issues;
- one recommended solution for each;
- a clear distinction between automatic repairs and genuine creative decisions;
- no suggestion entered into the approved-decision ledger until the author explicitly approves it.

After approval, all repairs should be applied in one revision and the complete screenplay should be tested again from the beginning. The author should not have to discover failures line by line.

## Author decision D1 — the sock objective

### Why the present action fails

**Affected:** L005–L010 and the corresponding Scene 1 entries in the decision ledger.

- The children leave cover and stand directly in front of the dresser.
- A lowest drawer on a raised human dresser cannot plausibly be chest-high to Frosya at the scale shown by her safety pin and Vasya’s shoelace.
- A sock that both children can barely drag cannot suddenly be bundled, lifted, and heaved over that edge.
- Pulling the drawer out farther changes its depth but does not lower its front wall.
- The prose declares the geometry convenient instead of proving a drawable action.

### Recommended direction

Do **not** make the drawer the destination. The children drag the sock together through the protected lane behind the dresser. At the side corner—the farthest point they dare expose themselves—they push the sock just far enough into the giants’ visible room to be found, then retreat behind the dresser.

This preserves everything the opening needs:

- cooperation;
- the danger of entering the giants’ world;
- the rule that they return lost things;
- side cover rather than prolonged exposure in front;
- the later view of the moving red car from beneath/behind the dresser.

The giant child can notice or retrieve the returned sock before the toy car gets stuck, providing a clean visual and audible payoff that the siblings succeeded.

If putting the sock **inside the drawer** is essential, the scene needs a separately designed elevated access route or hoisting mechanism that works from the protected side. That is a larger opening set-piece and must be proven with a plan and elevation before any new prose is written.

## Automatic repair package

### R1 — Baba Yaga’s cleanout hatch

**Affected:** L043–L045 and L220–L222.

The rear cleanout hatch opens on arrival but never closes. At departure, the scene behaves as though it must be traversed again and only then closes. Add a clear close immediately after arrival and a clear reopening before departure.

### R2 — Frosya’s pencil continuity

**Affected:** L156, L164–L165, and L189.

Frosya writes `БАК` while holding the pencil, runs from the wasp, and later finds it behind her ear without putting it there. Restore the visible action of securing it behind her ear before the chase leaves the underfloor road.

### R3 — Mama’s knowledge of the бак

**Affected:** L165–L168 and L208.

The бак remains on the underfloor road while Mama remains in the main room. Nothing currently lets her see it or learn about it, yet she specifically orders `Бак — с дороги`. The final blocking map must either give Mama a real sightline down the ramp or give her an audible information path before that instruction.

### R4 — the complete ОСА + МОТОК mechanics

**Affected:** L191 and L195–L202.

The partnership idea works, but the current choreography does not:

- Vasya-wasp cannot hold the thread with all six legs, fold his wings, and still move through the opening.
- Frosya cannot simply hold an unwinding ball and then generate pulling tension without changing her grip.
- A recognizable triple knot needs jaws/forelegs, a braced candle, and visible passes through a loop—not merely three decorative wraps.
- Vasya must retain a free means of movement when he guides the candle through the rim.

The storyboardable direction is:

1. Vasya takes the free end in his jaws, folds his wings, and crawls through on his legs.
2. Once below, he releases and opens his wings.
3. He lands beside the candle, braces it with his rear legs, and uses his jaws and forelegs to make three readable knotting passes.
4. Frosya pays out the strand while he descends; after his signal, she grips the live strand near the opening and pulls hand over hand rather than merely holding the ball.
5. At the rim, she slows while Vasya uses his free legs/body to turn the candle lengthwise.

This preserves `ОСА + МОТОК`, Frosya doing the pull, and `Я обвязал. Сам. Тройным узлом!`.

### R5 — radio clarity at the rescue and finale

**Affected:** L186 and L223–L230.

- `Я пролезу. Оса туда пролезет` briefly sounds as though boy-Vasya can enter. The intended thought should be unambiguous: he can get through **as a wasp**.
- The closing image `МА□□□А` is clear visually but not by sound. Add one brief, natural self-check from Frosya that connects the unfinished word, the three missing letters, and the absent car without turning the ending into an explanation.

### R6 — recording and translation cleanup

**Affected examples:** L031, L090, L115, L134, L138, L187, L219, L246, and the English under L092.

- Several actor directions describe only body movement and provide no playable vocal intention for `TAG_MAP_RU`. Convert them during the recording pass without changing the dialogue’s meaning.
- L092’s English loses the important singular/plural shift: Vasya learns the letter, but both children receive the next one. Restore that distinction.
- Handle any remaining idiomatic or translation polish in one batch after story mechanics are locked; do not interrupt the author with isolated punctuation or synonym choices.
