# «Фрося и Вася» — заглавная песня «Скажи — и стань!» (v1)

Form: series title track, ~1:20 full version. Sung BY Фрося and Вася AT the listener — the song wants something from the child (come closer, hush, now say it with us), per the lyrics-must-act law. Vocabulary deliberately tiny and repeatable; every load-bearing word is either taught in the season or shown on screen. Вася's lines carry correct masculine forms. Native Russian only, no anglicisms.

## SUNO — STYLE PROMPT (paste into Style of Music)

Joyful Russian children's television theme song, bright and cozy, 100 BPM, major key, 2/4 skipping rhythm. Warm female lead voice with a small children's chorus answering her, kids shouting the letters and the word VZHUKH with delight. Homemade acoustic band as if played on things found around a house: button accordion, plucked balalaika, toy piano, glockenspiel, wooden spoons, thimble taps, a jar of buttons shaken as a shaker, hand claps, brushed snare, upright bass, penny whistle. Gentle Slavic folk flavour, nursery-rhyme melody, extremely singable, big sing-along chorus. Starts with an intimate hush, almost whispered, then bursts into full band. Clean warm analog recording, close vocals, cozy and intimate, storybook charm.

## PRODUCTION NOTES (not for the song page)

- The hook does the teaching. «Буква к букве — будет слово» states the whole reading mechanic in five words, and «Скажи — и стань!» is the in-show chant, so the theme and the world reinforce each other every single episode.
- КОТ and СОМ in the chorus are the pilot's two magic words. Later seasons may swap the second one for that season's signature word without touching the melody.
- The bridge letters are Вася's real starting eight (А О М С К Т Л Б) — the eight the placement test confirmed — so the children shout letters they actually own. Add letters to the bridge as seasons add them.
- «Восемь букв в моём мешке» ties to his letter-pouch prop; keep the count honest as it grows.
- The whispered intro and outro are the show's signature sound: it invites the child to lean in and go quiet before the episode starts, and it is the cheapest possible way to establish that these are creatures nobody has ever properly seen.
- The instrument list is a design decision, not decoration: the score is played on the same found household objects the characters live among (spoons, thimbles, a jar of buttons), so the music belongs to the world.
- If generating several takes, keep the take whose children's chorus sounds like real children rather than processed ones — this show's whole warmth depends on it.
