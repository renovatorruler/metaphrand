# EP1 Scene 5 — staging plan

Written 2026-08-17. This is the authority for every scene-5 job. Jobs are derived from it; nothing about who is where, doing what, is invented inside a prompt any more.

It exists because the first three attempts at scene 5 each re-invented the room. Each prompt was written from a shot range alone, so the question "what were these people doing a moment before the shot starts" had no answer by construction, and the author had to catch the staging every time. Prose in a prompt is not a staging decision — it is a guess that survives until someone looks at it.

## What the script establishes, and is therefore not negotiable

Scene 5 is headed ДОМ ЗА ПЛИТОЙ — ЧУТЬ ПОЗЖЕ, a little later than scene 4. It is the same tea, still going.

The whole family is at ONE table. The script puts them there directly: Мама sets her thimble on it (SH063), Папа is at it (SH065C, "к Папе за столом"), Вася jumps up from his own place at it (SH062), and SH065B frames Фрося and Вася together. Бабушка-Яга and Фрося are opposite each other (SH056).

So Фрося is at the table because it is a family meal on her birthday, not because she has been seated for an adult ritual, and she is not separated from her siblings — they are all around it.

The pre-state, the thing happening in the second before SH057 begins: tea is pouring into a thimble, Бабушка-Яга raises hers, and Фрося is watching her and waiting for her to speak (SH056). The small box of candles Бабушка-Яга brought is still sitting where she left it on the edge of the table (SH054–055).

## Who is where

| | Position | What they are doing continuously |
|---|---|---|
| БАБУШКА-ЯГА | at the table, opposite Фрося | her thimble just raised, letting it cool, taking her time before she speaks |
| ФРОСЯ | at the table, opposite Бабушка-Яга | watching her, waiting for her to say something, both hands round her own thimble |
| МАМА | at the left end of the table, both hands free | one hand rocking the cradle, humming tunelessly under her breath without noticing — see the open question on where the cradle is |
| ПАПА | right end, on his feet at the start, his own plate at his own place | bent over Руся beside him, helping him eat and wiping his face; sits back down into his place partway through. A father managing a toddler — NOT serving the room, NOT waiting on anyone. |
| ВАСЯ | at the table, his own place | restless, not listening to the grown-ups, fidgeting with something small |
| РУСЯ | at or under the table | small child business, quick and clumsy |
| МУСЯ | OPEN — not on Мама, who has both hands free in f18 | asleep. Wherever she is, she is the reason Мама never raises her voice. |

**Open: where is Муся.** She is not on Мама and f18 does not contain her. The knitted cradle is established canon and is the obvious place, but nothing records where it sits relative to this table, and Мама can only rock it if it is within reach. Simplest resolution, and the recommended one: keep Муся OUT OF FRAME — asleep in the cradle elsewhere in the room. That removes a figure from the plate edit, removes a cast binding, and costs nothing, because the sleeping baby works on the scene by being audible-in-absence rather than visible. If she should be seen, her position has to come from the author; it must not be guessed again.

Nobody in this scene is ever still, blank or merely blinking. A character with nothing to do is a defect. A silent room is a defect.

## The beats, in order

1. **SH057** — Бабушка-Яга asks the birthday girl what she dreams of. As the line runs the room notices her: the humming stops mid-note, Папа looks up, the children's fidgeting settles. She has the table's attention by the end of the line and she knows it. This is the answer to "why is nobody paying attention to her" — attention is a written beat, not a hope.
2. **SH058** — Мама's rocking and humming stop together and her thimble stops halfway to her mouth. She was moving a moment ago and now she is not; the stillness is the event, not her default state.
3. **SH059** — Мама warns, low, over the resumed rocking, because the baby is asleep on her.
4. **SH060–061** — Фрося erupts. The second half of her line is OFFSCREEN over Бабушка-Яга, whose smile widens; Мама lowers her thimble behind her. Offscreen is deliberate and good for us: a line with no mouth on screen has no lip-sync to match when we dub.
5. **SH062** — Вася barges in from his place at the table before anyone can answer his sister.
6. **SH063** — Мама sets her thimble down, keeps her hand on it, and refuses.
7. **SH064** — Бабушка-Яга sets her thimble beside Мама's and overrules her, pleasantly.
8. **SH065** — three-part: Мама closes her eyes, the two children freeze and wait, Папа whistles once. Then silence.

Scene 6 follows continuous: the pencil comes out of Бабушка-Яга's pocket.

## The plate that has to be made first

An edit of `f18_master_candlebox_author.png`, with the children's sheets passed in alongside it. **FOUR figures are added, not three** — f18 holds only Бабушка-Яга, Мама and Папа:

- **ФРОСЯ** seated on the near side, back to camera, directly across from Бабушка-Яга
- **ВАСЯ** seated on the near side beside her, back to camera, toward Папа's end
- **РУСЯ** seated on the far side beside Бабушка-Яга, toward Папа's end
- **МУСЯ** — see the open question below; possibly not in frame at all

Two things f18 already settles, which earlier drafts of this plan got wrong and must not be re-invented. **МАМА is not holding a baby** — both hands free. And **ПАПА is on his feet at the end of the table, not seated.**

But he is on his feet for a reason, and the reason has to be legible or he reads as staff. An earlier draft of this plan had him "serving — setting food down, moving between the table and the counter," which makes the father of the family a waiter at his own daughter's birthday.

**The reason is delivered in motion, not in the plate.** He starts frame one in exactly the f18 pose — unchanged, because changing him is the one thing the edit is designed to avoid — then bends down to Руся to help him eat, and afterwards sits into his own place. A still can only show a pose, and a pose is what made him read as staff; twenty seconds of video can show why he is up.

This is the general rule the plate follows: **the plate adds people, the video gives them behaviour.** Anything that would require re-posing an adult already in f18 belongs in the shot, not in the plate.

Also: the table loaded with food, plates in front of everyone, the meal well under way. The candle box comes OFF the table and goes to the shelf.

Nothing else changes. The three adults keep their exact positions, the room, the light and the proportions are untouched. That is the whole point of editing rather than generating — a fresh plate is what shrank Мама's head last time.

## Which room — settled

**Neither room plate. The author's f18 tea-table plates**, in `production/kuku_flow/frames/f18_angles/`. Scene 4b was shot entirely against those and scene 5 is the same tea a little later, so it has to match them and nothing else.

This matters because the two are not reconcilable. The production record §14 states it: the f18 plates carry the bedding crates in frame at the left, so one wall reads as kitchen, sitting area and sleeping area at once, and the opposite wall was deliberately designed as its own space. Without a connecting wide the audience cannot assemble a floor plan, and the two read as separate rooms. Staging scene 5 against `@ROOM_FRONT` or `@ROOM_BACK` therefore puts it in a different room from the tea it continues.

**But the f18 master holds only three people** — Бабушка-Яга, Мама and Папа — and scene 5 needs six and a baby. So it cannot itself be the start frame. A new scene-5 plate has to be made, and made by EDITING the f18 master rather than generating a fresh one: pass the master plus the three children's sheets to an image model that takes multiple inputs, and add only what is missing. The room, the adults, the lighting and the proportions are then never regenerated and cannot drift. Generating a fresh plate is what shrank Мама's head last time.

**What changed between scene 4 and scene 5, and why the children are there.** Scene 4 is three adults over tea while the children are elsewhere. Scene 5 is the same table a little later, once the children have been called to it for the birthday — which is also why Бабушка-Яга asks the wish question now, at the birthday table, before the cake. Grandma brought the candles at SH054 and the box is still shut on the table edge, so the cake has not happened yet.

The plate therefore shows a birthday table loaded with food, the meal well under way: plates in front of everyone, tea still going for the adults. Everyone has something in their hands, which is what the dead-room problem needed.

**The candle box is NOT on this table.** Бабушка-Яга set it down in the middle at SH054–055, and it would eat the middle of every frame in this scene. It goes to the shelf. It comes back to the edge of the table beside the cake, once the plates and the food have been cleared — which is also the visual that says the meal is over and the cake has arrived.

## Camera — the long side, and why backs to camera are correct

It is a rectangular table and a family of seven, so every side is occupied and there is no angle from which nobody has their back to the lens. That is not a problem to be designed around. **Backs to camera in a wide are the grammar of table coverage** — the near heads become foreground and give the shot depth, and over-the-shoulder is how dialogue at a table has always been shot. A table where everyone faces the lens reads as a staged photograph, not a family eating.

So the f18 long-side angle stands as the master and no new angle is needed. The wide establishes the table with two backs in the near foreground; every line after it is a single on a long lens from the correct side, where the speaker faces us.

Nobody moves from where f18 has them, because the approved close-up plates are seat-dependent: `f18_mama_cu_author`, `f18_yaga_ots_author`, `f18_yaga_cu_talk_author` and `f18_papa_cu_author_FINAL` all stop matching the moment an adult changes chairs. Only the three children are new.

Going clockwise from the left end: **МАМА** (with Муся) at the left end, **БАБУШКА-ЯГА** then **РУСЯ** along the far side, **ПАПА** at the right end, then **ВАСЯ** and **ФРОСЯ** along the near side with their backs to the wide. Бабушка-Яга and Фрося end up across the table from each other, which is what the script asks for.

The walls, which the seating has to respect:

| Behind | What is there |
|---|---|
| Бабушка-Яга and Руся (far side) | the black riveted plate |
| Мама (left end) | the wall with the matchbox beds |
| Папа (right end) | the shelf and the little washstand |
| Вася and Фрося (near side) | the open room running away to the far end, and the ramp down to the road |

## The gap behind the children, and how the two singles handle it

**The set is not unknown.** `SET-HOME-ROOM-02_author_far_wall_v2` shows that end of the hall — it is a view taken from past the railing, looking back. What does not exist is that end seen FROM THE TABLE, with the railing of the ramp in the near ground. The author attempted it and could not get the railing to render properly.

So the two shots that fire in that direction — **SH060 on Фрося and SH062 on Вася** — have a known set behind them and no plate at the needed angle.

Worth one attempt before accepting the workaround permanently: the earlier tries appear to have been generated from text. Feeding ROOM-02 in as an image input, with the camera position stated and the railing named as the foreground object, is a different method and has not been tried. If it lands, the constraint below disappears for every shot in the show, not just these two.

They are handled with occlusion plus reach, which is the standard answer and is why the scene is on long glass at all. Both are over-the-shoulder from across the table — Фрося past Бабушка-Яга, Вася past Руся — so an oversized soft dark shoulder eats the near corner of frame. Both drop from 29° to **18°**, six to eight metres back, which compresses hard and leaves razor-thin focus on the eyes alone. And the prompt states outright that there is nothing readable back there: no railing, no posts, no opening, no ramp, no doorway, no straight lines. Warm blur only.

Every other single stays at 29° with a real wall behind it.

**This is a standing constraint, not a scene-5 workaround.** Any shot anywhere in this hall that looks toward that end faces the same missing angle. Until a plate exists for it, shots pointing that way are long-lens and occluded, or they are not shot.

**Consequence: nothing below the table is ever specified in a prompt.** Chair shapes, arm rests, what a child can hang on, how many seats there are. Three prompts invented furniture the script contradicts — a two-seat tea table, an armchair with an arm rest — because the room was being held by prose. It is held by the start frame now. Take it from there and invent none of it.
