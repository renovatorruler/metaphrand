# Staging doctrine — two laws

Everything else on this show is a consequence of these two. When a new defect appears, it belongs under one of them; if it seems to need a third law, the diagnosis is probably wrong.

---

# LAW 1 — ONLY SHOOT WHAT MOVES THE STORY

Every shot must add something. If a shot can be lifted out and the story still stands, it should not have been generated.

This is not a budget rule, though it saves money. It is what makes a scene feel like it is going somewhere. A shot that merely gets a character from one place to another, or merely confirms something the audience already knows, costs screen time and gives nothing back — and on this show it also costs about seven dollars.

**What this rules out, every time:**

- **Transit.** Nobody walks across a room on camera, gets up, sits down, or enters, unless the movement itself is the event. The audience arrives with the cut. Scene 6 was first drafted with the family rising from the table and crossing to Бабушка-Яга, which is thirty seconds of people relocating. The script already supplies the cut that does it for free: SH066 is a tight close-up on her pocket, and when we come out of it everyone is wherever the scene needs them.
- **Establishing for its own sake.** A wide that only says "this is a room" is a wide that says nothing. The wide at the end of s5job3 earns its place because a decision has just landed and someone stands up in it.
- **Confirmation.** A reaction shot that tells us what we already know. The children were first written frozen and waiting AFTER «А почему бы и нет?» — but the argument was already over, so they were waiting for nothing. Moved inside the argument, the same two shots became a fight landing on them.
- **Logistics.** Anything whose content is how a thing happened rather than what happened.

**The test:** lift the shot out. If the story survives, cut it.

---

# LAW 2 — SPECIFY EVERYTHING IN WHAT YOU DO SHOOT

## The root cause of every staging defect on this show

**Unspecified state does not stay empty. It gets filled — by the character sheet's default pose, or by the last instruction the model was given about that person.**

Every staging bug found in EP1 is one blank cell, not a separate mistake:

| What went wrong | The blank cell | What filled it |
|---|---|---|
| Бабушка-Яга holding her broom at dinner | her HANDS | the reference sheet, which puts a broom in them |
| Фрося wearing one grin through Вася's line | her FACE | the reference sheet's fixed expression |
| Nobody looking at Бабушка-Яга as she stood up | Мама's and Папа's EYELINE in the wide | their instructions from the previous singles |
| Руся and Муся doing nothing for a whole scene | their entire ROW | ambient guesswork |
| Папа reading as a servant | his POSITION and HANDS | a plausible invention |
| Бабушка-Яга turning toward someone she already faced | her EYELINE at the start of the shot | a movement invented to justify the end state |
| Мама setting down a cup she was not holding | her HANDS, carried from another staging | a stale earlier draft |

Writing prose about a shot is what allows this. Prose is *about its subject* — it lets a paragraph describe Бабушка-Яга beautifully and never mention that Папа is in the frame. The blanks are invisible because nothing in a paragraph has the shape of a hole.

## The requirement

For EVERY shot, EVERY character in frame has FOUR cells filled:

1. **POSITION** — where they are, and whether they move
2. **HANDS** — what is in them and what those hands are doing
3. **EYELINE** — where they are looking, in screen direction, at the START and at the END of the shot
4. **FACE** — what it does, as a change: what it starts as and what it becomes

A blank cell is a defect that has not surfaced yet. There is no fifth column and no shot is exempt — least of all a wide, which inherits every instruction from the singles unless it overrides them person by person.

## Consequences that follow automatically

These stop needing to be separate rules, because the matrix produces them:

- A character sheet fixes identity, never pose or expression — so HANDS and FACE must be written every time, and the broom is excluded because her hands are specified as empty.
- Somebody standing, entering, being summoned or being touched takes every EYELINE in the room — because every character's eyeline cell for that shot must be filled, and they will all point at the event.
- Nobody holds one expression through another person's line — because FACE is defined as a change.
- A person who is in frame but not in the writer's head is impossible — because they have a row.

## How it is used

The staging sheet for a scene IS the matrix, and it is filled and reviewed BEFORE any prompt exists. The prompt is then generated from the matrix and may contain nothing the matrix does not.

Any cell the writer is guessing at is marked as a guess. The author corrects the matrix in one pass, rather than correcting the same errors one prompt line at a time — which is what turned a sixteen-second clip into a two-hour evening on 2026-08-17.
