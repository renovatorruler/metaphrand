# Stove references — candidates, NOT canon

Filed 2026-08-13 by the author with the note: "not exactly what it's supposed to be, but I want to keep them on file." Nothing here is approved. Do not bind any of these into a generation job as an approved reference, and do not treat them as settling the stove's design.

## What canon already fixes

From `2026-08-03_EP1_APPROVED_DECISIONS.md` §3: the human house uses an older cast-iron wood-burning kitchen range — Russian плита — with a visible metal flue pipe, труба. It is explicitly NOT a monumental masonry русская печь. All three references below are the correct appliance class, which is why they are worth keeping even though none is right.

## What canon deliberately leaves open

The same document lists the stove's colour and the precise plaster/brick finish as art-direction choices that were never decided, and its correction table reads "The stove is orange" → "Colour remains an art-direction choice". These three references sit across that open decision rather than contradicting it.

## The three

**a — enamel cream and green, brick hearth.** An American enamelled range with nickel trim, standing on a hearth against a brick surround. The most directly useful part is not the stove but the staging: it shows the appliance sitting forward of a brick fireplace opening, which is the arrangement the whole home-behind-the-stove premise depends on. The pale enamel is furthest from a black cast-iron read.

**b — white enamel, high warming oven, farm kitchen.** Shows the full flue run from the stove top up and out, plus the high warming oven above the cooking surface. Useful for the труба geometry and for how much vertical space the range occupies in a room. The surrounding kitchen is plainly American and carries none of the Russian interior language.

**c — black cast iron, ornate, warming closet.** Closest to a black cast-iron read, with a high warming closet, ornate nickel door and decorative fretwork. The ornamentation is heavier and more Victorian than anything else in the show's low-poly register.

## Standing gap this does not close

The production still has no stove-corner reference plate — the stove seen in its corner of the room, from the angle the episode actually shoots it, with the iron door Baba Yaga comes out of. These are appliance references, not a set plate. See the scenes 7–9 asset list.
